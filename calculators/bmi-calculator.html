Quick answer. BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared. An adult who is 5 ft 10 in and weighs 160 lb has a BMI of 23.0, which is in the normal range of 18.5 to under 25. The calculator also shows the healthy weight range for your height, BMI Prime and the Ponderal Index.

How BMI is calculated

Body Mass Index is your weight divided by the square of your height. In metric units the formula is BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². In US units it is BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²; the 703 simply converts pounds and inches to the metric result. The calculator accepts either set of units and shows the same number.

Because height is squared, a taller person can weigh more at the same BMI. That is also why the healthy weight range the calculator shows changes with height.

Adult BMI categories

The CDC groups adults (age 20 and over) by BMI as follows: under 18.5 is underweight, 18.5 up to 25 is a healthy weight, 25 up to 30 is overweight, and 30 or more is obesity, which is split into class 1 (30 to under 35), class 2 (35 to under 40) and class 3 (40 and above). The result panel uses the same cut-offs and also splits the underweight range into mild, moderate and severe thinness.

Worked example

A person who is 5 feet 10 inches (70 in) tall and weighs 160 pounds: BMI = 703 × 160 ÷ 70² = 112,480 ÷ 4,900 = 23.0. That is in the normal range. For this height, a BMI between 18.5 and 25 corresponds to about 129 to 174 pounds.

BMI Prime and Ponderal Index

BMI Prime is your BMI divided by 25, the upper edge of the normal range. A value below 1 means you are under that limit and above 1 means you are over it. For the example, 23.0 ÷ 25 = 0.92, so the person is at 92% of the upper limit.

The Ponderal Index divides weight by height cubed (kg/m³) instead of squared. It is sometimes preferred for people who are much taller or shorter than average, because the squared formula tends to over-state BMI for tall people. The example comes to 12.9 kg/m³.

What BMI cannot tell you

  • It does not measure body fat directly. The CDC notes that BMI does not distinguish between fat, muscle and bone.
  • It does not show where fat is stored. Fat around the waist carries more health risk than fat on the hips and thighs, so waist size is a useful second number.
  • It can read high for muscular people and low for people who have lost muscle, such as some older adults.
  • Adult cut-offs do not apply to children and teens. Their BMI is compared with growth charts for their age and sex, which this calculator does not model.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing units, such as pounds with centimetres. Keep one system or use the unit switch above.
  • Entering height in inches only when the form asks for feet and inches (or the other way round).
  • Treating BMI as a diagnosis. It is a screening number; a doctor looks at your history, blood pressure and lab results as well.
  • Chasing the exact boundary. A BMI of 24.9 and 25.1 are not meaningfully different.

How to read your result

Use the category as a starting point and the healthy weight range as a rough guide for your height. If your BMI is outside the normal range, or you are an athlete, a more detailed look helps: compare it with your waist measurement and body fat estimate, and talk to a health professional before making big changes. Common questions

Does this calculator account for children and teens?

You can enter any age, but the category shown uses the adult ranges. Children and teens are assessed with age- and sex-specific growth charts, which this calculator does not model, so use a paediatric chart or ask a doctor for anyone under 20.

Why does the healthy weight range change with height?

BMI divides weight by height squared. For a fixed target BMI, a taller person can weigh more and a shorter person must weigh less, so the range shifts with height. See the table above for typical heights.

Is a BMI of 25 to 30 unhealthy?

Not automatically. It is classed as overweight, which is a screening flag rather than a diagnosis. Waist size, blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol and fitness give a much clearer picture of health risk.

Why can athletes have a high BMI?

BMI uses only height and weight. Muscle is denser than fat, so a muscular person can reach 25 or more with low body fat. In that case, body fat percentage or waist size is a better guide.

What is the difference between BMI and BMI Prime?

BMI Prime is BMI divided by 25. It turns the result into a ratio against the top of the normal range: below 1 is under the limit, above 1 is over it.

How accurate is BMI?

It is a quick and consistent screening tool for groups, but it is less precise for individuals because it cannot separate fat from muscle or show where fat sits. Treat it as a first signal, not a verdict.
