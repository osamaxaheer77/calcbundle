Interest Rate Calculator

Work out the real interest rate on a fixed-term loan when a dealer or lender only gives you the monthly payment and total price — not the rate itself.

years months

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. If you know the loan amount, term and payment, you can solve for the interest rate. A $32,000 loan repaid at $960 a month for 3 years has a rate of 5.065% and costs $2,560 in interest.

Why the rate is not always disclosed up front

Some lenders and dealers build an offer around the monthly payment and the total price and do not state the interest rate directly. Sometimes that is for simplicity, and sometimes because a payment-focused pitch is easier to sell. Either way, the rate is fully determined by the loan amount, the term and the payment, so you can solve for it. This calculator does exactly that.

How the rate is found

For a loan of L repaid with n equal monthly payments of P at monthly rate r, the payment satisfies L = P × [1 − (1 + r)−n] ÷ r. There is no simple way to rearrange this for r, so the calculator tries rates between 0% and 100% and homes in on the one that makes the equation true. The yearly rate shown is the monthly rate times 12, the same way an APR is quoted.

Worked example with the default numbers

A loan of $32,000 repaid at $960 a month for 3 years (36 payments). The payments total $34,560, so the loan costs $2,560 in interest. The hidden rate is 5.065%. Check it: at 5.065% a year, which is 0.4221% a month, a payment of $960 repays $32,000 in 36 months.

Why it matters for comparing offers

A monthly payment alone cannot tell you whether a deal is good. A lower payment stretched over a longer term can hide a worse rate and a larger total cost. Working out the real rate lets you compare two offers on equal footing, the same way an APR does for loans with different fees.

Finding a hidden rate

A dealer says a $30,000 car costs $600 a month for 60 months. The calculator shows a rate of 7.42%. If $2,500 of add-ons were rolled into a $32,500 loan at the same $600 a month, the rate would be 4.10%, different from what the headline suggests. Use the full amount financed, including any fees, and the exact payment.

What the calculator assumes

  • A standard fixed-rate, fixed-term loan with equal monthly payments, paid at the end of each month.
  • No fees or points. If there are upfront costs, the APR Calculator gives the true cost.
  • If the payments total less than the loan, no rate fits (even 0% would not repay it) and the calculator says so. If they total exactly the loan, the rate is 0%.
  • A balloon payment or a skipped payment is not modelled.

If the rate looks too high

  • Check that the amount includes only what you borrowed, not the full price including a down payment.
  • Look for add-ons, fees or insurance rolled into the payment.
  • Ask the lender for the rate and the APR in writing, and compare with the rate you find here.
  • A rate far above the advertised one usually means fees, add-ons or a balloon payment.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the car's price instead of the amount financed.
  • Entering a payment that includes taxes, insurance or other items.
  • Comparing payments across different terms without checking the rate.
  • Forgetting that a zero rate means payments exactly equal the loan: $12,000 over 12 payments of $1,000.

How to read your result

The large number is the yearly interest rate hidden in the payment. Below it are the total of all payments and the total interest, with a chart of principal and interest. Compare the rate with other offers or with the lender’s stated rate; a big difference is a prompt to ask questions.

Common questions

What if my monthly payment doesn't fully pay off the loan?

Then no interest rate fits, because even at 0% the payments would add up to less than the loan. The payment has to be large enough to retire the full loan within the given term for a rate to exist.

Does this work for any type of loan?

Yes, as long as it is a standard fixed-rate, fixed-term loan with equal monthly payments. Auto loans, personal loans and most installment loans fit.

Why is the rate I find higher than the advertised rate?

The payment may include fees, add-ons or insurance, or the amount financed may be larger than the advertised price. Use the exact amount financed and the exact payment, and ask the lender for a breakdown.

How is the yearly rate calculated?

The calculator finds the monthly rate that makes the payments repay the loan, then multiplies by 12, the way an APR is quoted.

What does a zero rate mean?

The payments add up to exactly the loan amount. For example, 12 payments of $1,000 repay $12,000 at a 0% rate.

Should I use this or the APR calculator?

Use this when you know the payment but not the rate. Use the APR Calculator when you know the rate and want to include fees.
