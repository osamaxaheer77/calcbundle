Interest Rate Calculator

Work out the real interest rate on a fixed-term loan when a dealer or lender only gives you the monthly payment and total price — not the rate itself.

years months

Why the rate isn't always disclosed upfront

Some lenders and dealers structure an offer entirely around the monthly payment and total price, without stating the interest rate directly — sometimes just for simplicity, sometimes because a payment-focused pitch is easier to sell than a rate-focused one. Either way, the rate is fully determined by the loan amount, term, and payment; it's just a matter of solving for it instead of being told it outright.

Why this matters for comparing offers

A monthly payment alone can't tell you whether a deal is good — a lower payment stretched over a longer term can hide a worse rate. Working out the actual rate lets you compare two offers on equal footing, the same way APR lets you compare loans with different fee structures.

This calculator assumes a standard, fully-amortizing loan with equal monthly payments and no fees. If the loan includes points, origination fees, or other upfront costs, the APR Calculator is the more accurate tool for finding the true cost.

A worked example

A loan of $32,000 repaid at $960 a month for 3 years has an interest rate of 5.065%. The 36 payments total $34,560, so the loan costs $2,560 in interest. This is useful when a dealer or lender quotes only the payment and the term, and you want to know the rate hiding inside it.

Tips for comparing offers

Use the full loan amount, including any fees rolled in, and the exact monthly payment. Remember that a longer term lowers the payment but raises the total interest. If the rate you find is much higher than advertised, the difference usually comes from fees, add-ons or a balloon payment. Compare offers using the rate and the total interest paid, and not the monthly payment alone.

Common questions

What if my monthly payment doesn't fully pay off the loan?

Then there's no finite positive interest rate that fits — the payment has to be large enough to eventually retire the full loan amount within the given term for a rate to exist.

Does this work for any type of loan?

Yes, as long as it's a standard fixed-rate, fixed-term loan with equal monthly payments — auto loans, personal loans, and most installment loans all fit this pattern.
