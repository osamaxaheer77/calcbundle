Time Zone Calculator

Select a starting date, time and zone, then the destination zone. Offsets may change with daylight saving time, so do not assume the same time difference throughout the year.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Time zone gaps change with daylight saving. 2:00 PM in New York on January 15, 2026 is 4:00 AM on January 16 in Tokyo (+14 hours), but on July 15 the gap is +13 hours and the result is 3:00 AM.

What the calculator does

Choose a date, a time, the zone you are converting from and the zone you are converting to. The calculator shows the date and time in the destination zone, the day of the week and the difference from the origin in hours. It covers 22 zones, from Hawaii to New Zealand, including UTC.

How daylight saving is handled

The tool does not use one fixed offset per region. It looks up each zone’s real rules for the exact date you enter, taken from the standard time zone database built into your browser. That is why the same pair of cities can be an hour apart from their usual gap at some times of year. You pick both zones yourself, so the result does not depend on the time zone of the device you are using.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens on January 15, 2026 at 2:00 PM in Eastern Time, converted to Japan. New York is on standard time (EST, UTC−5) and Tokyo is on UTC+9, so Tokyo is 14 hours ahead. The result is January 16, 2026, 04:00:00 AM, a Friday, with a difference of +14 hours. The destination label reads GMT+9, which is how the browser names Japan’s zone in English.

The same conversion in July

Change the date to July 15, 2026 and the answer changes. New York is now on daylight time (EDT, UTC−4) while Japan stays on UTC+9, so the gap is only +13 hours. 2:00 PM in New York becomes July 16, 2026, 03:00:00 AM, a Thursday. A fixed “14 hours ahead” rule would be an hour wrong for roughly eight months of the year.

When the US and Europe are out of step

The US and Europe change their clocks on different dates, so the New York to London gap is not always 5 hours. On Sunday, March 15, 2026, New York is already on daylight time but London is still on GMT. A call at 2:00 PM in New York is 6:00 PM in London, a gap of +4 hours, compared with 7:00 PM and +5 hours on January 15. The gap is 4 hours for about 28 days a year.

Crossing midnight and the date

Converting a late-evening time can land on a different calendar day. Take 8:00 PM on December 31, 2026 in New York. In Tokyo it is already January 1, 2027, 10:00:00 AM, a Friday, and the result says +14 hours. Sydney to Los Angeles at 9:00 AM on January 15 goes the other way: it is 2:00 PM on Wednesday, January 14 in Los Angeles, a difference of −19 hours. Always read the date line of the result, not only the time.

Half-hour zones

Not every zone is a whole number of hours from UTC. India is UTC+5:30, so 2:00 PM in New York on January 15 is 12:30 AM on Friday, January 16 in India, with a difference of +10.5 hours. The result label for these zones appears as GMT+5:30.

Times near a clock change

On the day the clocks change, some times are special. In New York on March 8, 2026, the clock jumps from 2:00 AM to 3:00 AM, so 2:30 AM does not exist. On November 1, 2026 the clock goes back from 2:00 AM to 1:00 AM, so 1:30 AM happens twice. The calculator picks one reading in these cases: 1:30 AM on November 1 is taken as the first one (EDT, 5:30 AM UTC). A time just after the spring change, like 3:30 AM on March 8, is read correctly as EDT, which is 7:30 AM UTC and 4:30 PM in Tokyo. For scheduling on these two days, choose a time outside the 2:00 AM hour.

What the result shows

  • Large line: the destination time zone name, the date and the time, including seconds.
  • Day of week: the weekday in the destination zone, which can differ from the weekday where you start.
  • Difference from origin: hours ahead (+) or behind (−), for that specific date. It says “Same time” when the two zones match.

Common mistakes

  • Using the same hour gap all year. Check the actual date.
  • Forgetting that the US and Europe change their clocks on different days.
  • Reading the time without checking the date in the result.
  • Booking an event in the skipped or repeated hour on a clock-change night.
  • Assuming the zone name matches a country. A name like Pacific Time covers a region, and the same country can have several zones.

How to read your result

Read the date first, then the time. If the difference is a whole number, add it to the starting time. If it is negative, subtract it. When you plan a meeting across more than two zones, convert each from the same date and starting time so you can compare the results side by side.

Time zone rules change when governments change them. The tool uses your browser’s zone data, so very new rule changes may not be included on an old device or browser.

Common questions

Which time zone is my browser using?

This tool does not use your device's time zone. You pick both the origin and the destination, so the result is the same wherever you are.

Why does the same pair of cities have a different gap in summer?

Some places change their clocks for daylight saving and some do not. New York is 14 hours behind Tokyo in January but 13 hours behind in July.

Why is the New York to London gap sometimes 4 hours?

The US and Europe start and end daylight saving on different dates. For about four weeks a year, the gap is 4 hours instead of the usual 5.

Why does the date change in the result?

When the destination is ahead or behind by many hours, a late or early time can fall on the next or previous calendar day. Check the date shown in the result.

What happens to times that are skipped or repeated at a clock change?

A time in the skipped hour does not exist, and a time in the repeated hour happens twice. The calculator picks one reading, so avoid those hours when scheduling.

Does it handle half-hour zones?

Yes. India is UTC+5:30, so the difference from New York in January is +10.5 hours.
