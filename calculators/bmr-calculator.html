BMR Calculator

Quick answer. BMR is the number of calories your body burns at rest. A 25-year-old man who is 5 ft 10 in and weighs 160 lb has a BMR of about 1,717 calories a day (Mifflin-St Jeor). Multiply it by an activity factor, from 1.2 to 1.9, to estimate total daily needs, from about 2,060 to 3,262 calories.

What BMR means

Basal metabolic rate is the energy your body uses just to stay alive at rest: keeping the heart beating, the lungs working and body temperature steady. For most people it is the largest part of daily energy use, often around 60% to 70%. The rest comes from moving around, exercise and digesting food. BMR is not the amount you should eat. It is the baseline your body burns doing nothing, which you then scale up for your activity.

The three formulas in this calculator

  • Mifflin-St Jeor (the default): men = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5; women = same but − 161. It was built from measurements on 498 adults and is widely used because it tends to be more accurate than the older equation.
  • Revised Harris-Benedict: men = 13.397 × kg + 4.799 × cm − 5.677 × age + 88.362; women = 9.247 × kg + 3.098 × cm − 4.330 × age + 447.593. The original 1919 version was found to over-estimate by about 5% in the Mifflin study.
  • Katch-McArdle: 370 + 21.6 × lean body mass (kg). It needs your body fat percentage, which is why it can be better for very lean or very muscular people.

Worked example

A 25-year-old man who is 5 ft 10 in (177.8 cm) and weighs 160 lb (72.6 kg). Mifflin-St Jeor: 10 × 72.6 + 6.25 × 177.8 − 5 × 25 + 5 = 1,717 calories a day.

How much the formula choice matters

Different equations give different answers for the same person. With a body fat of 20%, the example gives 1,717 (Mifflin-St Jeor), 1,772 (Harris-Benedict) and 1,624 (Katch-McArdle). The gap is under 10%, which is normal; no formula captures every person.

A second example: a 30-year-old woman who is 5 ft 5 in and weighs 140 lb gets 1,356 calories from Mifflin-St Jeor (10 × 63.5 + 6.25 × 165.1 − 5 × 30 − 161).

What raises or lowers BMR

  • Body size and muscle: bigger bodies and more lean mass burn more at rest.
  • Age: BMR tends to fall gradually with age, partly because muscle declines.
  • Sex: men on average have more lean mass, which is why the formulas differ.
  • Other factors: genetics, thyroid function, pregnancy, illness and extreme temperatures. None of these is captured by a formula.

Using BMR to plan food intake

To keep your weight, eat about your total daily needs (BMR × activity factor). A common rule of thumb is that a daily deficit of about 500 calories leads to roughly one pound of loss a week. Going below your BMR for long is not recommended without medical supervision, because it makes it hard to get enough nutrients. Recalculate whenever your weight changes by more than a few pounds.

Common mistakes

  • Eating your BMR and ignoring activity. BMR excludes everything you do all day.
  • Overestimating the activity level. Most people who sit for work and exercise a few times a week fit the 1.375 or 1.465 rows.
  • Entering weight in pounds with the metric tab selected (or the reverse).
  • Treating the number as exact. It is an estimate with an error of roughly 10%.

How to read your result

Read your BMR as a floor and the activity table as your range of daily needs. Pick the row that matches your real week, then watch your weight for two to three weeks. If it is stable, you have found your maintenance level. If it rises or falls, adjust by about 100 to 200 calories and check again.

This calculator gives estimates for healthy adults and is not medical advice. Ask a doctor or dietitian before large changes to your eating.

Common questions

How is this different from the Calorie Calculator?

They use the same underlying formulas. This page focuses on BMR itself and shows the full range of activity levels. The Calorie Calculator frames the same numbers around maintaining, losing or gaining weight.

Which formula should I choose?

Mifflin-St Jeor is the default and is a good general choice. Katch-McArdle can be more accurate if you know your body fat percentage, because it works from lean body mass instead of total weight.

Is BMR the same as calories I should eat?

No. BMR is only the energy used at rest. Your daily needs are BMR multiplied by an activity factor, which is why the activity table is included.

Can I eat less than my BMR to lose weight faster?

It is not recommended for long periods without medical advice. Very low intakes make it hard to get enough nutrients and are hard to keep up. A moderate deficit of about 500 calories a day is a common starting point.

Why does my result differ from other calculators?

Different websites use different formulas and different activity factors. Differences of a few percent are normal. Pick one tool and use real changes in your weight to fine-tune it.

Does BMR change with age?

Yes, it tends to decrease gradually as muscle mass falls. Recalculate when your age, weight or body composition changes noticeably.
