Simple Interest Calculator

Calculate simple interest and end balance, or solve for the principal, term, or rate — using the plain Interest = Principal × Rate × Time formula.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Simple interest is charged only on the original amount: Interest = Principal × Rate × Time. $20,000 at 3% for 10 years earns $6,000, giving a balance of $26,000, and each year pays the same $600.

What simple interest is

Simple interest is charged only on the original amount (the principal), never on interest that has already built up. Each year earns the same amount, so the total grows in a straight line. The formula is Interest = Principal × Rate × Time, with the rate as a decimal per year and time in years. The end balance is the principal plus the interest.

The four ways to use the calculator

Solve forFormula
Interest and end balanceI = P × r × t; balance = P + I
PrincipalP = balance ÷ (1 + r × t)
Rater = (balance − P) ÷ (P × t)
Termt = (balance − P) ÷ (P × r)

If you enter the term in months, divide by 12 first: 18 months is 1.5 years.

Worked examples with the default numbers

  • End balance: $20,000 at 3% for 10 years earns 20,000 × 0.03 × 10 = $6,000, so the balance is $26,000. Each year pays the same $600.
  • Principal: to end at $26,000 after 10 years at 3%, the starting amount is 26,000 ÷ (1 + 0.03 × 10) = $20,000.
  • Rate: turning $20,000 into $26,000 over 10 years needs (26,000 − 20,000) ÷ (20,000 × 10) = 3.0% a year.
  • Term: earning $6,000 on $20,000 at 3% takes 6,000 ÷ (20,000 × 0.03) = 10 years.

Simple vs compound interest

Compound interest adds the interest to the balance so it earns interest too. In the first period the two agree exactly; after that, compounding pulls ahead. For $20,000 at 3% with yearly compounding:

Where simple interest is used

  • Short-term loans and some personal loans, where interest is calculated on the amount owed for a number of days.
  • Add-on interest loans, where interest is worked out once on the full amount and added to the loan.
  • Late-payment and legal interest, which is often charged as simple interest.
  • Bonds and notes that pay a fixed coupon on the face value.

Add-on interest can cost more than it looks

Suppose a lender offers a $10,000 loan at 6% add-on interest for 3 years. The interest is 10,000 × 0.06 × 3 = $1,800, so you repay $11,800 in 36 payments of $327.78. That looks like 6%, but you are paying back principal each month, so on average you owe far less than $10,000. The true annual rate (APR) is about 11.1%. Always compare loans by APR.

Daily simple interest and day counts

Short-term interest is often calculated per day: Interest = Principal × Rate × Days ÷ 365 (or 360). On $5,000 at 8% for 90 days that is $98.63 on a 365-day basis or $100.00 on a 360-day basis.

More everyday examples

  • Income target: to earn $1,000 a year in simple interest at 4%, you need a principal of 1,000 ÷ 0.04 = $25,000.
  • Late invoice: a $2,400 invoice that charges 1.5% simple interest per month for 3 months adds 2,400 × 0.015 × 3 = $108.
  • Short loan: borrowing $3,000 at 9% for 6 months (0.5 years) costs 3,000 × 0.09 × 0.5 = $135 in interest.
  • Doubling time: at simple interest, money doubles when rate × time = 100%. At 5% that takes 20 years, whereas compounding at 5% doubles it in about 14 years.

To check any result by hand, write down the principal, the yearly rate as a decimal and the time in years, multiply the three together for the interest, and add the principal for the balance. If the term is in months or days, convert it to years first. Doing it once by hand makes it easy to spot a typo in a quote from a lender.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the rate as 0.03 instead of 3 (or the reverse).
  • Mixing months and years. Convert the term to years before using the formula by hand.
  • Assuming a savings account pays simple interest. Most compound.
  • Comparing add-on interest loans by the quoted rate instead of by APR.

How to read your result

The interest is the total cost (or earnings) over the term, and the balance is what you owe or end up with. Divide the interest by the number of years to see the flat yearly amount. Common questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus the interest already added, so it grows faster over time.

Which formula does the calculator use?

Interest = Principal × Rate × Time, with the rate as a yearly decimal and time in years. The end balance is the principal plus the interest.

Can I solve for the rate or the time?

Yes. Choose the value to solve for and enter the other three. Rate = (balance − principal) ÷ (principal × time), and time = (balance − principal) ÷ (principal × rate).

How do I enter a term in months?

Use the months box, or divide the months by 12 and enter years. Eighteen months is 1.5 years.

Do banks use simple interest?

Most savings accounts compound. Simple interest is common on short-term loans, some auto loans with add-on interest, bonds and late-payment charges.

Why is an add-on interest rate higher than it seems?

Interest is charged on the full original amount even though you repay part of it every month. The true rate (APR) is therefore much higher than the quoted add-on rate.
