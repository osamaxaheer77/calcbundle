Quick answer. A 105 inch total rise with a 7 inch rise and 10 inch run needs 15 risers, or 14 steps, a total run of 11 feet 8 inches and a stringer 14 feet 2.89 inches long, at an angle of 34.99°.

The parts of a stair

The total rise is the height from the lower floor to the upper floor. The rise of a step is its height, and the run is its depth. The stringer is the sloping board that carries the treads and risers. A staircase has one more riser than it has runs, because the top step is the upper floor itself.

How the calculator works

With a fixed rise, it divides the total rise by the rise you want and rounds up to a whole number of risers. The first step may be shorter than the rest, and the result shows its height. With a fixed number of steps, every rise is the same. The stringer height is the total rise less one rise, and the stringer length follows from the Pythagorean theorem using that height and the total run. The angle is the arctangent of rise over run. You can enter lengths in feet, inches, metres or centimetres, and give either the run of one step or the total run.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens with a total rise of 105 inches, a run of 10 inches per step, and a rise of 7 inches. Dividing, 105 ÷ 7 = 15, so there are 15 risers and, since the top step is the floor, 14 steps. The first step is 105 − 14 × 7 = 7 inches or 17.78 cm, an even 7 inches. The total run is 14 × 10 = 11 feet 8 inches or 355.60 cm. The stringer height is 105 − 7 = 8 feet 2 inches or 248.92 cm, and the stringer length is √(98² + 140²) = 14 feet 2.89 inches or 434.07 cm. The angle is arctan(7 ÷ 10) = 34.99°.

A comfort check

Stairs feel comfortable when the rise and run work together. A traditional rule, going back to Blondel in the 1600s, is that twice the rise plus the run should be about a step length, often quoted as 24 to 25 inches. The default gives 2 × 7 + 10 = 24 inches, so it is in that range. Building codes set their own limits on the rise and the run, so check yours before you build.

Other settings

  • Fixed number of steps. Enter 14 steps and the tool divides the 105 inch rise by 15 risers to give a rise of 7 inches on every step.
  • Total run. If you have 140 inches of horizontal space, choose total run, and each step is 140 ÷ 14 = 10 inches or 25.40 cm.
  • Treads with thickness. With a 1 inch tread the stringer height drops to 8 feet 1 inch or 246.38 cm, the first step without its tread is 6 inches or 15.24 cm, and the stringer is placed 8 inches or 20.32 cm from the lower floor.
  • Flush mount. A flush mount sets the stringer against the upper floor, with a run for every riser: the stringer height is the whole rise, 8 feet 9 inches or 266.70 cm, and with the same run it is 15 feet 3.1 inches or 465.07 cm long.

Headroom

The headroom check finds the height of the stairs under the start of the floor opening and measures what is left below the underside of the upper floor. Suppose the opening is 7 feet long, the floor is 8 inches thick and you need 80 inches. The underside of the upper floor is 105 − 8 = 97 inches above the lower floor. At the start of the opening the stair has climbed 6 steps and 42 inches, which leaves 4 feet 7 inches or 139.70 cm. That is less than 80 inches, so the requirement is not met. The tool then reports that the floor opening needs to be at least 10 feet or 304.80 cm long.

Common mistakes

  • Using the finished floor-to-floor height. The total rise runs from finished floor to finished floor.
  • Counting steps and risers the same way. A stair has one more riser than steps unless it is flush mounted.
  • Mixing inches and centimetres.
  • Ignoring the thickness of the treads, which changes the first step and the stringer.
  • Skipping the local building code for the rise, the run and the headroom.

Common questions

How do I work out the number of steps?

Divide the total rise by the rise you want and round up to a whole number of risers. A stair has one more riser than it has steps, unless it is flush mounted. For a 105 inch rise at 7 inches, that is 15 risers and 14 steps.

How is the stringer length calculated?

Use the Pythagorean theorem with the stringer height and the total run. For a height of 98 inches and a run of 140 inches, the length is about 170.9 inches.

What is a comfortable rise and run?

A traditional rule is that twice the rise plus the run should be about 24 to 25 inches. A 7 inch rise with a 10 inch run gives 24. Building codes set limits on both, so check yours.

What is the first step height?

When you give a rise and it does not divide the total rise evenly, the first step is shorter than the rest. With an even division, as in the default, it equals the rise.

What does a flush stringer mount mean?

The stringer sits against the upper floor with a run for every riser, so its height is the whole rise.

How does the headroom check work?

It finds how high the stair is where the floor opening starts and compares the space left below the upper floor with the headroom you need. If it is too small, it tells you how long the opening must be.
