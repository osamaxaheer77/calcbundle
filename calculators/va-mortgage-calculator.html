Quick answer. A VA loan has no monthly mortgage insurance but charges a one-time funding fee. On a $500,000 home with no down payment, 6.5% interest and 30 years, a first-time user pays a 2.15% fee ($10,750), borrows $510,750 and has a payment of $3,228.29 a month.

What a VA loan is

A VA loan is a mortgage for eligible veterans, service members and some surviving spouses, backed by the Department of Veterans Affairs. Lenders make the loan and the VA guarantees part of it, which is why borrowers can often buy with no down payment and without monthly mortgage insurance. Unlike an FHA loan, there is no annual insurance premium. In its place is a single one-time VA funding fee.

How the funding fee is set

Down paymentFirst use of the benefitUsed a VA loan before
Under 5%2.15%3.3%
5% to under 10%1.5%1.5%
10% or more1.25%1.25%

The fee is a percentage of the base loan amount (price minus down payment). It is waived for veterans with a service-related disability rating of 10% or higher, for most surviving spouses and for some others, as written into VA policy. The calculator asks about your eligibility, whether you have used the benefit before, and whether you have a qualifying disability, then picks the rate automatically.

Worked example with the default numbers

A $500,000 home, no down payment, 6.5% interest, 30 years, a first-time user who finances the fee. The fee is 2.15% of $500,000 = $10,750, so the loan is $510,750. The monthly principal and interest payment is $3,228.29 and the total interest over 30 years is $651,433. There is no mortgage insurance on top.

Financed or paid upfront?

Most borrowers roll the fee into the loan, which keeps cash needs low but means paying interest on it. If you pay the $10,750 fee in cash at closing, the loan is $500,000, the payment falls to $3,160.34 (about $68 a month less) and total interest drops by $13,711 over 30 years.

How a down payment helps

You do not need a down payment, but one lowers the loan, the fee rate and the payment. Because the fee rate drops at 5% and again at 10% down, those thresholds are worth reaching if you can.

VA, FHA or conventional?

On a $500,000 home an FHA loan with 3.5% down ($17,500) has principal and interest of about $3,103.10 plus $221.15 a month of annual mortgage insurance, $3,324.25 together, using the FHA Loan Calculator’s defaults. The VA loan above has $3,228.29 with no down payment and no monthly insurance. VA loans are often the cheapest option for eligible borrowers, but you must still meet the lender’s credit and income requirements and the VA’s occupancy rules.

Taxes, insurance and other costs

Tick Include Taxes & Costs to add property tax (a percentage of the price or a dollar amount), home insurance, HOA fees and other yearly costs to the monthly total. By default these are left out so you see the loan payment itself.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting that a financed funding fee increases the loan and the interest.
  • Assuming every veteran pays the same fee. The rate depends on use, down payment and exemptions.
  • Comparing a VA payment with a conventional payment that does not include PMI.
  • Treating the estimate as an approval. Eligibility and rates come from the VA and your lender.

How to read your result

The large number is the monthly principal and interest. Under it you see the loan amount including any financed fee, the fee itself and the payoff date. Try a first-time user versus a repeat user, a small down payment, and financed versus paid upfront to see which combination fits your budget.

This calculator gives estimates. VA eligibility, funding fees and loan limits can change, so confirm them with the VA and your lender. It is not financial advice.

Common questions

Is a VA loan always cheaper than a conventional or FHA loan?

Often, especially with little or no down payment, because VA loans have no monthly mortgage insurance. But the funding fee is a real cost, and a borrower with a large down payment and strong credit may find a conventional loan competitive.

Do all veterans pay the same funding fee?

No. It depends on your down payment, whether you have used the benefit before, and whether you have a qualifying service-related disability. Two veterans buying identical homes can owe different fees.

Can I put 0% down on a VA loan?

Yes. VA loans let qualified borrowers finance the whole purchase price with no down payment, which is one of their main benefits.

Should I finance the funding fee or pay it upfront?

Financing keeps your cash needs low but you pay interest on the fee. Paying it upfront costs more at closing but gives a smaller loan and lower monthly payments.

Who is exempt from the funding fee?

Veterans with a service-related disability rating of 10% or higher and most surviving spouses are exempt, along with some others listed by the VA. The calculator asks about disability and spouse status.

Does this calculator include mortgage insurance?

There is none to include. VA loans do not charge monthly mortgage insurance; the one-time funding fee replaces it.
