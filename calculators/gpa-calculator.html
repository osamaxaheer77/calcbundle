GPA Planning

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. GPA is total grade points divided by total credits, where each course's points are its grade value times its credits. A 4-credit A+ (4.3), a 2-credit B (3.0) and a 3-credit A (4.0) give 35.2 points over 9 credits, a GPA of 3.911.

How GPA is calculated

Grade point average is a weighted average. Each course turns its letter grade into grade points, multiplies that by its credit hours, and the total is divided by the total credits: GPA = Σ(grade points × credits) ÷ Σ(credits). Because of the weighting, a 4-credit course affects your GPA twice as much as a 2-credit course.

The calculator uses the standard unweighted scale shown above, where A+ is 4.3. Schools differ: many cap an A+ at 4.0, some have no plus or minus grades, and some give extra points for AP, IB or honors courses. Check your school's scale before relying on any result. Pass/fail (P, NP) courses are normally left out of the GPA.

Worked example: planning a target GPA

A student has a 2.8 GPA over 25 credits and wants a 3.0 overall after 15 more credits. The GPA needed in those 15 credits is (3.0 × 40 − 2.8 × 25) ÷ 15 = 3.333. If the answer is above 4.3 the target is out of reach on this scale in the credits you have left.

Why GPA gets harder to move over time

Credits you have already earned carry the same weight as new ones, so the more you have, the less one semester can change your GPA. A student with a 3.0 and 15 credits who earns straight 4.0 grades for 15 credits ends at 3.50. With 120 credits earned, the same perfect semester only reaches 3.11.

A single course matters less than people fear when many credits are behind it. For a student with a 3.4 GPA over 60 credits, a 3-credit B− takes the GPA to 3.37 and a 3-credit C takes it to 3.33. Similarly, to raise a 3.2 GPA over 45 credits to 3.5 by earning only 4.0 grades you would need about 27 more credits.

Retakes, pass/fail and transfer credit

  • Retaken courses: some schools replace the old grade, others average the two. Enter what your school does.
  • Pass/fail: usually earns credit but does not count in GPA. Leave those courses out.
  • Transfer credit: often counts toward credits but not toward your GPA.
  • Weighted grades: AP, IB and honors courses may add a point at some high schools. This calculator is unweighted.

Common mistakes

  • Averaging the grade points without weighting by credits.
  • Using a 4.0 scale when your school gives A+ a 4.3, or the reverse.
  • Counting a pass/fail course.
  • Setting a target that the remaining credits cannot reach.

How to read your result

The GPA is only as good as the scale behind it, so compare it with the one on your transcript. In the planning tool, check whether the required GPA is under 4.0 (realistic), between 4.0 and 4.3 (possible only with plus grades on an A+ scale) or above 4.3 (out of reach). If it is out of reach, either lower the target or add more credits to spread the effort.

This calculator is for planning. Your school's official GPA policy and transcript decide your real GPA.

Common questions

What about P (Pass) or NP (Not Pass) grades?

Leave those courses out of the GPA. Pass/fail grades normally earn credit but do not count toward GPA at most schools.

Can I calculate GPA for just one semester?

Yes. Enter only the courses from that semester. For a cumulative GPA, include every course across all semesters, or use the planning tool with your current GPA and credits.

Does this calculator support weighted GPAs?

No. It uses the standard unweighted scale where A+ is 4.3. Schools that weight AP, IB or honors courses add points, so your official weighted GPA will be higher.

How do I raise my GPA?

Use the planning tool to see the GPA you must earn in the remaining credits. Earlier semesters carry more leverage because fewer credits are already behind you.

Why does my school show a different GPA?

Scales differ (A+ may be 4.0 or 4.3), retaken courses may be treated differently, and transfer credit may be excluded. Your transcript is the official figure.

What GPA do I need for the next semester to reach my target?

Enter your current GPA, current credits, target GPA and the credits you will take. The result is the average GPA you need across those credits.
