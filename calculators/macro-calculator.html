Macro Calculator

Find a daily protein, carb, and fat gram target based on your calorie needs and a diet style.

Quick answer. Macros are the grams of protein, carbohydrate and fat in your daily calories. Protein and carbs give about 4 calories per gram and fat about 9. At 2,549 calories with a 30/40/30 split, the targets are about 191 g protein, 255 g carbs and 85 g fat.

How the macro targets are worked out

The calculator takes four steps. First it estimates your resting energy (BMR) with the Mifflin-St Jeor formula. Second it multiplies by your activity level to get total daily energy (TDEE). Third it adds or subtracts calories for your goal. Fourth it splits that calorie target between protein, carbohydrate and fat using the diet style you choose.

Grams come from calories using energy per gram: protein and carbohydrate about 4 kcal per gram, fat about 9 kcal per gram. So grams = calories × share ÷ 4 (or ÷ 9 for fat).

Worked example with the default numbers

A 25-year-old man who is 5 ft 10 in and weighs 165 lb, with moderate activity (factor 1.465), a maintain-weight goal and the Balanced split:

BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor)1,740 kcal
Daily energy (BMR × 1.465)2,549 kcal
Goal adjustment (maintain)0 kcal
Protein: 30% ÷ 4191 g
Carbs: 40% ÷ 4255 g
Fat: 30% ÷ 985 g

The four diet styles

For the same 2,549 calorie target, the styles give:

StyleProtein / carbs / fat (% of calories)ProteinCarbsFat
Balanced30 / 40 / 30191 g255 g85 g
Low Fat30 / 55 / 15191 g350 g42 g
Low Carb40 / 20 / 40255 g127 g113 g
High Protein40 / 30 / 30255 g191 g85 g

These are simple percentage splits, not prescriptions. Balanced suits most general goals. High Protein is used to hold on to muscle in a deficit or support muscle gain in a surplus. Low Carb and Low Fat move the remaining calories toward one nutrient by preference.

Your goal changes the calories, not the proportions

The goal setting moves calories up or down (about 500 calories a day is roughly one pound a week), and the split is then applied to the new total:

Do the percentages match official ranges?

Public health guidance describes acceptable ranges for adults: roughly 10% to 35% of calories from protein, 20% to 35% from fat and 45% to 65% from carbohydrate. The Balanced split sits within the protein and fat ranges but below the carbohydrate range, and the Low Carb and Low Fat styles deliberately fall outside some ranges. They are styles people use, not recommendations for everyone.

Note also that a percentage-based protein target grows with calories. The default Balanced split gives about 191 g of protein, which is 2.6 g per kilogram of body weight. The recommended minimum for most adults is 0.8 g per kg, and active people often aim between 1.2 and 2.2 g per kg. If you are smaller or eat fewer calories, the result will be lower.

Turning grams into food

Grams are easier to use when you know roughly where they come from. These are typical approximate values; labels and cooking methods change them:

For example, in the default plan an 85 g fat target is about six tablespoons of oil equivalent across the whole day, including the fat already in meat, dairy and nuts. Most people hit their targets by building each meal around a protein source, adding carbohydrate foods for the energy they need, and using fats for cooking and flavour.

Tracking without overdoing it

  • Weigh or measure your main foods for a week to learn what portions look like, then estimate.
  • Hit protein first, then keep total calories near the target; carbs and fat can flex a little.
  • Review the weekly trend rather than any single day.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the split as a medical prescription. It is a starting point.
  • Counting only grams and forgetting that fat has more than twice the calories per gram.
  • Chasing exact numbers. Food labels and tracking apps have a margin of error, and day-to-day variation is normal.
  • Setting an aggressive deficit with a high protein share and no check-in with a professional.

How to read your result

Use the calorie target to manage weight and the macro grams to shape what you eat. Weigh yourself on a weekly average for two to three weeks and adjust calories by 100 to 200 if the trend does not match your goal. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or are an athlete with special needs, ask a doctor or registered dietitian for personal targets.

These are general guidelines and not medical advice. Individual needs vary.

Common questions

Which diet style is best?

There is no universally best split. Adherence matters more than the exact ratio for most people. Pick a style you can keep up and adjust based on how your body responds over a few weeks.

Why don't the gram amounts add up exactly to the calorie target?

Each gram figure is rounded on its own, so the total can differ by a calorie or two. That is rounding, not a calculation error.

How many calories are in a gram of each macro?

Protein and carbohydrate have about 4 calories per gram, fat about 9, and alcohol about 7. The calculator uses 4, 4 and 9.

Do I need to hit my macros exactly?

No. Food labels are approximate and daily intake varies. Aim to be close on most days and watch the weekly trend.

Is a high protein share safe?

For healthy adults, higher-protein eating is commonly used, but people with kidney disease or other conditions should get advice from a doctor first.

How is the calorie target set?

It starts from your estimated daily energy (BMR times activity) and is adjusted by your goal: about 250 calories a day per half pound a week of change.
