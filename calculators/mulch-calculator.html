Mulch Calculator

Estimate how much mulch you need to cover a garden bed or landscaped area, plus an optional cost estimate.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Mulch needed is area times depth. A 200 square foot bed with a 2-inch layer needs 33.33 cubic feet, which is 1.23 cubic yards, or 17 bags of 2 cubic feet. A cubic yard covers about 162 square feet at 2 inches.

How much mulch to use

The volume you need is simply area × depth. A layer of 2 to 4 inches (5 to 10 cm) is the usual recommendation: thick enough to suppress weeds and hold moisture, but not so thick that air and water cannot reach the soil. Extension services give slightly different ranges, from 2 to 3 inches for compost-type materials to 3 to 4 inches for coarse wood chips, so check what suits your plants.

Worked example with the default numbers

To cover 200 square feet with a 2-inch layer: 200 × (2 ÷ 12) = 33.33 cubic feet. That is 1.23 cubic yards (divide by 27), 0.94 cubic metres or 943.89 litres. If your mulch comes in 2-cubic-foot bags, you need 17 bags; in 3-cubic-foot bags, 12. At $30 per cubic yard, bulk mulch for the job costs about $37.04.

Bulk or bags?

Mulch is sold in bulk by the cubic yard, or in bags of 1 to 3 cubic feet or in litres. Compare your cubic-yard total with the supplier’s bag size or minimum delivery. At the example prices here, bulk costs $37.04 and bags $76.50. For larger beds bulk is usually far cheaper, but it needs space to be dropped and a wheelbarrow to move it; bags suit small jobs. Measuring awkward beds

  • Split an irregular bed into rectangles and circles, work out each area and add them. The Area Calculator helps.
  • For a round bed, area = π × radius². A bed 8 feet across has an area of about 50 square feet.
  • For a mound or raised bed, find its volume with the Volume Calculator and use it directly.
  • Enter the area in the unit you measured. The calculator converts square metres, yards and acres.

Buy a little extra

Mulch settles and spreads unevenly, so buy about 10% extra. For the default job that is 1.36 cubic yards instead of 1.23. Top up about an inch each year as the mulch breaks down.

How to apply it

  • Keep mulch a few inches away from tree trunks and plant stems. Piling it against the trunk, the “mulch volcano”, holds moisture against the bark and invites rot, insects and disease.
  • Spread an even layer out to the edge of the canopy rather than heaping it close to the stem.
  • Remove or thin old mulch if it has built up past the recommended depth.
  • Keep weeds out first; mulch suppresses weeds but does not kill established ones.

Example: a ring around a tree

For a tree, leave a gap at the trunk and mulch a ring out from it. A ring from 6 inches out to a 3-foot radius has an area of about 27.5 square feet (π × (3² − 0.5²)). At a 3-inch depth that is 6.9 cubic feet, or 0.25 cubic yards, which is about 4 bags of 2 cubic feet. Enter 27.5 in the area box with the unit set to square feet.

Does the type of mulch change the amount?

The volume calculation is the same for any material, but heavy materials such as bark nuggets or rubber settle less than light organic mulches, which affects how often you top up. Weight also changes the cost of delivery.

Common mistakes

  • Entering depth in inches and area in square metres without checking the unit selectors.
  • Using a 6-inch layer for weed control. Thicker is not better.
  • Forgetting to count how many bags you can fit in the car.
  • Not allowing for settling.

How to read your result

The large number is the mulch needed in cubic feet, with cubic yards, cubic metres and litres below it, and an estimated cost if you entered a price. Use cubic yards for bulk orders and cubic feet or litres to count bags.

Common questions

Does mulch type change how much I need?

The volume calculation is the same regardless of mulch type, but denser materials such as rubber mulch may settle less than lighter organic mulches, which affects how often you need to top it up.

Should I remove old mulch before adding new?

Not necessarily. A thin existing layer can often be topped up rather than replaced, as long as the combined depth does not get excessive.

How deep should mulch be?

About 2 to 4 inches (5 to 10 cm) is the usual range. Thinner for fine or composted mulch, thicker for coarse wood chips, and never piled against trunks or stems.

How many bags are in a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, so it is 13.5 bags of 2 cubic feet or 9 bags of 3 cubic feet.

How much does a cubic yard cover?

About 324 square feet divided by the depth in inches: 162 square feet at 2 inches, 108 at 3 inches and 81 at 4 inches.

How much extra should I buy?

Around 10% extra allows for settling and uneven coverage.
