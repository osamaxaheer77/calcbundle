Mulch Calculator

Estimate how much mulch you need to cover a garden bed or landscaped area, plus an optional cost estimate.

How much mulch to use

Volume needed is simply area × depth. A 2 to 4 inch (5 to 10 cm) layer is the common recommendation — thick enough to suppress weeds and retain moisture, but not so thick that water and air can't reach the soil.

Buying mulch

Mulch is commonly sold in bulk by the cubic yard, or in bags measured in cubic feet or liters. Compare the total cubic yardage from this calculator against your supplier's bag size or bulk delivery minimum to figure out how many bags (or how many truckloads) to order.

For irregular garden beds, use our Area Calculator to find the total square footage first, then enter that area here.

A worked example

To cover 200 square feet with a 2-inch layer of mulch you need 33.33 cubic feet, which is 1.23 cubic yards, or 0.94 cubic meters. If the mulch is sold in 2-cubic-foot bags, that is 17 bags. At $30 per cubic yard, bulk mulch for the job costs about $37, so for larger beds bulk delivery is usually far cheaper than bags.

Tips for mulching

A layer of 2 to 3 inches works for most beds. Keep mulch a few inches away from tree trunks and plant stems to prevent rot. Replace or top up an inch each year as mulch breaks down. Measure irregular beds by splitting them into rectangles and circles and adding the areas, and buy about 10 percent extra to allow for settling.

Common questions

Does mulch type change how much I need?

The volume calculation is the same regardless of mulch type, but denser materials (like rubber mulch) may settle less over time than lighter organic mulches, which can affect how often you need to top it up.

Should I remove old mulch before adding new?

Not necessarily — a thin existing layer can often be topped up rather than replaced, as long as the combined depth doesn't get excessive.
