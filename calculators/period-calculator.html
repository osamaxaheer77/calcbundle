Period Calculator

Estimate your next several period dates and most probable ovulation windows from your last period and cycle length.

Next 6 Cycles

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Each period is projected by adding your average cycle to the last start date, and ovulation is estimated 14 days before the next period. A period starting September 24 with a 28-day cycle is next due Oct 22, 2026, with ovulation around Oct 6 to Oct 10.

What the calculator does

Enter the first day of your last period, how many days it lasted and the average length of your cycles. The calculator shows when your next period is likely to start and end, the most probable ovulation days for that cycle, and a table for the next six cycles. It is meant for tracking and planning, not for medical or contraceptive use.

How the dates are projected

Each future period is found by repeatedly adding your average cycle length to the start date of the last one. The period itself runs for the number of days you entered, counting the first day as day 1. Ovulation for each cycle is estimated about 14 days before the next period starts, and the tool shows a window of 2 days either side. The Ovulation Calculator uses the same rule.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens with a last period that began on September 24, 2026, lasted 5 days, and a cycle of 28 days. The first period is September 24 to 28. The next one starts 28 days later, on Oct 22, 2026 and runs to Oct 26, 2026. Ovulation for the cycle that ends then is estimated 14 days earlier, Oct 8, 2026, so the most probable ovulation days are Oct 6 – Oct 10, the window of 2 days each side.

Different cycle lengths

A cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. Normal cycles vary, and ovulation moves with them, because the day that follows ovulation is fairly fixed while the days before it vary. With the same September 24 start:

Period length

Period length changes only the end date. With the September 24 start, a period of 3 days ends on Sep 26, 5 days on Sep 28 and 7 days on Sep 30. It does not move the next start or ovulation.

Why these are estimates

Cycle length varies from month to month for most people. Stress, illness, travel and other changes can shift the timing. The further ahead a projected cycle is, the more variation can add up. If your real cycle is 2 days longer or shorter than the average, the error grows by 2 days each cycle, to 12 days by the sixth row.

Getting a better average

  • Record the first day of each period for several months and average the gaps.
  • If your cycles vary a lot, use the average of the last three to six, and note the range.
  • Update the last-period date each month rather than relying on the long projection.
  • Apps and temperature charts can add information, but the calculator uses dates only.

When to talk to a doctor

See a healthcare provider if your cycles are very irregular, much shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, if periods stop for months without pregnancy, if bleeding is unusually heavy or painful, or if you have other concerns about your cycle. The tool cannot diagnose anything.

Common mistakes

  • Counting from the last day of the period rather than the first.
  • Using the length of the period as the cycle length.
  • Entering a single unusual cycle as the average.
  • Relying on the later rows as though they were exact.
  • Using the result as birth control. It is not reliable for that.

How to read your result

The large range is the expected start and end of the next period, and the line below it gives the most probable ovulation days. The table repeats the dates for six cycles. If your period starts earlier or later than predicted, enter the real start date and the table updates.

These dates are estimates from an average cycle. They are not medical advice or a method of contraception. If your cycles are irregular or you are worried, speak to a healthcare provider.

Common questions

What if my cycle length varies a lot month to month?

Use your typical or average length. If your cycles vary a lot, treat the later rows of the table as rough, and update the last-period date each month.

How is ovulation estimated?

About 14 days before the next period starts. The calculator shows a window of 2 days either side of that date as the most probable ovulation days.

How do I count the length of a cycle?

From the first day of one period to the first day of the next. A cycle of 28 days means the next period starts 28 days after the last one started.

Does the length of my period change the dates?

Only the end date of each period. The start of the next period and the ovulation estimate depend on the cycle length, not the number of days of bleeding.

Can I use this as birth control?

No. The estimates are for tracking and planning, not for medical or contraceptive use.

When should I see a doctor?

If your cycles are very irregular, much shorter or longer than usual, if periods stop for months without pregnancy, or if you have heavy or painful bleeding or other concerns.
