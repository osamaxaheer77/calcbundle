Boat Loan Calculator

Find the monthly payment on a boat loan, or the most boat a monthly payment will buy, with a down payment, trade-in, sales tax and fees.

Amortization Schedule

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A $35,000 boat with $7,000 down, 7% interest over 10 years has a payment of $325.10 on a $28,000 loan. Add $2,450 of tax and $2,000 of fees, and the total cost comes to $50,462.

How a boat loan is worked out

The amount you borrow is the boat price minus your down payment and any trade-in. If you choose to roll the sales tax and fees into the loan, they are added on top, which raises the payment. Otherwise you pay them up front along with the down payment. The monthly payment is the level amount that repays the loan with interest over the term, and the total cost is the price plus the interest, the tax and the fees.

Two ways to use it

  • Monthly payment from a boat price. Enter the price and see the payment you would owe.
  • Boat price from a monthly payment. Enter the payment you can afford, and the calculator works out the loan it supports and then the boat price that goes with it, after the down payment, trade-in, tax and fees. It is a good way to set a budget before you shop.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens on a $35,000 boat, a 10-year term at 7%, a $7,000 down payment, 7% sales tax, $2,000 of title and registration fees, no trade-in, and the box that rolls tax and fees into the loan unchecked. The loan is $35,000 − $7,000 = $28,000. The monthly rate is 7% ÷ 12 = 0.58333%, so the payment over 120 months is $325.10. Sales tax is $35,000 × 7% = $2,450. Because the tax and fees are not in the loan, the upfront payment is $7,000 + $2,450 + $2,000 = $11,450.

Over 120 payments you pay $39,012.45 in total, of which $11,012.45 is interest. Adding the price, interest, tax and fees gives a total cost of $50,462.45.

Rolling tax and fees into the loan

Tick the box and the loan becomes $28,000 + $2,450 + $2,000 = $32,450.00. The monthly payment rises to $376.77, and the interest to $12,762.64, but the upfront payment falls to just the $7,000 down payment. It is easier on cash today but costs $1,750.19 more in interest over the loan.

Finding the price from a payment

Switch the question and enter a payment of $350 a month, the page’s alternate default, with the same term, rate and costs. The payment supports a loan of $30,144.22, and with the $7,000 down payment the boat price is $37,144.22 (tax and fees paid up front). If the tax and fees are rolled into the loan instead, the same $350 buys a price of $32,845.07.

Costs a loan calculator does not include

  • Insurance, which lenders usually require.
  • Storage or a slip, and winter storage in cold climates.
  • Fuel, maintenance and repairs, which often add up to a notable share of the purchase price each year.
  • A trailer, safety equipment and a survey for a used boat.
  • Depreciation. A boat may be worth less than the loan balance for several years.

A note on taxes

A boat can count as a home for the mortgage interest deduction in the United States if it has sleeping, cooking and toilet facilities, as IRS Publication 936 explains. That depends on your own situation, so ask a tax adviser before you count on it.

Common mistakes

  • Looking only at the monthly payment and ignoring the term and total interest.
  • Forgetting the tax and fees, which here add $4,450 to the price.
  • Rolling tax and fees into the loan without checking the extra interest.
  • Not budgeting for insurance, storage and fuel.
  • Borrowing for so long that you owe more than the boat is worth.

How to read your result

The large number is the monthly payment or the boat price, depending on your question. Below it are the loan amount, the sales tax, the upfront payment, the total of all loan payments, the interest and the total cost, followed by a chart and a schedule. Compare offers by the total cost, not the payment alone.

This calculator gives estimates. Lenders may round differently, charge other fees or require a minimum down payment, so use your loan offer for exact figures.

Common questions

Is sales tax charged on the price before or after the trade-in?

It depends on the state. This calculator works out the tax on the full boat price, which is the safer assumption. In states that tax only the price after the trade-in, the tax will be a little lower.

What does including fees and tax in the loan do?

It adds the sales tax and fees to the amount borrowed, which raises the payment and the interest but lowers the cash you need up front.

How long are boat loan terms?

Boat loans commonly run 10 to 20 years for larger boats, which is longer than most car loans. The calculator accepts terms from 1 month to 50 years.

How is the monthly payment calculated?

The loan amount is multiplied by the payment factor i divided by 1 minus (1 + i) to the power of minus the number of months, where i is the annual rate divided by 12. For $28,000 at 7% over 10 years, that is $325.10.

Does the calculator include insurance and storage?

No. Insurance, storage, fuel and maintenance are extra costs of owning a boat that the calculator does not include.

Can I find the boat price from a payment?

Yes. Choose the second question, enter the monthly payment you can afford, and the calculator works out the loan and the boat price that go with it.
