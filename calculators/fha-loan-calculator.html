Quick answer. An FHA loan is insured by the government and charges an upfront and an annual mortgage insurance premium. On a $500,000 home with 3.5% down at 6.5% for 30 years, the loan with upfront MIP is $490,944, principal and interest is $3,103.10 a month, and the total with tax, insurance and MIP is $4,032.58.

What makes an FHA loan different

An FHA loan is not issued by the government; it is insured by the Federal Housing Administration. The insurance protects the lender if the borrower defaults, which lets lenders approve smaller down payments and lower credit scores than many conventional loans. Borrowers pay for that protection through two mortgage insurance premiums (MIP). A down payment as low as 3.5% is generally available with a qualifying credit score, and larger down payments are required for lower scores.

The two mortgage insurance charges

  • Upfront MIP: a one-time premium, 1.75% of the base loan amount (the home price minus your down payment). It is normally added to the loan rather than paid in cash, which is why the loan is larger than the price minus the down payment, and you pay interest on it.
  • Annual MIP: a yearly premium paid in monthly instalments. The calculator charges it as a flat amount, the annual percentage times the base loan amount divided by 12, for as long as you choose.

In real FHA loans the annual premium is figured on the average outstanding balance, so it falls a little each year. The calculator’s flat amount is a simple, slightly conservative estimate. Always check your lender’s Loan Estimate for the exact figures.

Worked example with the default numbers

A $500,000 home with 3.5% down ($17,500) leaves a base loan of $482,500. The upfront MIP is 1.75% of that, $8,443.75, so the loan with upfront MIP is $490,943.75. At 6.5% over 30 years the principal-and-interest payment is $3,103.10. Annual MIP at 0.55% adds $221.15 a month, property tax at 1.2% of the price adds $500.00 and home insurance of $2,500 a year adds $208.33. The total is $4,032.58 a month. Total interest over the life of the loan is $626,172.

How long annual MIP lasts

Under current FHA rules, annual MIP on a loan with more than 10% down generally ends after 11 years, and on a loan with less than 10% down it lasts for the full loan term. The calculator lets you model the full term, 11 years, 5 years, no annual MIP, or “until 78% LTV”, which mirrors older FHA cancellation rules and is useful for comparing. On the default loan the annual MIP adds up to $79,612 over 30 years, $29,191 over 11 years and $13,269 over 5 years.

FHA or conventional?

Whether an FHA loan is cheaper than a conventional loan with private mortgage insurance (PMI) depends on your down payment and credit score. PMI can usually be cancelled once your equity reaches 20%, while FHA annual MIP often lasts much longer. For borrowers with good credit and a down payment of at least 5%, a conventional loan is often cheaper over the long run; FHA tends to help borrowers with lower credit scores or smaller savings. Compare both with the Mortgage Calculator and look at the whole cost, not only the first payment.

Taxes, insurance and other costs

Besides principal, interest and MIP, the calculator includes property tax (a percentage of the price or a dollar amount), home insurance, HOA fees and any other yearly cost. The payment table can be shown by year or by month, and the payoff date is based on the start date you choose.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting that the upfront MIP is added to the loan and accrues interest.
  • Assuming annual MIP can be dropped at 20% equity like PMI. Usually it cannot.
  • Comparing monthly payments without including MIP, taxes and insurance.
  • Using the default 0.55% and 1.75% without checking the current rates for your loan.

Common questions

Can I ever remove annual MIP from an FHA loan?

Only by refinancing out of the FHA loan, often into a conventional loan once you have enough equity, unless your loan qualifies for the 11-year cutoff based on your original down payment.

Why is my loan amount higher than the house price minus my down payment?

Because the upfront MIP is financed into the loan. It is added on top of the base amount you borrow, so you pay interest on the premium itself over the life of the loan.

Is an FHA loan cheaper than a conventional loan with PMI?

It depends on your down payment and credit score. FHA MIP is often costlier over the long run than conventional PMI for borrowers with good credit, but FHA loans can be easier to qualify for. Compare both for your numbers.

What is the upfront MIP based on?

It is a percentage of the base loan amount, which is the home price minus the down payment, not a percentage of the home price.

How does the calculator work out annual MIP?

It multiplies the base loan amount by the annual MIP rate and divides by 12, a flat monthly amount for as long as you choose. Real FHA loans base the premium on the average outstanding balance, so the actual cost is slightly lower.

What is the minimum down payment?

FHA loans allow as little as 3.5% down for borrowers who meet the credit score requirement. Lower credit scores generally need a larger down payment.
