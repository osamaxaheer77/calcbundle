Quick answer. Ideal weight formulas estimate a reference weight from height and gender. For a man who is 5 ft 10 in they give 155.0 to 165.3 lb, depending on the formula, while a healthy BMI range for that height is 129 to 174 lb.

Where ideal weight formulas come from

“Ideal body weight” formulas were first developed to help doctors estimate medicine doses, not to set a cosmetic target. Four are in common use. They were derived from different groups of people, so they never agree exactly. The calculator shows all four next to each other, plus the weight range that corresponds to a healthy BMI for your height.

The four formulas

Each one starts from a base weight for a height of 5 feet and adds a set amount for every inch above 5 feet (weights in kilograms):

FormulaMenWomen
Robinson (1983)52 kg + 1.9 kg per inch49 kg + 1.7 kg per inch
Miller (1983)56.2 kg + 1.41 kg per inch53.1 kg + 1.36 kg per inch
Devine (1974)50 kg + 2.3 kg per inch45.5 kg + 2.3 kg per inch
Hamwi (1964)48 kg + 2.7 kg per inch45.5 kg + 2.2 kg per inch

They only apply from 5 feet up, which is why the calculator asks for a height of at least 5 ft. Hamwi (1964) and Devine (1974) are the oldest; Robinson and Miller (both 1983) were later adjustments.

Worked example: a man who is 5 ft 10 in

He is 10 inches over 5 feet. Devine: 50 + 2.3 × 10 = 73 kg, which is 160.9 lb. The other formulas give 156.5 (Robinson), 155.0 (Miller) and 165.3 lb (Hamwi). They sit within about 10 lb of one another, while the healthy BMI range for 5 ft 10 in is 129 to 174 lb.

Worked example: a woman who is 5 ft 5 in

She is 5 inches over 5 feet. Devine: 45.5 + 2.3 × 5 = 57 kg, or 125.7 lb. Robinson gives 126.8 lb, Miller 132.1 lb and Hamwi 124.6 lb. The healthy BMI range for her height is 111 to 150 lb.

What these formulas leave out

  • Only height and gender go in. Age, frame size, muscle and body composition are ignored.
  • A muscular person can weigh more than every formula and still be healthy.
  • The formulas were developed in the 1960s to 1980s on groups that may not match you.
  • Weight does not show where fat is stored. Waist size and body fat give a clearer picture.

Ideal weight or healthy range?

The BMI range is based on categories used by the CDC: a BMI of 18.5 up to 25 is a healthy weight for adults. It is a wide band because people of the same height differ a lot in build. In practice, a range is more useful than a single number. If you are inside it, a few pounds either way matter little. If you are outside it, the other measures (waist, body fat, blood pressure, labs) tell you whether it matters for you.

Common mistakes

  • Treating one formula as the target. They differ by about 10 lb at 5 ft 10 in.
  • Applying the formulas below 5 feet, where they do not work.
  • Using the result for children or teenagers, who need growth charts.
  • Ignoring muscle. An athlete can sit above the formulas in perfect health.

How to read your result

Look at where your weight sits relative to the band formed by the four formulas and the BMI range, not at one number. If you are far outside the BMI range or have health concerns, talk to a doctor. For help setting a calorie target for any weight change, use the Calorie Calculator.

These formulas are reference points for adults and are not medical advice or a recommended goal.

Common questions

Why does age not change the result?

These formulas use only height and gender. Healthy weight can shift with age, muscle mass and frame size, which is why the BMI range and a health professional's advice matter as well.

Which ideal weight formula is the best?

None is definitively correct. Devine is the most widely used in clinical settings because of its long history in dose calculations, but the four formulas are independent estimates and the differences between them are normal.

Is ideal body weight the same as a healthy weight?

No. Ideal body weight formulas give one number. A healthy weight is a range, such as the BMI range of 18.5 to under 25 for adults. Many healthy people are not at the “ideal” number.

Why do the formulas stop at 5 feet?

They were built as a base weight at 5 feet plus an amount per extra inch. For shorter heights the equation would give less than the base, and it has not been validated there.

Can I use this for children?

No. Children and teens are assessed with growth charts for their age and sex, not with adult formulas.

Should I try to reach the ideal weight?

Use it as a reference, not a target to chase. Talk to a doctor if you are unsure what weight is right for you, especially if you have a health condition.
