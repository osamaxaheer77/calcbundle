By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A rental's return comes from cash flow and from selling. The default $200,000 property with 20% down and $2,000 rent has a 8.05% cap rate, a year-1 cash flow of $4,589, and a 18.42% IRR over 20 years.

The three numbers that actually matter

  • Cap rate = net operating income ÷ purchase price. It shows how the property performs on its own, ignoring financing.
  • Cash-on-cash return = cash flow ÷ cash you put in. It shows how your actual cash performs.
  • IRR is the yearly return across the whole holding period, including the money you get when you sell.

Worked example with the default numbers

A $200,000 property bought with 20% down ($40,000), a 6% 30-year mortgage, $6,000 of closing costs, rent of $2,000 a month rising 3% a year, 5% vacancy, property tax $3,000, insurance $1,200, maintenance $2,000 and other costs $500 (each rising 3% a year), held for 20 years and sold with 3% yearly appreciation and 8% selling costs. Your cash in is $46,000.

Year 1. Rent $24,000 less 5% vacancy ($1,200) is $22,800. Operating expenses are $6,700, so net operating income (NOI) is $16,100 and the cap rate is 8.05%. The mortgage payment is $959.28 a month ($11,511 a year), so cash flow is $4,589, a first-year cash-on-cash return of 9.98%.

Over 20 years. Total rental income is $612,645, mortgage payments $230,227, operating expenses $180,032 and total NOI $432,613. When sold after 20 years you receive $245,919 after selling costs and the remaining loan. Total profit is $402,304 and the IRR is 18.42% a year.

Why NOI leaves out the mortgage

Net operating income deliberately ignores financing. Two investors could buy the identical property, one with cash and one with a big loan, and NOI would be the same for both. That makes it a fair way to compare properties. Cash flow is what is left after the mortgage, which is why a property with a great cap rate can still have thin or negative cash flow if it is financed aggressively.

Why early years can show a poor return

Selling costs several percent of the price and the mortgage balance shrinks slowly at first. Sell soon after buying and those costs plus the large balance can outweigh the equity and cash collected, giving a low or negative return that improves the longer you hold.

Leverage: cash versus a mortgage

With a loan, your cash investment is only a fraction of the price, so the same cash flow is a much larger percentage of your money. The cost is risk: the mortgage must be paid even if the rent stops.

Vacancy, management and interest rates

Vacancy matters: at 0% vacancy year 1 cash flow is $5,789 and at 12% it is $2,909. A property manager charging 8% of rent would cut year 1 cash flow to $2,765. Higher mortgage rates also reduce cash flow:

What the calculator leaves out

  • Income tax on rent and on the gain when you sell, and depreciation. The IRS explains rental income and expenses in Publication 527.
  • Capital expenses such as a new roof, which maintenance estimates may not cover.
  • Changes in the interest rate on an adjustable loan.
  • The time and effort of being a landlord.

Common mistakes

  • Using an optimistic rent and zero vacancy.
  • Leaving out maintenance, insurance and property tax increases.
  • Ignoring selling costs when judging a short hold.
  • Comparing cap rates across very different markets.

How to read your result

The large number is the IRR over your holding period. Below it are total profit, cash-on-cash return, the year-1 cap rate, totals for income, mortgage and expenses, a first-year income table and a year-by-year breakdown. Change one assumption at a time and watch which one moves the IRR most.

Common questions

What's a "good" cap rate?

It varies heavily by market and property type. A stable, low-risk market often has lower cap rates, while a higher-risk or less liquid market demands a higher one. What matters is comparing against similar properties in the same area.

Why does cash-on-cash return look so much higher than the cap rate?

Leverage. Financing most of the purchase means your cash investment is a fraction of the price, so the same cash flow is a much larger percentage of that smaller amount of cash.

Does the management fee apply even if I self-manage?

Only if you choose to model one. Leaving it at 0% assumes you handle tenants, maintenance and administration yourself.

What happens if I hold longer than the mortgage term?

Once the loan is paid off, the mortgage payment drops out of the cash flow, so cash flow jumps because you only cover operating expenses on a property you own outright.

Why is the IRR low or negative for a short holding period?

Selling costs and a large remaining loan balance can outweigh the equity and cash collected in the first years, so the return improves the longer you hold.

Does the calculator include taxes?

No. It does not include income tax on rent, depreciation or tax on the gain at sale. See IRS Publication 527 and a tax professional.

Sources: IRS: Publication 527, Residential Rental Property; IRS: Topic no. 414, Rental income and expenses
