Surface Area Calculator

Pick a shape, enter its dimensions in any mix of units, and get the total surface area with each part broken out.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Surface area is the total area of the outside of a solid. A sphere of radius 2 m has 50.27 m², a closed cylinder of radius 3 m and height 5 m has 150.80 m², and a 3 m cube has 54 m².

What surface area is for

Surface area is the total area of the outside of a solid shape, in square units. It answers practical questions: how much paint covers a tank, how much wrapping paper a box needs, how much sheet metal makes a can, or how much material a tent uses. Choose a shape, enter its dimensions in any mix of units, and the calculator shows the total and, where it helps, the separate parts such as top, bottom and sides, so you can leave out the part you do not need.

Worked example: painting a tank

A closed cylinder with a radius of 3 m and a height of 5 m has a top and a bottom of π × 3² = 28.27 m² each, and a curved side of 2 × π × 3 × 5 = 94.25 m². The total is 150.80 m². The calculator lists the top, bottom and lateral parts separately, so for an open tank you simply drop the top: 122.52 m². For a cube with an edge of 3 m the answer is 6 × 3² = 54 m², and a rectangular tank of 2 × 3 × 4 m has 2 × (2×3 + 2×4 + 3×4) = 52 m².

Cones, pyramids and the slant height

The sloping side of a cone or pyramid is longer than its vertical height. The calculator finds the slant height for you from the right triangle made by the height and the base radius (for a cone), or the height and half the base edge (for a square pyramid). For a cone with a radius of 3 m and a height of 4 m, the slant height is √(3² + 4²) = 5 m, so the sloping side has an area of π × 3 × 5 = 47.12 m² and the total with the base is 75.40 m². A square pyramid with a base edge of 4 m and a height of 5 m has a slant height of √(2² + 5²) = 5.39 m, a sloping area of 43.08 m² and a total of 59.08 m².

Capsule, frustum and spherical cap

  • Capsule. A cylinder of height 5 m with a hemisphere on each end, radius 2 m: the two hemispheres together are 4πr² = 50.27 m² and the middle is 2πrh = 62.83 m², a total of 113.10 m².
  • Conical frustum. A cone with its top cut off, with top radius 2, bottom radius 4 and height 5 m: top 12.57, bottom 50.27 and slanted side 101.51, a total of 164.34 m².
  • Spherical cap. A slice of a ball. You give any two of the base radius, the ball radius and the height. With base radius 3 and height 1 the ball radius is (1² + 3²) ÷ 2 = 5, so the area is π×9 + 2π×1×5 = 59.69 m². If you give the base radius and the ball radius, there can be two caps (a small one and a large one), and the tool shows both.

The ellipsoid is an approximation

There is no simple exact formula for the surface area of a general ellipsoid. The calculator uses an approximation with an exponent of p = 1.6, which has a relative error of at most about 1.2%, and it marks the answer as approximate. For the default axes 2, 3 and 4 m it gives about 111.57 m². For a sphere (all three axes equal) it is exact.

Mixing units

Each dimension has its own unit box, so you can mix them. A tank 6 feet long, 2 metres wide and 1 metre high works in either unit: the result is 14.97 m², and the tool also shows it in square feet, 161.17 ft², since you used both units. The answer for each unit is worked out by converting every dimension to that unit first.

Why the sphere is special

Of all shapes enclosing the same volume, the sphere has the smallest surface area. For 1,000 cm³, a sphere has a surface area of 483.60 cm², a cylinder as tall as it is wide 553.58 cm² and a cube 600.00 cm². That is why drops and bubbles are round, and why a spherical tank needs the least material for its volume.

For wrapping paper, add a margin to the box area for overlaps, since the formula gives only the area of the box itself.

Surface area and volume

Surface area grows with the square of the size and volume with the cube. Doubling every dimension of a cube from 3 m to 6 m raises the surface area from 54 to 216 m², which is four times as much, but the volume rises from 27 to 216 m³, eight times as much. That is why small objects lose heat faster for their size, and why large tanks need less skin per litre. The Volume Calculator handles the cubic side.

Common mistakes

  • Using the height instead of the slant height for a cone or pyramid. The tool finds the slant height for you.
  • Counting the base when a tank is open at the top. Use the part list to leave it out.
  • Giving the diameter where the radius is expected. Check the label on each box.
  • Mixing up surface area (square units) with volume (cubic units).
  • Treating the ellipsoid result as exact.

How to read your result

The large number is the total surface area with its unit, and the rows beneath it show the separate parts. A formula box repeats the equation used. At the bottom the same area appears in square metres, centimetres, feet, inches, yards and acres.

Results are exact for the formulas shown, except the ellipsoid, which is an approximation. Allow extra for waste when you buy paint or sheet material.

Common questions

What is surface area?

It is the total area of the outside of a solid shape, measured in square units. It tells you how much paint, wrapping or sheet material covers the shape.

How do I find the surface area of a cylinder?

Add the top and bottom, 2 times pi times r squared, to the curved side, 2 times pi times r times h. For a radius of 3 and a height of 5 that is about 150.80 square units.

What is slant height?

It is the length of the sloping side of a cone or pyramid, measured along the surface. The calculator works it out from the height and the base size.

Why is the ellipsoid answer approximate?

There is no simple exact formula for a general ellipsoid. The calculator uses an approximation whose error is at most about 1.2 percent.

Can I mix units?

Yes. Each dimension has its own unit box. The result is shown in each unit you used.

Which shape has the smallest surface area for its volume?

The sphere. For a volume of 1,000 cubic centimetres a sphere has about 484 square centimetres of surface, against 600 for a cube.
