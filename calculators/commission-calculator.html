Define the tiered commission structure below.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Commission is a percentage of the sale. 3% of a $200,000 sale is $6,000. In a tiered plan paying 3% on the first $20,000 and 5% above that, the same sale earns $9,600.

Simple commission

A basic sales commission is a percentage of the sale. Commission = sales price × commission rate. If you know the commission and the rate, divide the commission by the rate to find the sales price; if you know the commission and the sales price, divide to find the rate. Fill in any two of the three boxes and the calculator finds the third and shows the working.

With the defaults, a $200,000 sale at 3% pays $6,000. Going the other way, a $6,000 commission at 3% means a sales price of $200,000, and $6,000 on a $200,000 sale is a rate of 3%. Another example: a flat 5% on $20,000 of sales is $1,000.

Tiered commission

Many pay plans use tiers, so the rate changes as sales grow. In a tiered plan each rate applies only to the part of the sales inside its range. With the default plan, 3% on the first $20,000 and 5% above that, a $200,000 sale earns 3% of $20,000 ($600) plus 5% of the remaining $180,000 ($9,000), a total of $9,600.

Enter the upper limit of each tier and its commission, and leave the last upper limit empty for no cap. You can set up to eight tiers, and each can be a percentage or a flat dollar amount.

Flat-dollar tiers and base commission

  • Flat-dollar tier: a tier set in dollars pays that amount once when sales reach into the tier. If the first tier paid $500 flat instead of 3%, the same $200,000 sale would pay $9,500 ($500 plus $9,000).
  • Base commission: some plans add a fixed amount or a percentage of the whole sale on top. A $1,000 base added to the default plan makes $10,600.
  • No tiers: if the commission does not change with the price, choose No for the varying option and enter a single rate or amount.

Because only the part above each limit earns the higher rate, the overall rate you earn, commission divided by sales, rises gradually. For the default plan:

Which plan is better?

To compare a tiered plan with a flat rate, find the sale size where they pay the same. The default plan beats a flat 4% as soon as the sale is above $40,000, because 600 + 5% of the amount above $20,000 overtakes 4% of the whole sale. For smaller sales the flat 4% pays more. Use the first calculator for the flat rate and the second for the tiers, with the same sales price.

A three-tier example

Suppose a plan pays 3% on the first $20,000, 4% from $20,000 to $100,000 and 6% above $100,000. A $200,000 sale earns $600 + $3,200 + $6,000 = $9,800.

Commission and tax

Commissions are pay, and are generally taxed as income. Employers often withhold on them as supplemental wages, and the IRS lists commissions among the payments covered by its withholding rules in Publication 15. Use the Take-Home Pay or Income Tax calculators to see what you keep.

What real plans add

  • Caps on the total commission.
  • Clawbacks if a sale is cancelled.
  • Splits between several people.
  • Draws against future commission.

This calculator shows the arithmetic only, so check your own plan for the exact terms.

Common mistakes

  • Applying the top tier rate to the whole sale instead of only the part above the limit.
  • Mixing up the rate in percent and as a decimal.
  • Forgetting a base commission in the total.
  • Entering the last tier’s upper limit when the plan has no cap. Leave it empty.

How to read your result

For simple commission, the large number is the missing value with the steps below it. For a tiered plan, the large number is the total commission, followed by a line for each tier or base amount and a total. Compare it with a flat rate to see what the tiers are worth to you.

Common questions

How do I calculate a commission?

Multiply the sales price by the commission rate as a decimal. A 3% commission on a $200,000 sale is 200,000 times 0.03, which is $6,000.

How does a tiered commission work?

The sales are split into ranges, and each range has its own rate. The commission is the sum of what each range earns. Only the part of the sale inside a range is paid at that range's rate.

What is a base commission?

A base commission is an amount paid on top of any other commission, either a fixed dollar amount or a percentage of the sale price. It is added to the tiered or fixed commission.

What does a dollar amount tier mean?

A tier set in dollars pays that flat amount once the sale reaches into the tier. It does not grow with the part of the sale inside the tier.

How do I find a commission rate?

Divide the commission by the sales price and multiply by 100. Enter the sales price and the commission and leave the rate empty.

Is commission taxed?

Yes. Commission is income. Employers often withhold on it as supplemental wages, and the IRS covers commissions in its withholding guidance.

Sources: IRS: Publication 15, Employer's Tax Guide (commissions and supplemental wages)
