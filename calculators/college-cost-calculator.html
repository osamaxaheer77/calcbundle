College Cost Calculator

Project what college will cost when your student starts, and work out the monthly saving needed to cover all of it or a share.

Use 0% tax for savings held in a 529 plan.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. College prices rise each year, so the bill depends on when your student starts. At $30,990 a year today, 5% yearly increases and a start in 3 years, four years cost $154,625 in total, or about $1,773 a month to save for it in full.

How the cost is projected

College prices tend to rise faster than most things, so the calculator grows today’s yearly cost by the rate you enter. If college starts in three years, the first year costs today’s figure grown for three years, the next year grows once more, and so on for each year of study. Adding those years gives the total you would pay at the time.

Worked example with the default numbers

Today’s annual cost is $30,990, the average of a four-year in-state public college. It rises 5% a year, college lasts 4 years and starts in 3 years. The first year costs $35,875, then $37,669, $39,552 and $41,530, for a total of $154,625.

Today’s money and the monthly saving

A dollar saved today can earn a return before it is spent. The calculator takes your return rate, removes the tax rate, and uses what is left (5% less 25% tax is a 3.75% after-tax return) to bring each year’s cost back to today’s value, assuming each bill is paid at the start of its year. For the full cost, that is $130,836 in today’s money. The monthly saving is the level amount you would put away each month, from now until the last year of college is paid (84 months), so that your savings plus their growth cover the target: $1,773 a month for the full cost.

Paying for part of it

Many families expect grants, scholarships, student loans or other aid to cover part of the bill. Enter the share you plan to pay from savings. With the default 35%, you will need to save $54,119 in total, a target of $45,792 in today’s money, or $621 a month. The freshman-year amount from savings is $12,556. Any balance you already have, valued today, comes off the target first; if it is large enough the calculator says your savings already cover it.

Cost growth and tax

Use a tax rate of 0% if the money is in a 529 plan, where growth is tax-free when spent on qualified education costs. The default tax of 25% requires $1,773 a month; with 0% tax it needs $1,752.

What the calculator assumes

  • A steady return and a steady cost increase every year.
  • Each year's bill is paid at the start of the year.
  • Savings are made monthly through the end of college.
  • It does not include aid you may receive or loan interest on any share you borrow.

Common mistakes

  • Using today's price for a student who starts in many years.
  • Assuming a high return will always be earned.
  • Forgetting that tax on returns reduces growth in taxable accounts.
  • Ignoring the other costs of college besides tuition: housing, food, books and travel.

How to read your result

The first block is the cost if you pay in full, with the total cost, the cost in today’s money and the monthly saving. The second block shows the same figures for the share you chose. Try a different start date or a different return to see which makes the largest difference to the monthly amount.

This is a planning estimate. Real returns, costs and aid vary. It is not financial advice.

Common questions

How do I estimate college costs for the future?

Take today's yearly cost, grow it by the expected yearly increase for each year until the student starts, and then keep growing it for each year of study. The calculator does this and adds up all the years.

What does total college cost in today's money mean?

It is the value today of all the future bills, discounted by your after-tax investment return. It is the amount that, if saved now and invested, would grow to pay for college.

How is the monthly saving calculated?

It is the level monthly amount, from now until the last year of college, that together with its after-tax growth reaches the target in today's money. A balance you already have is taken off the target first.

What tax rate should I use?

Use your combined federal, state and local tax rate on investment returns. If you save in a 529 plan and spend the money on qualified education costs, use 0%.

Why does it say my balance is enough already?

The savings you already have, valued today, are at least as large as the target in today's money. With that balance invested at your return rate, it should cover the goal without further saving.

Where do the average costs come from?

The presets are yearly budgets of the kind published in the College Board's Trends in College Pricing report. Check the latest report and each college's net price calculator for your own numbers.
