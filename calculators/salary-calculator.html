Salary Calculator

Turn a salary into its hourly, daily, weekly, monthly and yearly equivalents, with and without holidays and vacation days.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. To convert pay, turn it into a yearly figure and divide by the periods you want. At $50 an hour for 40 hours a week that is $104,000 a year before time off, or $94,000 a year after 10 holidays and 15 vacation days are unpaid, which is $7,833 a month.

How the conversion works

Everything is converted through a yearly figure. An hourly rate becomes a year by multiplying by your weekly hours and 52 weeks. A daily rate uses your days per week instead. A weekly, bi-weekly, semi-monthly, monthly or quarterly amount is multiplied by its number of pay periods in a year (52, 26, 24, 12 or 4). From the yearly figure the calculator divides back out to every other period.

Note the difference between bi-weekly and semi-monthly. Bi-weekly pay arrives 26 times a year, so two months each year contain three paydays. Semi-monthly pay arrives 24 times, always twice a month. The same salary is split into 26 smaller bi-weekly cheques or 24 larger semi-monthly ones.

Unadjusted vs adjusted pay

The two columns answer different questions. Unadjusted pay assumes you are paid for all 260 weekdays of the year. Adjusted pay takes off unpaid holidays and vacation days, which gives what you actually receive across the year.

  • If you enter an hourly or daily rate, it is treated as unadjusted and the adjusted column shows how much less you earn after unpaid time off.
  • If you enter a weekly, monthly or yearly salary, it is treated as already adjusted, and the unadjusted column shows the higher rate you earn on each day you actually work.

Worked example: $50 an hour

At $50 an hour, 40 hours a week and 5 days a week, with 10 holidays and 15 vacation days (the calculator’s starting values), the unadjusted year is 50 × 40 × 52 = $104,000. Time off takes (260 − 25) ÷ 260 = 90.4% of that, so the adjusted year is $94,000.

Worked example: starting from a salary

Say you earn $60,000 a year and have 25 days off. The calculator treats that as your adjusted pay. Spread over the 235 days you actually work, the unadjusted equivalent is $66,383 a year, or about $31.91 an hour on a 40-hour week. Compared with simply dividing by 2,080 hours ($28.85 an hour), the difference is what the time off is worth.

Comparing an hourly offer with a salary

A higher hourly rate does not always mean higher pay. Suppose one job pays $60,000 a year with paid time off, and another pays $30 an hour with unpaid time off. The hourly job looks bigger: 30 × 2,080 = $62,400. But with 10 holidays and 15 vacation days unpaid, the adjusted pay is $56,400, which is $3,600 less than the salary. Always compare the adjusted figures, and add benefits such as health insurance and retirement matching before deciding.

Overtime is not included in the calculator. In the United States, covered non-exempt employees are generally paid at least one and a half times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a week, so five overtime hours at $25 an hour would add about $187.50 to a week’s pay.

What this calculator does not include

  • Taxes and deductions. All figures are gross (before tax). For take-home pay use the Take-Home Pay Calculator, and for tax use the Income Tax Calculator.
  • Overtime, bonuses and commission.
  • Employer benefits such as health insurance and retirement contributions.
  • Part-year work or irregular hours.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up bi-weekly (26 cheques) and semi-monthly (24 cheques).
  • Assuming 4 weeks in a month. A month averages about 4.33 weeks (52 ÷ 12), so monthly pay is not four weekly cheques.
  • Ignoring unpaid time off when comparing an hourly job with a salaried one.
  • Comparing gross pay with a net figure from another source.

How to read your result

Use the unadjusted column to compare rates between jobs on a like-for-like basis, and the adjusted column to estimate real yearly income when time off is unpaid. If you are weighing an hourly offer against a salary, convert both to the same period and also compare benefits and taxes.

This calculator converts gross pay using standard assumptions and is not tax or financial advice.

Common questions

What is the difference between unadjusted and adjusted pay?

Unadjusted figures assume you are paid for every working day of the year. Adjusted figures subtract your holiday and vacation days. The adjusted number is a better estimate of what you receive if that time off is unpaid.

How is a monthly salary turned into an hourly rate?

Multiply by 12 to get the yearly salary, then divide by your yearly hours (weekly hours × 52). For 40 hours a week that is 2,080 hours.

Why is a bi-weekly cheque smaller than a semi-monthly one?

There are 26 bi-weekly pay periods in a year but only 24 semi-monthly ones. The same yearly salary is split into more, smaller cheques.

Does this include tax?

No. All figures are gross, before tax and deductions. Use the Take-Home Pay Calculator or Income Tax Calculator to see net pay.

How many working days are there in a year?

There are 260 weekdays in a normal year (52 weeks × 5 days). The calculator subtracts your holidays and vacation days from that number.

Can I use this for part-time work?

Yes. Enter your actual hours per week and days per week, and the conversions will follow your schedule.
