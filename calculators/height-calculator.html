Predict Height from Parents' Heights

Height Converter

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A child's adult height can be estimated from the parents. A mother of 5 ft 5 in and a father of 5 ft 10 in predict a son of 5 ft 10 in (177.8 cm) and a daughter of 5 ft 5 in (165.1 cm), give or take about 4 inches.

How the prediction works

The calculator uses the classic mid-parental height method. Add the mother’s and father’s heights in inches, then add 5 inches for a boy or subtract 5 inches for a girl, and divide by 2: boy = (mother + father + 5) ÷ 2 and girl = (mother + father − 5) ÷ 2. That is the same as taking the parents’ average and adding 2.5 inches for a boy or subtracting 2.5 for a girl, which roughly allows for the average height difference between men and women.

Worked example with the default numbers

A mother who is 5 ft 5 in (65 inches) and a father who is 5 ft 10 in (70 inches). Their average is 67.5 inches. For a son: (65 + 70 + 5) ÷ 2 = 140 ÷ 2 = 70 inches, or 5 ft 10 in (177.8 cm). For a daughter: (65 + 70 − 5) ÷ 2 = 130 ÷ 2 = 65 inches, or 5 ft 5 in (165.1 cm).

How accurate is it?

This is a rough estimate of the average height a child will reach. The American Academy of Pediatrics’ HealthyChildren site says to expect a margin of error of about 4 inches up or down. Other sources describe most children landing within 2 inches or so of the figure. Individual results vary because adult height also depends on nutrition, sleep, health and growth patterns that the parents’ heights do not show. Very tall parents tend to have children a little shorter than predicted, and very short parents children a little taller, a pattern called regression to the mean.

The same method in centimetres

If you prefer metric, 5 inches is 12.7 cm, so the formulas become boy = (mother + father + 12.7) ÷ 2 and girl = (mother + father − 12.7) ÷ 2, with heights in centimetres. For the default parents (165.1 cm and 177.8 cm) that gives 177.8 cm for a son and 165.1 cm for a daughter, matching the figures above. Some textbooks round the 12.7 to 13 cm, which changes the answer by 0.15 cm at most.

When the prediction does not apply

  • For adopted children or when a parent’s height is unknown, the method has little meaning.
  • It does not use the child’s own growth. After the first few years, a pediatrician’s growth chart of the child’s own measurements is a better guide.
  • Measured heights are better than remembered ones: people often overstate their height by an inch or so.
  • Medical conditions or medicines that affect growth can change the outcome.

Tips on accuracy

  • Use heights measured without shoes, in inches.
  • Treat the result as a range, about 4 inches either side.
  • Good nutrition, enough sleep and regular health checks help children reach their genetic potential.
  • If you are worried about a child’s growth, talk to a pediatrician rather than relying on a calculator.

Common mistakes

  • Entering both parents’ heights in the wrong units.
  • Adding 5 inches to the average instead of 2.5. The 5 applies to the sum before dividing by 2.
  • Treating the prediction as a guarantee.
  • Using the method for a child whose growth is already well tracked on a chart.

How to read your result

Common questions

Why does the formula add or subtract 5 inches?

It approximates the average height gap between adult men and women. Adding 5 to the parents' total for a boy, or subtracting 5 for a girl, and dividing by 2, centres the prediction on realistic values for each sex.

Does this work for a child who has already been born?

Yes. It is most useful for infants and very young children. As a child grows, their own growth chart becomes a better predictor than the parents' heights alone.

How accurate is the mid-parental height method?

It is a rough estimate. A margin of about 4 inches either way is commonly quoted, and many children end up closer. Nutrition, health and other factors matter too.

Can a child be taller or shorter than both parents?

Yes. The prediction is an average expectation. Individual children can be well above or below it.

How do I convert feet and inches to centimeters?

Multiply the total number of inches by 2.54. For 5 ft 5 in, that is 65 x 2.54 = 165.1 cm.

Should I use measured or remembered heights?

Measured heights, without shoes. Remembered heights are often a little too high, which makes the prediction too high.
