Quick answer. A HELOC usually has an interest-only draw period followed by a repayment period with a much higher payment. A $50,000 line at 8% with a 5-year draw and 15-year repayment costs $333.33 a month at first and $477.83 afterwards.

How a HELOC works

A home equity line of credit (HELOC) is a revolving credit line secured by your home. Unlike a home equity loan, it does not pay out a lump sum: you open a limit and borrow against it as needed. Most HELOCs have two phases. In the draw period you can borrow and often pay only interest. In the repayment period you can no longer borrow and must pay back principal and interest.

What the calculator assumes

  • The whole line is drawn at the start and held flat during the draw period. This is the maximum-interest case; borrowing less or paying down early lowers the real cost.
  • A fixed interest rate. Real HELOCs usually have variable rates, so payments can rise or fall.
  • Interest-only payments in the draw period (balance × rate ÷ 12), then a standard amortized payment over the repayment period.
  • Optional closing costs and an annual fee, used to estimate an APR.

Worked example with the default numbers

A $50,000 line at 8% with a 5-year draw period and a 15-year repayment period. During the draw period the payment is 50,000 × 8% ÷ 12 = $333.33. In the repayment period it becomes $477.83 (the balance amortized over 15 years). Over the 20 years you pay about $106,009 in total, of which $56,009 is interest.

The payment goes up by about $144.49 a month (43%) when repayment begins, because the full balance now has to be paid off in a limited time.

Adding closing costs and fees

If you add $2,000 of closing costs and a $50 annual fee during the draw period, the cost of the loan (interest plus fees) is $58,259 and the estimated APR is 8.56%, higher than the 8% rate because fees are included.

How the repayment period changes the cost

Variable rates

Because a HELOC’s rate usually moves with a benchmark, the draw-period payment can change. For the same $50,000 balance, the interest-only payment and the 15-year repayment payment at different rates are:

How much can you borrow?

The second tool estimates the limit. Lenders cap total borrowing (your existing mortgage plus the HELOC) at a share of your home’s value, called the loan-to-value (LTV) ratio. The calculator’s example: a home worth $600,000, an existing mortgage of $250,000 and an 80% LTV limit. The most total debt allowed is $480,000, so the available HELOC is about $230,000.

Risks to understand

  • Your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, the lender can foreclose.
  • Payment shock: payments are often much higher in the repayment period, and some HELOCs require a balloon payment of the whole balance.
  • Variable rates: payments can change from month to month.
  • Interest-only trap: paying only interest in the draw period does not reduce the balance.
  • Fees: check closing costs, annual fees, early-closure fees and inactivity fees.

Common mistakes

  • Looking only at the draw-period payment and ignoring the repayment period.
  • Borrowing the full limit just because it is available.
  • Not planning for a higher rate.
  • Using a HELOC for spending that does not build value, which leaves you owing against your home.

How to read your result

Compare the two payments side by side and check you could afford the higher one, preferably at a higher interest rate too. Look at the total interest and APR to compare with a home equity loan, and keep a margin below the maximum you could borrow. The Home Equity Loan Calculator shows the fixed-rate alternative.

This calculator gives estimates only. Your lender’s disclosures show the actual rate, fees and terms of the HELOC.

Common questions

Can I pay down principal during the draw period?

Yes, and it is usually a good idea. Any principal you pay in the draw period reduces both the interest charged and the balance you will have to repay later.

What happens if I do not draw the full amount?

You only owe interest on what you have actually borrowed. The calculator assumes the whole line is drawn, so a smaller balance would cost less than shown.

Why does the payment jump after the draw period?

During the draw period you may owe only interest. When repayment begins, the whole balance must be paid off over a shorter period, so the payment includes principal and rises noticeably.

Is a HELOC rate fixed or variable?

Most HELOCs have variable rates tied to a benchmark, so payments can change. The calculator uses a fixed rate for simplicity, so test a higher rate as well.

How much can I borrow with a HELOC?

Lenders usually allow total borrowing up to a share of your home's value, often around 80% to 90% including your existing mortgage. The second tool on the page estimates the limit.

What is the risk of a HELOC?

Your home secures the line, so missed payments can lead to foreclosure. Rising rates and payment shock when repayment starts are the main risks.
