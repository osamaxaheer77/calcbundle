How the U.S. Navy method works

This method estimates body fat from just a few circumference measurements — neck, waist, and (for women) hip — rather than requiring specialized equipment. It was developed at the Naval Health Research Center and remains a common estimation method precisely because it only needs a tape measure. It's an estimate, not a precise measurement — tools like bioelectrical impedance or hydrostatic testing are more accurate but far less accessible.

Why measurement technique matters

Small measuring errors get amplified by the formula's math, so consistency matters: measure to the nearest 1/4 inch (0.5 cm), keep the tape snug but not compressing the skin, and don't pull your stomach in when measuring your waist.

The "Ideal Body Fat for Age" figure comes from Jackson & Pollock reference tables — a population average, not a personal target. Athletic goals vary widely by sport and individual.

Common questions

Why does the calculator show two different body fat percentages?

The U.S. Navy (circumference) method and the BMI-based method are two independent estimation approaches. They usually land close together, but BMI doesn't distinguish fat from muscle, so the two can diverge more for very muscular or very lean people.

What counts as a healthy body fat range?

Essential fat is roughly 2-5% for men and 10-13% for women — the minimum needed for basic health. Healthy ranges above that vary by age, activity level, and goals; see the reference table for the American Council on Exercise's categories.
