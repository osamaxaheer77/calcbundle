Quick answer. The U.S. Navy method estimates body fat from height, neck and waist (plus hips for women). A 25-year-old man who is 70.5 in tall with a 19.5 in neck and a 37.5 in waist comes out at about 15.3% body fat, which is 23.2 lb of fat and 128.8 lb of lean mass at 152 lb.

How the U.S. Navy method works

The Navy method estimates body fat from tape measurements instead of special equipment. It was developed for military fitness screening and is popular because all you need is a tape measure. It uses the logarithm of your measurements in inches:

  • Men: % body fat = 86.010 × log10(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log10(height) + 36.76
  • Women: % body fat = 163.205 × log10(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log10(height) − 78.387

The calculator also shows a second estimate based on BMI and age (1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 16.2 for men, − 5.4 for women) and compares your result with the average figure for your age.

Worked example

A 25-year-old man who weighs 152 lb, is 70.5 in tall, with a 19.5 in neck and a 37.5 in waist (the values the calculator starts with):

For comparison, a 30-year-old woman who is 65 in tall with a 13 in neck, 29 in waist and 38 in hips gets about 27.3% from the same method.

Why measurement technique matters

The formula uses the difference between waist and neck, so small tape errors move the answer a lot. In the example, a waist reading one inch larger raises the estimate by about two percentage points:

  • Measure the neck just below the larynx, looking straight ahead.
  • Measure the waist at the navel for men and at the narrowest point for women, relaxed and without pulling your stomach in.
  • Measure the hips (women) at the widest point.
  • Round to the nearest quarter inch (or half centimetre) and take each measurement twice.

Limits of the Navy method

  • It is an estimate. Compared with a DEXA scan or other clinical methods, it can be off by several percentage points for an individual.
  • It works less well for people who are very lean, very muscular or very heavy, and for pregnant women.
  • It does not tell you where fat is stored, which matters for health. Waist size alone is a useful extra number.
  • Use it to track changes over time with the same tape and technique, not to compare yourself with a number from another method.

Common mistakes

  • Entering feet and inches in the wrong boxes.
  • Measuring the waist at the belt line instead of at the navel (men) or narrowest point (women).
  • Pulling the stomach in while measuring.
  • Expecting the Navy and BMI-based results to match exactly. They use different inputs.

How to read your result

Look at the category first, then at fat mass and lean mass in pounds. If you are trying to change your body composition, repeat the measurement every two to four weeks under the same conditions and watch the trend. If your result is in the essential-fat range or in the obese range, or you have health concerns, talk to a doctor, who can measure more directly.

This calculator gives an estimate for adults and is not medical advice. BMI and tape formulas cannot replace a clinical assessment.

Common questions

Why does the calculator show two different body fat percentages?

The Navy (tape measurement) method and the BMI-based method are independent estimates. They are often close, but BMI cannot tell fat from muscle, so the two can differ more for very muscular or very lean people.

What counts as a healthy body fat range?

Essential fat is roughly 2 to 5% for men and 10 to 13% for women. Above that, healthy ranges depend on age, activity and goals. The category table above shows the ACE ranges used by the calculator.

How accurate is the Navy method?

It is a useful estimate but can differ from clinical methods such as a DEXA scan by several percentage points. It is best for tracking your own changes over time using the same measuring technique.

Why do women need hip measurements?

The women's formula includes the hips because fat is distributed differently. Leave the hip box out of mind for men: it is only used for the women's calculation.

How often should I measure?

Every two to four weeks is enough. Measure at the same time of day, ideally in the morning, so that water and food do not distort the numbers.

Can I use this during pregnancy or as a teenager?

No. The formulas were built for healthy adults. Pregnant women and people under 18 should rely on advice from a doctor.
