Square Footage Calculator

Enter the length and width of the space in consistent units. If estimating material costs, account separately for waste, offcuts and irregular borders.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Square footage is length times width. A room 30 feet by 20 feet is 600 square feet, which is 66.67 square yards or 55.74 square metres. At $5 per square foot the materials cost $3,000 before waste.

A simple length times width calculation

For a rectangular space, square footage is length × width, with both measured in feet. The calculator converts other units for you (inches, yards, metres and centimetres) and lets you set a quantity to total several identical rooms or sections at once. It reports the total in square feet, square yards and square metres, and, if you enter a price, a rough material cost.

Worked example with the default numbers

A space 30 feet long and 20 feet wide has 30 × 20 = 600 square feet. That is 66.67 square yards (divide by 9) or 55.74 square metres (multiply by 0.092903). For flooring at $5 per square foot the material costs $3,000 before waste, taxes and labour. With 3 identical rooms, set the quantity to 3 for 1,800 square feet in total.

A smaller example: a 12-foot by 15-foot room is 180 square feet, and at $3 per square foot the material cost is $540 before taxes, labour or extra waste.

Mixing units

Each dimension is converted separately before the area is calculated, so you can enter the length in metres and the width in feet. A room 10 metres by 12 feet is 32.81 × 12 = 393.7 square feet.

Estimating cost

Enter a price per square foot for flooring, turf, paint coverage or other material and the calculator multiplies it by the total area. It is an estimate only: always check the actual coverage rates and add a margin for waste and cuts, usually 5 to 10 percent, and more for diagonal patterns.

Irregular rooms

  • Measure each wall at its longest point and split L-shaped rooms into rectangles.
  • For triangles, circles or other shapes use the Area Calculator, which supports more shapes.
  • Do not include closets unless they will get the same flooring.
  • Subtract a built-in feature, such as a kitchen island, only if it will not be covered.

Square footage in real estate

Listings follow local rules and industry standards and can differ from your own measurement. The ANSI standard used by many appraisers for single-family homes measures finished above-grade area to the outside of the exterior walls and reports basement area separately, so a listing can show a larger figure than the floor area you would carpet. Always measure the actual floor you plan to cover.

Larger areas and price per square foot

For land, one acre is 43,560 square feet, so a quarter-acre lot is 10,890 square feet. To compare homes, divide the price by the area: a $360,000 house with 1,800 square feet costs $200 per square foot, and one with 2,400 square feet at the same price costs $150. Compare homes using the same measuring rule, since listings may include or leave out areas such as basements and garages.

Measuring tips

  • Use a tape measure or laser measure along the floor, not the top of a baseboard, and round to the nearest half inch.
  • Measure twice, and measure the longest and shortest points of a room that is not perfectly square.
  • Write each dimension down in one unit before entering it, to avoid mixing feet and inches in one box.

Where square footage is used

  • Pricing flooring, paint, carpet and tile.
  • Comparing apartments and homes.
  • Sizing heating and cooling.
  • Estimating land and garden areas.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing square feet and linear feet.
  • Forgetting to multiply by the quantity of rooms.
  • Using the listing's square footage to buy flooring.
  • Not allowing for waste.

Common questions

Can I mix units for length and width?

Yes. Each dimension converts independently before the area is calculated, so entering the length in feet and the width in metres works correctly.

How do I calculate an irregular room?

Split it into rectangles or other simple shapes, calculate each separately and add the results together.

How do I convert square feet to square metres?

Multiply by 0.092903. For 600 square feet that is 55.74 square metres. To go the other way, multiply square metres by 10.7639.

How much extra material should I buy?

Usually 5 to 10 percent for cuts and mistakes, and more for diagonal or patterned layouts.

Why does the listing say a different square footage?

Real estate listings follow local rules and measurement standards, often measuring to the outside of the walls, so they can differ from the floor area you measure inside.

What does the quantity field do?

It multiplies the area by the number of identical rooms or sections, so you can total them in one step.
