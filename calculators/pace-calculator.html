Quick answer. Pace is time divided by distance. A 5 km run in 50 minutes 25 seconds is a pace of 10:05 per kilometre (16:14 per mile), or 5.95 km/h, and holding that pace for a 10K would take 1:40:50.

Pace, speed and time

Pace is how long it takes to cover a set distance, such as minutes per mile or per kilometre. Speed is the distance covered in a set time, such as miles or kilometres per hour. Runners and walkers usually use pace and cyclists use speed, but they describe the same thing: a faster pace is a smaller time per distance and a higher speed.

The three formulas

  • Pace = time ÷ distance
  • Time = distance × pace
  • Distance = time ÷ pace

Give the calculator any two of time, distance and pace and it finds the third. It also shows pace in every unit: per mile, per kilometre, miles per hour, kilometres per hour, metres per minute and metres per second.

Worked example with the default numbers

A 5 km run in 50 minutes and 25 seconds is 3,025 seconds over 5 kilometres, so the pace is 3,025 ÷ 5 = 605 seconds per kilometre, or 10:05 per kilometre. In other units that is 16:14 per mile, 5.95 km/h, 3.70 mph, 99.2 metres per minute and 1.65 metres per second.

Splits

The splits table shows your elapsed time at each kilometre and mile for a steady pace. At 5:00 per kilometre you pass 1 km at 5:00, 2 km at 10:00, 5 km at 25:00 and 10 km at 50:00. If your first kilometre is faster than your goal pace, check your effort: starting too fast is the most common reason for fading later.

Predicting longer races

Multiplying a pace over a longer distance assumes you never slow down, which is rarely true. Many runners expect to slow as distance grows. A widely used rule of thumb scales time with distance to the power of about 1.06: a 25:00 5K predicts a 10K of about 52:07, against 50:00 with constant pace, and a half marathon of about 1:55:00, against 1:45:29 with constant pace. Treat any prediction as a guide, since training, terrain and weather matter.

Training paces from a race pace

Coaches often describe training in relation to race pace. As a rough rule of thumb, easy and long runs are done 1 to 2 minutes per mile (about 40 to 75 seconds per kilometre) slower than 5K race pace, tempo runs near 10K to half-marathon race pace, and short intervals around or a little faster than 5K pace. For a runner with a 25:00 5K (5:00 per kilometre), that means easy runs near 6:00 to 6:15 per kilometre and a tempo effort near 5:10 to 5:25 per kilometre. These are starting points. Heart rate and how the effort feels matter as much as the number on the watch.

Walking and cycling

The calculator works for any steady-effort activity. A brisk walk of 15 minutes per kilometre is 4 km/h, and a leisurely cycle of 3 minutes per kilometre is 20 km/h. Use the unit that fits your sport, and the converter if you need to compare pace with speed.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing units: a pace per mile and a distance in kilometres give the wrong answer unless converted.
  • Entering time as minutes only. Use mm:ss or hh:mm:ss.
  • Expecting a steady pace over hills and in wind.
  • Using a 5K pace for a marathon. Long races need slower paces.

How to read your result

Use the pace in the unit you train in, and the splits for pacing a race. Aim to run evenly, or slightly faster in the second half. Compare your pace against your effort: easy runs should feel conversational, and your target race pace should feel controlled at the start.

Race times assume a steady pace. Real pace varies with terrain, weather, fatigue and tactics, so treat the results as planning guides.

Common questions

How do I calculate running pace?

Divide your total time by the distance. A 5 kilometre run in 50 minutes and 25 seconds is 10 minutes and 5 seconds per kilometre, or about 16 minutes and 14 seconds per mile.

How do I convert minutes per mile to miles per hour?

Divide 60 by the pace in minutes. A pace of 8 minutes per mile is 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5 miles per hour.

How do I convert pace per mile to pace per kilometre?

Divide the pace per mile by 1.609344. For example, 8:00 per mile is about 4:58 per kilometre.

Why does the finish time tool use a steady pace?

It assumes you keep the same pace for the whole distance, which is a rough guide. In a long race most people slow down, so a real finish time is usually somewhat longer.

What is a split?

A split is your elapsed time at a point in a run, usually at each kilometre or mile. Even splits mean an even pace.

Can I use this for cycling or swimming?

Yes. Pace works for any steady-effort activity. Enter the distance and time, and read the result as speed or pace in the unit you prefer.
