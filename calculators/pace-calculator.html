What pace means

Pace is how long it takes to cover a set distance, such as minutes per mile or minutes per kilometer. Runners and walkers usually think in pace, while cyclists often use speed, such as miles or kilometers per hour. The two describe the same thing from opposite sides: a faster pace is a smaller time per distance and a higher speed. The calculator shows both, along with meters per minute and meters per second.

Finding pace, time or distance

Give any two of time, distance and pace and the third follows. Pace is the time divided by the distance. The time needed is the distance multiplied by the pace. The distance covered is the time divided by the pace. The race table shows what your pace would give over common distances, and the splits show your elapsed time at each kilometer and mile.

Multipoint, converter and finish time

The multipoint tool is for runs where you know your total time at several points. It works out the pace of each section so you can see where you sped up or slowed down. The converter changes a pace between units. The finish time tool takes the distance and time you have covered and shows how long the full distance would take if you kept the same pace, which is a rough guide because pace usually drifts during a long race.

Results assume a steady pace over the whole distance. In practice pace changes with hills, weather, fatigue and race tactics, so treat the race times as a guide for planning.

Common questions

How do I calculate running pace?

Divide your total time by the distance. A 5 kilometer run in 50 minutes and 25 seconds is 10 minutes and 5 seconds per kilometer, or about 16 minutes and 14 seconds per mile.

How do I convert minutes per mile to miles per hour?

Divide 60 by the pace in minutes. A pace of 10 minutes per mile is 6 miles per hour. The converter does this for any pair of units.

What distances are used for the marathon and half-marathon?

A marathon is 42.195 kilometers, which is 26.2 miles, and a half-marathon is 21.0975 kilometers. The race table uses these exact distances.

How are the splits worked out?

The splits assume you hold the same pace the whole way. Each split is the pace multiplied by the distance so far, for every whole kilometer and every whole mile you cover.

How accurate is the finish time estimate?

It assumes you keep your current pace to the end. Most people slow down over a long race, so a longer race can take more time than the estimate suggests.
