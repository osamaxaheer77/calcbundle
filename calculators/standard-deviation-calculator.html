Standard Deviation Calculator

Use population standard deviation when the data represents the whole population, and sample standard deviation when estimating population variation from a sample. The sample formula divides by n − 1.

Margin of Error by Confidence Level

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Standard deviation shows how far values typically sit from their mean. For 10, 12, 23, 23, 16, 23, 21 and 16 the mean is 18, the population standard deviation is 4.899 and the sample standard deviation is 5.237.

What standard deviation tells you

Standard deviation measures how spread out numbers are around their mean. A small value means the numbers sit close to the average; a large value means they are scattered. It is in the same units as the data, which makes it easier to read than variance, which is in squared units.

The formulas

  • Mean: add the values and divide by how many there are (N).
  • Variance: add up the squared differences from the mean, then divide by N (population) or N − 1 (sample).
  • Standard deviation: the square root of the variance.
  • Standard error of the mean: the standard deviation divided by the square root of N.

Population or sample?

Use population when your data covers every member of the group, such as all the test scores in one class. Use sample when the data is only part of a larger group you want to learn about, such as 50 customers out of thousands. Dividing by N − 1 corrects for the fact that a sample’s own mean sits closer to its values than the true mean does, so the spread would otherwise be underestimated. For the values 2, 4 and 6 the mean is 4, the population variance is 8/3 = 2.6667 and the sample variance is 8/2 = 4. The gap between the two shrinks as N grows.

Same average, different spread

Two exam classes can both average 70, but if one class scored between 68 and 72 and the other between 30 and 100, they need different teaching. Always report spread next to the mean.

The 68-95-99.7 rule

For data that follows a bell-shaped (normal) distribution, about 68% of values lie within one standard deviation of the mean, 95% within two and 99.7% within three. The rule is a guide, not a law: skewed data or data with outliers does not follow it.

Common mistakes

  • Using the sample formula when you really have the whole population, or the other way around.
  • Reading a large standard deviation as an error. It may be a real feature of the data.
  • Applying the 68-95-99.7 rule to skewed data.
  • Entering values with a comma inside a number such as 1,200. Separate numbers with commas or spaces and write 1200.
  • Calculating a sample standard deviation from a single value, which is not defined.

How to read your result

Compare the standard deviation with the mean. Here 4.9 against a mean of 18 means a typical value is within about 4.9 of the average. A value more than two standard deviations from the mean is unusual and worth checking. Common questions

Why does sample standard deviation divide by N-1 instead of N?

This is Bessel's correction. A sample's own mean is the value that minimizes the sum of squared differences for that sample, so dividing by N slightly underestimates the true spread. Dividing by N-1 corrects the bias.

What if I only have one data point?

Sample standard deviation is undefined for a single value because it would divide by zero. The population standard deviation of a single value is 0, since the value equals the mean.

What is the difference between variance and standard deviation?

Variance is the average squared difference from the mean. Standard deviation is its square root, which puts the spread back in the units of your data.

What is the standard error of the mean?

It is the standard deviation divided by the square root of N and describes how much the sample mean would vary if you repeated the sampling. It shrinks as the sample grows.

What counts as a high standard deviation?

It is relative. Compare it with the mean: a standard deviation of 5 is large for numbers near 10 but small for numbers near 10,000.

Can standard deviation be negative?

No. It is a square root of an average of squares, so it is zero or positive. It is zero only when every value is the same.
