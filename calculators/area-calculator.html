Area Calculator

Calculate the area of seven common shapes — rectangle, triangle, trapezoid, circle, sector, ellipse, or parallelogram.

Seven shapes, seven formulas

Rectangle and parallelogram both boil down to base × height. Triangle uses Heron's formula (no angle needed, just three side lengths). Trapezoid averages its two parallel bases, multiplied by height. Circle, sector, and ellipse all trace back to π — a sector is just a circle's area scaled by what fraction of the full 360° it spans, and an ellipse is a circle stretched along two different radii.

Breaking down complex shapes

Most irregular shapes can be estimated by splitting them into a combination of these simple shapes, calculating each piece's area separately, and adding them together — the same way an L-shaped room can be measured as two rectangles.

For a triangle where you know an angle instead of all three sides, or need sides, angles, and area together, use the more complete Triangle Calculator instead.

Common questions

What's the difference between semi-major and semi-minor axes?

They're the longest and shortest radii of an ellipse, measured from its center. When they're equal, the ellipse is just a circle — that's why the circle formula (πr²) is a special case of the ellipse formula (πab).

Why does a sector need the angle in degrees or radians?

A sector's area is the fraction of the full circle it covers — that fraction is angle÷360 in degrees, or angle÷2π in radians. Make sure to pick the matching unit for whatever angle you enter.
