Quick answer. Extra payments go to principal, so they cut the interest charged on every later month and end the loan sooner. On a $400,000 6% mortgage with 25 years left, adding $500 a month pays it off 7 years 9 months earlier and saves about $122,306 of interest.

Why paying early saves money

Interest is charged on your remaining balance. An extra payment that goes to principal reduces that balance right away, so every later month is charged less interest and the loan ends sooner. The saving depends on your rate, the balance, how much time is left and when the extra money arrives. Ask your servicer to apply extra money to principal, and check whether your loan has a prepayment penalty.

The two ways to enter your loan

If you have the original paperwork, use the first tool: loan amount, original term, rate and the time remaining. If you only have a recent statement, use the second tool: unpaid balance, monthly payment and rate. Both are the same math underneath, a balance paid down at a fixed rate.

Worked example with the default numbers

A $400,000 loan at 6% for 30 years has a principal-and-interest payment of $2,398.20. With 25 years left, the unpaid balance is about $372,217. On the standard schedule the loan ends in 25 years and costs $347,243 in interest from here. Adding $500 a month to principal ends it in 17 years 3 months, 7 years 9 months sooner, and the remaining interest falls to $224,937, a saving of $122,306.

The biweekly option pays half the monthly amount every two weeks, which is 26 half-payments or 13 full payments a year. The calculator treats it as one extra monthly payment each year. Some servicers hold partial payments until a full instalment is received or charge a fee for a biweekly programme, so check before signing up. You can get the same effect on your own by adding one-twelfth of your payment to each monthly payment.

Pay it off in one go?

Paying the entire balance avoids every future interest charge, but it also uses cash that could be invested or kept as an emergency fund. The better choice depends on what your money could earn compared with your loan rate. With a 6% mortgage rate, a payoff earns a guaranteed 6% on the balance; after-tax investment returns are uncertain. Keep an emergency fund before sending extra money.

The balance-and-payment tool

The second tool starts from a statement. With a balance of $230,000, a payment of $1,500 and a rate of 6%, the loan ends in 24 years 4 months with $207,677 of interest. Adding $500 a month brings that to 14 years 4 months and $113,123.

Checks before you send extra money

  • Ask whether extra payments are applied to principal. Mark them as principal-only if needed.
  • Check for a prepayment penalty. Small extra payments usually are not affected, but a large lump sum or a full payoff within the first few years can be.
  • Make sure your emergency fund and higher-rate debts (credit cards) come first.
  • Keep paying your escrow items. Extra payments do not replace taxes and insurance.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming extra money is applied to principal automatically.
  • Paying a biweekly service fee for something you can do yourself.
  • Putting every spare dollar into the mortgage while carrying credit card debt at a much higher rate.
  • Forgetting that taxes, insurance and fees are not part of the principal-and-interest figures here.

How to read your result

Look at the new payoff time and the interest saved, then ask whether the extra payment fits your budget every month. A smaller extra amount you keep up beats a larger one you stop. Common questions

Do extra payments really reduce my interest?

Yes. An extra payment applied to principal lowers the balance used to calculate each later month's interest, so the loan ends sooner and total interest falls. Ask your servicer to apply extra money to principal.

Is a lump sum better than monthly extra payments?

It depends on the amounts and the timing. A lump sum reduces principal once, while monthly extras reduce it gradually. Compare both in the calculator instead of assuming one always wins.

How does the biweekly option work?

Paying half the monthly amount every two weeks makes 26 half-payments, equal to 13 full payments a year. The calculator models it as one extra monthly payment a year. Some servicers hold partial payments or charge fees, so check.

Should I pay off my mortgage early or invest?

Paying it off earns a guaranteed return equal to your loan rate, while investments may earn more or less. The answer depends on your rate, taxes, risk tolerance and emergency savings.

Is there a penalty for paying early?

Some loans have a prepayment penalty, usually on paying off the whole loan or a large amount within the first few years. Small extra payments normally are not affected. Check your loan documents.

Why are there two ways to enter the loan?

Some people have the original loan details, others only a statement with the balance and payment. Both give the same result, so use whichever you have.
