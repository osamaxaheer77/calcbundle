Auto Lease Calculator

Estimate a monthly car lease payment from the vehicle price, money factor, and residual value — and see how it compares to buying the same car.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A lease payment is depreciation plus a rent charge plus tax. A $50,000 car with a $10,000 down payment, a $24,000 residual, a 0.00208 money factor, 36 months and 7% tax costs $617.99 a month.

A lease payment is two payments in one

Every lease payment bundles two charges. The depreciation covers the value the car is expected to lose over the lease: the gap between the capitalized cost (the price less your down payment and trade-in) and the residual value when you hand it back. The rent charge, which works like interest, is the lessor’s fee for letting you use their asset, and is worked out with the money factor instead of an interest rate. Sales tax is then added to the payment.

The formulas

  • Capitalized cost = price − down payment − trade-in.
  • Monthly depreciation = (capitalized cost − residual value) ÷ months.
  • Monthly rent charge = (capitalized cost + residual value) × money factor.
  • Monthly payment = (depreciation + rent charge) × (1 + sales tax rate).

Worked example with the default numbers

A $50,000 car, 36 months, a money factor of 0.00208, a $10,000 down payment, no trade-in, 7% sales tax and a $24,000 residual value. The capitalized cost is $40,000. Depreciation is ($40,000 − $24,000) ÷ 36 = $444.44 a month. The rent charge is ($40,000 + $24,000) × 0.00208 = $133.12. Together they are $577.56, tax adds $40.43, and the monthly lease payment is $617.99. Over 36 months you pay $22,247.78 in payments plus $700 of tax on the down payment, and the money factor is equivalent to a 4.99% interest rate.

A simpler check: a car with a $30,000 capitalized cost and an $18,000 residual loses $12,000 over 36 months, which is $333.33 a month in depreciation before any rent charge or tax.

What a money factor is

A money factor is just an interest rate in disguise. Multiply it by 2,400 to get roughly the equivalent APR; divide an APR by 2,400 to get the money factor. Dealers often quote the money factor because a number like 0.00208 sounds smaller than 4.99% APR, though they are the same cost. You can enter the rate either way: the calculator has a switch for money factor or interest rate.

Residual value has an outsized effect

A higher residual means less depreciation to pay for, so cars that hold their value are cheaper to lease relative to their price. The residual is set by the lessor, usually as a percentage of the price.

Leasing versus buying

The calculator also shows the same car bought with a loan at the equivalent rate, with the same price, down payment, term and tax. The loan payment is $1,198.69 a month and total interest is $3,152.92. The total cost to own after a lease, which assumes you buy the car at the end for its residual value plus tax, is $58,628, against $56,653 to buy with a loan.

The comparison leaves out fees and limits. Leases often add acquisition, security deposit and disposition fees, and mileage and wear charges, that a purchase does not. Buying leaves you owning an asset at the end; leasing leaves you with nothing unless you use the purchase option.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing lease payments without checking the capitalized cost, residual and money factor.
  • Forgetting taxes, fees and mileage limits.
  • Putting a large down payment on a lease.
  • Assuming a lower payment means a cheaper deal. It may only mean a longer or riskier one.

How to read your result

The large number is the monthly payment including tax. Below it are the parts: depreciation, rent charge and tax, plus the totals and the buying comparison. Ask the dealer for the capitalized cost, residual value and money factor in writing, enter them here and check that the payment matches their quote.

This calculator gives estimates. Fees such as acquisition, disposition and registration, and mileage charges, are not included. It is not financial advice.

Common questions

Why do leases charge tax differently than purchases?

Many states tax each lease payment as it is made, and the down payment as an upfront payment, rather than taxing the full vehicle price upfront as a purchase is taxed. This calculator models both mechanisms separately.

Is a lower money factor always a better deal?

All else equal, yes. But the residual value and the negotiated price matter just as much. A great money factor on an inflated price, or a low residual that overstates depreciation, can still make an expensive lease.

What happens if I want to keep the car after the lease?

Most leases include a purchase option letting you buy the car for its residual value plus tax at the end. That is the assumption behind this calculator's total cost to own after the lease.

How do I convert a money factor to an APR?

Multiply the money factor by 2,400. For example, 0.00208 x 2,400 = 4.99%. To go the other way, divide the APR by 2,400.

What is the capitalized cost?

It is the price the lease is based on: the vehicle price less your down payment and trade-in (the capitalized cost reduction), plus any fees you roll in.

Are fees included?

No. Acquisition, disposition, security deposit, registration and mileage charges vary by lease and are not included.
