Annuity Payout Calculator

Start with the available annuity principal and choose whether to calculate the payout amount for a specified term or the time your chosen payment might last. This is a mathematical payment projection, not a quote from an insurer.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. $500,000 at 6% paid out monthly over 10 years gives $5,511.20 a month, and the 120 payments total $661,344. A fixed $5,000 a month on the same balance lasts about 11.4 years.

The payout phase of an annuity

An annuity has two stages. In the accumulation phase you build up a balance. In the payout phase, which this calculator covers, the balance is paid back to you in equal payments while the money that remains keeps earning interest. Because the leftover money keeps growing, the total you receive is more than the principal you start with.

Two questions the calculator answers

  • How much can I withdraw over a fixed length? You choose the years, and the tool finds the largest equal payment that brings the balance to exactly zero on the last payment. It is the arithmetic of a loan payment run in reverse.
  • How long will a fixed payment last? You choose the amount per period, and the tool counts the payments until the money is gone. If the payment is no more than the interest earned each period, the balance never falls and the tool says Forever.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens on a principal of $500,000, a return of 6% a year, a payout of 10 years and monthly payments. A 6% yearly rate is converted to the matching monthly rate, 1.061/12 − 1 = 0.486755%, so the balance grows by exactly 6% over a full year. With 120 payments, the payment is P × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)−120) = $5,511.20 a month. The 120 payments total $661,344.16, which is your $500,000 plus $161,344.16 of interest earned on the part still invested.

When the answer is forever

If the payment is no more than the interest earned each period, the balance never falls. At 6% a year the first month’s interest on $500,000 is $2,433.78, so a payment of $2,400 a month is below that and the tool reports Forever, with the principal intact. The tool also reports Forever if the payout would last longer than 200 years.

What this tool leaves out

  • Fees. Real contracts may charge fees that reduce the return.
  • Life expectancy. Insurers often set payouts from your age and life expectancy, and a life annuity pays for as long as you live, which a fixed-term calculation does not.
  • Taxes. A part of each payment may be taxable income, depending on the contract and how it was funded.
  • Guarantees and inflation. The tool assumes a constant return and level payments, so it does not model rising prices.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the result as an insurer’s quote. It is a mathematical projection.
  • Using a return rate that is too optimistic. A small change in the rate moves the payment a lot.
  • Forgetting that the payout length is in years, not payments.
  • Entering a payment below the interest and expecting an end date.
  • Mixing up the monthly rate and the yearly rate. The tool takes the yearly rate.

How to read your result

The large number is the payment, or the number of years, depending on your question. Below it are the number of payments, the total you receive, your starting principal and the interest earned, with a chart of the balance and a yearly table. If the tool says Forever, the interest each period is at least as large as the payment.

This is a planning estimate, not a quote or a guarantee. For a real annuity, compare the contract terms, fees and tax treatment with a qualified adviser.

Common questions

What is the difference between a fixed length and a fixed payment payout?

A fixed length payout chooses how many years the money should last and works out the largest equal payment that uses it up exactly. A fixed payment payout chooses the amount you want to receive each period and works out how long the money lasts.

How is the payout payment calculated?

The yearly rate is converted to a rate for each period, and the payment is the principal times that rate divided by one minus the rate plus one raised to the power of minus the number of payments. For $500,000 at 6% over 10 years, monthly, that is $5,511.20.

Why does the tool say Forever?

When the payment is no more than the interest the balance earns each period, the balance never falls, so you can withdraw that amount indefinitely and keep the principal.

Why is the total I receive higher than the principal?

The money that has not yet been paid out keeps earning interest, so the payments add up to more than the starting balance.

Does this include taxes and fees?

No. Real annuity contracts can charge fees and tax part of each payment, none of which is included here.

Is this a quote from an insurer?

No. It is a mathematical projection. Insurers often set payouts from life expectancy and add guarantees and charges, so the actual offer will differ.
