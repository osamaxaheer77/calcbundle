Electricity Calculator

Estimate an appliance's electricity usage and cost per day, week, month, and year.

How usage becomes a cost

Power (in watts or kilowatts) tells you the rate of energy use; multiplying by how many hours the appliance actually runs gives energy in kilowatt-hours (kWh) — the unit your electricity bill is priced in. The "capacity" field scales this down for appliances that don't run at full power the whole time, like a dimmed light or a partial-load motor.

Why monthly and yearly figures use 365.25 days

Using an average year length (accounting for leap years) keeps the monthly and yearly figures consistent with each other, rather than the monthly total drifting depending on which specific month you'd pick.

Your appliance's real usage varies day to day — treat this as a planning estimate, and adjust the usage field to match your actual habits for a more accurate figure.

A worked example

A 1,000-watt appliance that runs 24 hours a day uses 24 kilowatt-hours a day. At $0.15 per kWh that costs $3.60 a day, $25.20 a week, $109.58 a month and $1,314.90 a year, which is 8,766 kWh. Run the same appliance for 2 hours a day instead and the yearly cost drops to about $109.58.

Tips for saving power

Find an appliance's wattage on its label, then enter how many hours a day it really runs. Devices that cycle on and off, such as a refrigerator or an air conditioner, run at less than full capacity, so use the capacity percentage. Standby power adds up too. Your price per kWh is on your bill, and it may include delivery charges as well as the energy charge.

Common uses

Use it to compare an old and a new appliance, to check what a space heater costs per month, to estimate the running cost of a home server or a gaming PC, and to size a solar system.

Common questions

Where do I find my appliance's power rating?

Check the nameplate or spec sheet, usually printed as watts (W) or amps × volts. If it's rated in amps, multiply by your outlet's voltage (typically 120V in the US) to get watts.

Where do I find my electricity price per kWh?

It's on your utility bill, usually listed as a rate per kWh. National averages are a rough substitute if you don't have a bill handy.
