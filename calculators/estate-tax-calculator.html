Lifetime Gifts

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. The federal estate tax applies only to the amount above the lifetime exemption, which is $15 million per person for 2026, at a rate of 40%. An $18 million estate with $1 million of debts owes $800,000; an estate under the exemption owes nothing.

How the estate tax is worked out

The gross estate is the market value of everything you own when you die: property, investments, savings, retirement accounts, life insurance and other assets. Debts, funeral and administration costs, charitable gifts and any state death tax are subtracted to give the net estate. Taxable gifts you made during your lifetime are added back, and the total is compared with the lifetime exemption. Only the amount above the exemption is taxed, at a flat 40% in the calculator.

The formula

  • Net estate = total assets − (debts + funeral and administration costs + charitable gifts + state death taxes).
  • Taxable estate = net estate + taxable lifetime gifts.
  • Federal estate tax = 40% × (taxable estate − exemption), or $0 if the taxable estate is within the exemption.

The lifetime exemption

Every person has a lifetime exemption that covers both gifts during life and the estate left at death. It is $15 million for 2026, up from $13.99 million in 2025, so most estates owe no federal estate tax at all. A surviving spouse can often use any exemption the first spouse did not, which can shelter even more for a married couple. The IRS publishes the current amounts on its estate tax page.

Past amounts were $5.34 million in 2014, $11.18 million in 2018, $12.92 million in 2023, $13.61 million in 2024 and $13.99 million in 2025. The rate has been 40% since 2013.

Worked examples

An estate of $2 million with $200,000 of deductions has a net estate of $1,800,000. That is far below the $15 million exemption, so the federal estate tax is $0. A figure like $1.8 million is a net value, not a tax bill.

An $18 million estate with $1 million of debts and costs. The net estate is $17,000,000. The amount over the exemption is $2,000,000, and 40% of that is $800,000 of federal estate tax. The same estate taxed under the 2025 exemption of $13.99 million would owe $1,204,000.

Lifetime gifts count. An estate of $12 million plus $5 million of taxable gifts made during life has a taxable estate of $17,000,000. That is $2,000,000 over the exemption, so the tax is $800,000, even though the estate on its own is under $15 million.

Deductions can matter. A $20 million estate with $0.5 million of debts and costs, $1.5 million left to charity and $2 million of state death tax has a net estate of $16,000,000. The tax is $400,000.

Gifts, spouses and state taxes

  • Gifts: gifts above the yearly gift tax exclusion use part of your lifetime exemption and are added back. Enter the total of those taxable gifts, not the small gifts under the yearly exclusion.
  • Spouses: assets left to a surviving spouse who is a U.S. citizen are generally deducted through the marital deduction, and a spouse can often carry over an unused exemption. Enter only what passes to other heirs to estimate the tax on them.
  • State taxes: some states add their own estate or inheritance tax, often with a much lower threshold. If you know the amount, enter it as a deduction, because state death taxes reduce the federal taxable estate.

Estate tax or inheritance tax?

Estate tax is paid by the estate before assets are handed out, based on the total value left behind. Inheritance tax is paid by the person who receives the assets. The federal government charges only an estate tax, while a few states charge an inheritance tax.

What this calculator does not cover

  • Trusts, business interests, valuation discounts and special-use valuation.
  • Generation-skipping transfer tax.
  • Future changes in the law and indexing of the exemption.
  • The details of portability for a surviving spouse.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the net estate as a tax bill.
  • Forgetting to add back taxable lifetime gifts.
  • Ignoring state estate or inheritance taxes, which can apply to far smaller estates.
  • Counting assets that pass to a spouse as taxable.

How to read your result

If the taxable estate is within the exemption, the result is $0. Otherwise it shows the amount over the exemption, the tax, the value left after tax, and what the same estate would owe under the 2025 exemption. The default inputs are all zero, so enter your own figures to see a result.

This is an estimate for planning, not legal or tax advice. Estate rules are complex, state taxes can apply at far lower amounts, and trusts and business interests can change the result. Talk to an estate attorney before relying on it.

Common questions

What is the federal estate tax exemption for 2026?

For 2026 each person can pass on up to $15 million before federal estate tax applies. Only the amount above that is taxed, at a rate of 40%.

Do lifetime gifts count toward the estate tax?

Gifts above the yearly gift tax exclusion use up part of your lifetime exemption, so they are added back to your estate when the tax is worked out. Enter the total of those taxable gifts, not the small gifts that fall under the yearly exclusion.

Is anything I leave to my spouse taxed?

Generally no. Assets left to a surviving spouse who is a U.S. citizen are deducted through the marital deduction, and a spouse can often carry over an unused exemption. Enter only what passes to other heirs, such as children.

Does this include state estate or inheritance tax?

No. It estimates only the federal tax. Some states add their own estate or inheritance tax, often with a much lower threshold. If you know the state tax amount, enter it as a deduction, since state death taxes reduce the federal taxable estate.

What is the difference between estate tax and inheritance tax?

Estate tax is paid by the estate before assets are handed out, based on the total value left behind. Inheritance tax is paid by the person who receives the assets. The federal government charges only an estate tax, while a few states charge an inheritance tax.

Why are the starting values all zero?

Every person's estate is different, so the calculator starts blank. Enter your own assets, debts and gifts to see a result.
