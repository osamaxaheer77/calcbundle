Age Calculator

To find your age on another date, enter a birth date and the date you want to measure against. This is also useful for comparing any two calendar dates; the result is a calendar interval, not just a number of 365-day years.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Age is the calendar interval between a birth date and another date. Someone born on January 15, 1990 is 36 years, 8 months and 9 days old on September 24, 2026, which is 13,401 days in total.

How age is counted here

The calculator uses the most common Western convention: a person’s age goes up by one on each birthday. Someone who has lived 3 years and 11 months is “3 years old” until their next birthday one month later. Enter a date of birth and the date you want to measure to, and you get the age in years, months and days, plus the same span as total months, weeks, days, hours, minutes and seconds.

Worked example with the default dates

Born on January 15, 1990 and measured on September 24, 2026, the person is 36 years, 8 months and 9 days old. The same span is 440 months and 9 days, 1,914 weeks and 3 days, 13,401 days, 321,624 hours, 19,297,440 minutes or 1,157,846,400 seconds. They were born on a Monday, and the interval includes 9 leap days. Their next birthday is 113 days away, and their 10,000th day was June 2, 2017.

How the years, months and days are worked out

  • Start with the difference in years, months and days between the two dates.
  • If the day difference is negative, borrow a month and add the number of days in the month before the end date.
  • If the month difference is negative, borrow a year and add 12 months.
  • Total days is the exact count of calendar days between the dates, so leap days are included.

Why month and day results can look surprising

Months have different lengths, so counting how many months and days lie between two dates is not always intuitive. From February 28 to March 31, the calculator counts February 28 to March 28 as one full month, leaving 3 remaining days: 1 month and 3 days. Treating both as “end of month” and calling it exactly one month is another reasonable convention. Neither is wrong; the tool uses the first and says so.

Leap-day birthdays

A person born on February 29 still gets a correct result. For someone born on February 29, 2000, the tool shows 0 years 11 months 30 days on February 28, 2001, and 1 year 0 months 0 days on March 1, 2001, so the birthday year ticks over on March 1 in non-leap years. Leap years are every year divisible by 4, except century years that are not divisible by 400, so 2000 was a leap year and 1900 was not.

Other ways to count age

Other cultures count differently. In one traditional East Asian system, a baby is age 1 at birth and gains a year at the Lunar New Year, regardless of how many days old they are. Always check which convention a form or law uses.

Uses beyond birthdays

  • School and sports cut-off dates.
  • Insurance and benefit age limits.
  • Forms that ask for age in months.
  • Planning milestones, such as a 10,000-day birthday.
  • Any gap between two dates: enter them as the first and second dates.

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying years by 365. That ignores leap days and gives an inexact count of days.
  • Entering the dates in the wrong order. The birth date must come first.
  • Expecting months to equal exactly 30 days.
  • Using the age for a legal purpose without checking the local rule.

Common questions

Can I use this to find the days between any two dates, not just an age?

Yes. Date of Birth and Age at the Date of are just labels. Enter any two dates to see the full interval broken down every way.

Why is the total in months different from years x 12?

It is not. Total months is exactly years x 12 plus the leftover months, paired with the same leftover days as the main year, month and day result.

How are leap years handled?

Leap days are counted in the total number of days, so a person born on February 29 still gets a correct result.

Why does the month and day result depend on the dates?

Months have different lengths, so the same number of months can cover different numbers of days. The calculator counts full calendar months first and then the leftover days.

Does age ever count differently?

Yes. Some traditional systems count a baby as age 1 at birth and add a year at New Year rather than on the birthday. This calculator uses the common convention of adding a year on each birthday.

Can I find my age on a future date?

Yes. Change the second date to any date, past or future, such as a retirement date.
