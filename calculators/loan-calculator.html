Loan Calculator

Compare repayment costs for a standard installment loan, a balance repaid at maturity, or a bond-value calculation. Select the loan type first, then enter the amount, term and interest assumptions to see the matching payment schedule.

Amortization Schedule

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A loan payment spreads the amount borrowed plus interest over equal installments. $100,000 at 6% over 10 years with monthly payments costs $1,110.21 a month and $33,225 in total interest. If nothing is paid until the end, the amount due is $181,940.

Three kinds of loan calculation

  • Amortized loan: repaid in equal payments until nothing is owed. This is what most people mean by a loan: a car loan, a student loan, a personal loan or a mortgage.
  • Deferred payment loan: no payments until the end, when you owe the principal plus all the interest that built up.
  • Bond: the reverse view. You enter the fixed amount due at maturity and the calculator finds how much the lender hands over at the start.

Select the type first, then enter the amount, term, interest rate, compounding and, for amortized loans, how often you pay.

Worked example with the default numbers

Borrow $100,000 at 6% for 10 years, compounded monthly and paid monthly. The payment is $1,110.21 a month, the total of 120 payments is $133,224.60 and interest is $33,224.60. In the first month interest is $100,000 × 0.5% = $500.00, so only $610.21 of the first payment reduces the balance.

How the payment is calculated

The interest rate is converted to the matching rate for each payment period, so compounding and payment frequency can differ. Compounding monthly and paying monthly gives the usual rate divided by twelve. The payment is the level amount that brings the balance to exactly zero after the last payment, with each payment made at the end of its period. Interest each period is the balance times the period rate and the rest of the payment reduces principal. If the term is not a whole number of payments, such as 4 years and 5 months of weekly payments, the last payment is smaller.

How payment frequency changes the cost

Paying more often reduces interest slightly, because the balance falls sooner. On the default loan, paying every two weeks means $511.71 per payment and $33,046 of interest, and paying weekly means $255.71 per payment and $32,969, against $33,225 of interest for monthly payments. Each payment is smaller, but the totals are close because the rate is converted per period rather than adding extra payments.

Bonds

For a bond, enter the amount due at maturity (the face value), the term and the rate. The calculator discounts it back to today. A $100,000 amount due in 10 years at 6% compounded monthly is worth $54,963.27 at the start, so the interest is $45,037. The higher the rate, the less the lender hands over today.

Reading the schedule

For amortized loans the schedule can be shown by year or by each payment, with the beginning balance, interest, principal and ending balance. For the deferred loan and the bond it shows the balance growing year by year, with a partial year at the end if the term is not whole.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing loans by monthly payment alone, instead of total interest.
  • Mixing up the interest rate and the APR when fees are involved. Use the Personal Loan or APR calculators for fees.
  • Choosing different compounding and payment settings without understanding the conversion.
  • Expecting the balance to fall evenly. Early payments are mostly interest.

How to read your result

The large number is the payment (or, for the other modes, the amount due or received). Below it are the total paid and total interest. Try a shorter term or more frequent payments and watch the interest fall, then check the payment still fits your budget. Common questions

What is an amortized loan?

An amortized loan is repaid with equal payments at regular intervals until the balance reaches zero. Each payment covers the interest that has built up since the last one, and the rest reduces the principal. Mortgages, car loans, student loans and most personal loans work this way.

What is a deferred payment loan?

A deferred payment loan has no payments during its life. Interest builds up on the balance, and the principal plus all the interest is due in one lump sum at the end.

What does the bond calculation tell me?

A bond pays a fixed amount, its face value, at maturity. Given that amount, the term and the interest rate, the calculator finds how much the lender hands over at the start, which is the face value discounted back at the rate.

How do compounding and payment frequency work together?

The compounding setting says how often interest is added to the balance, and the pay back setting says how often you make a payment. The calculator converts the rate to the equivalent rate for each payment period, so the two can differ.

Why does a lower payment cost more in total?

Stretching the same loan over a longer term lowers each payment but keeps the balance outstanding for longer, so more interest builds up.

Does this include fees?

No. It covers interest only. For loans with origination fees or insurance, use the Personal Loan Calculator or the APR Calculator.
