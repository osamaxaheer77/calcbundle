VAT Calculator

Add VAT to a price, take it out of a total, or find the rate that was charged. Fill in any two boxes and the other two are worked out.

Please provide any two values from the following to calculate the remaining values.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Gross = net × (1 + rate). At 20% VAT, a net price of $1,200 has $240 of VAT and a gross price of $1,440. To remove VAT, divide the gross price by 1.20, not subtract 20%.

What VAT is

Value-added tax, or VAT, is a consumption tax charged on the value added to goods and services at each stage of their production and sale. Businesses collect it on behalf of the government and pass the net amount on. Most countries outside the United States use VAT or a similar goods and services tax, often called GST. The price before the tax is the net price, and the price including the tax is the gross price.

How the four numbers relate

Gross = net × (1 + rate), and VAT amount = gross − net. Any two of the four values, the rate, the net price, the gross price and the VAT amount, give the other two. To add VAT, enter the rate and the net price. To remove VAT from a total, enter the rate and the gross price. To discover the rate on a receipt, enter the net and gross prices.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens with a rate of 20% and a net price of $1,200. The VAT amount is 1,200 × 20% = $240.00, and the gross price is 1,200 + 240 = $1,440.00. The result box shows the gross price, the rate, the net price, the VAT amount and the gross price again.

Removing VAT from a total

A common mistake is to take the rate off the gross price. With a gross price of $1,440 at 20%, taking 20% of 1,440 gives $288.00, which is too much. The right VAT is gross − gross ÷ 1.20 = $240.00, a net price of $1,200.00. VAT is a fixed share of the net price, so it is a smaller share of the gross price: 16.7% at a 20% rate.

Finding the rate on a receipt

If a receipt shows a net price of $100 and a gross of $123, the tax is $23 and the rate is 23 ÷ 100 = 23%. The calculator shows the rate to three decimals, so a rate that does not come out evenly, such as 7.5%, appears as 7.5. You can also use the net price and the VAT amount, or the gross price and the VAT amount, as in the table above.

Rates and categories

VAT rates differ between countries and between types of goods. In the UK, according to GOV.UK, the standard rate is 20% and applies to most goods and services, a reduced rate of 5% applies to some items such as home energy, and a zero rate applies to most food and children’s clothes. Zero-rated goods are taxable at 0%, which is different from being outside the scope of VAT. Other countries have different rates and rules, so always check the rate that applies to you. Rounding

The calculator shows amounts to two decimals, as on an invoice. Shops and tax authorities have their own rules for rounding on each line or on the total, which can differ by a cent or two. For an invoice, follow the method of your tax authority.

Common mistakes

  • Taking the VAT rate of the gross price instead of dividing by one plus the rate.
  • Filling in more than two boxes. The calculator then works out the others again from the first pair it can use.
  • Using a rate that does not apply to the goods, such as a standard rate for a reduced-rate item.
  • Mixing up zero-rated and exempt supplies.
  • Forgetting that this tool uses a dollar sign. The arithmetic is the same for any currency.

How to read your result

The large number is the gross price with VAT, with a line below that gives the rate and the net price. The rows list the rate, the net price, the VAT amount and the gross price. If you filled in more than two boxes, a note says which values were used.

Common questions

How do I add VAT to a price?

Multiply the net price by one plus the VAT rate. At 20 percent, $1,200 becomes $1,200 times 1.20, which is $1,440, and the VAT is $240.

How do I remove VAT from a price?

Divide the gross price by one plus the VAT rate. At 20 percent, $1,440 divided by 1.20 gives a net price of $1,200, and the VAT is $240.

Why is taking 20 percent off the gross price wrong?

VAT is 20 percent of the net price, which is only one sixth, about 16.7 percent, of the gross price. Taking 20 percent of the gross price removes too much.

How do I find the VAT rate from two prices?

Subtract the net price from the gross price to get the VAT, divide it by the net price and multiply by 100. For $100 net and $123 gross, the rate is 23 percent.

How many boxes should I fill in?

Any two of the four. If you fill in more, the calculator uses the first pair it can and works the rest out again, so clear a box to solve for it.

Do VAT rates differ by country?

Yes, and by type of goods. In the UK the standard rate is 20 percent, with a reduced rate of 5 percent and a zero rate for some goods. Check the rate that applies to you.
