Rent Calculator

Work out what rent fits comfortably within your monthly budget, based on your income and existing debt.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Recommended rent is 28% of gross monthly income minus debt, and the maximum is 36%. On $80,000 a year with no debt that is $1,867 and $2,400 a month, and a one-third income rule would cap rent at $2,222.

What the calculator works out

Enter your pre-tax income, per year or per month, and your total monthly debt payments. The calculator turns the income into a monthly figure and applies two limits: recommended rent = 28% of gross monthly income minus your debt payments, and maximum rent = 36% of gross monthly income minus your debt payments. Neither can fall below zero. The defaults, $80,000 a year and no debt, give a monthly income of $6,667, a maximum rent of $2,400 and a recommended rent of $1,867.

Worked example with the default numbers

$80,000 ÷ 12 = $6,666.67 a month. Multiply by 0.36 to get $2,400.00, then take off the debt payments (none here), so the most you can afford is $2,400 a month. Multiply by 0.28 to get $1,866.67, shown as $1,867. Entering the same pay as $6,667 per month gives the same results, since the tool simply skips the division by 12.

Where the 28% and 36% figures come from

The two numbers come from the 28/36 guideline lenders use for mortgages: no more than 28% of gross income on housing and no more than 36% on all debt together, housing included. The calculator borrows them for rent. Staying near the lower figure leaves more room for savings, emergencies and new debt over a lease term. Treat both as guidelines, not rules, because a landlord sets its own income test.

The one-third rule and the 30% line

Many landlords ask for gross income of about three times the rent, which means rent of no more than one third of income. On the default income that is $2,222. The tool compares your maximum with that figure and shows a note when the maximum is higher, because some landlords would turn down an application at the top of the range. HUD, the US housing agency, treats households that spend more than 30% of income on housing as cost-burdened; on $80,000 that line is $2,000 a month.

How debt shrinks your rent budget

Debt is subtracted dollar for dollar, so a $500 car payment cuts the maximum from $2,400 to $1,900 and the recommended figure from $1,867 to $1,367. The table shows the effect on the default income.

Gross or net income?

Use gross, pre-tax income. Landlords and the 28/36 guideline are both measured against gross pay, even though your take-home is lower. If your budget is tight after tax, work from take-home pay instead and use the lower figure as a personal limit. The Budget Calculator is better for that.

What the result does not include

  • Utilities, renters insurance, parking and pet fees, which can add a few hundred dollars a month on top of rent.
  • A security deposit and first month’s rent due when you sign.
  • Local rules, such as minimum income tests set by a landlord or a city.
  • Savings goals, childcare, student loans not counted as debt, and other spending.

Common mistakes

  • Entering net pay instead of gross, which understates the budget.
  • Leaving out debt, such as car loans, student loans and credit card minimums.
  • Treating the maximum as the target. The recommended figure is the safer one.
  • Mixing up per-year and per-month income. Check the drop-down beside the income box.

How to read your result

The large number is the most you can afford. The line under it is the recommended ceiling, and the bar shows how the recommended figure compares with the maximum. The two stat rows repeat the monthly income and the debt you entered, so you can confirm the inputs. If a note about the one-third rule appears, check the listing’s income requirement before applying.

These figures are guidelines. A landlord can set a stricter or looser income test, and this calculator is not a guarantee of approval.

Common questions

How much rent can I afford?

A common guide is rent of no more than 28% of gross monthly income after subtracting other debt payments, and no more than 36% as an upper limit. On $80,000 a year with no debt, that is about $1,867 recommended and $2,400 at most.

Should I use gross or net income?

Use gross, pre-tax income. Landlords and the 28/36 guideline are both measured against gross pay.

What is the one-third rule?

Some landlords will not accept rent above one third of gross income. On $80,000 a year that is about $2,222 a month. The calculator shows a note when your maximum is higher.

How does debt change what I can afford?

Your monthly debt payments are taken off the 28% and 36% limits dollar for dollar. A $500 car payment lowers both figures by $500.

Why is the recommended rent lower than the maximum?

The maximum is the outer limit most landlords and lenders tolerate. The recommended figure leaves more income free for savings and unexpected costs.

Is the result a guarantee I will be approved?

No. It is a guideline only. Each landlord sets its own income requirement, and it may be stricter or looser.
