Due Date Calculator

Estimate your due date from your last period, an ultrasound, the date of conception or an IVF transfer, and see how far along you are.

Pregnancy week by week

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A due date is 280 days (40 weeks) after the first day of the last period, or 266 days after conception. A last period starting March 10, 2026 gives a due date of December 15, 2026; a longer or shorter cycle moves that date by the same number of days.

How a due date is estimated

A full-term pregnancy is counted as 40 weeks, or 280 days, from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP). The standard due date is that date plus 280 days. It is an estimate: roughly 1 baby in 20 is born on the due date, and most arrive within a couple of weeks either side. Think of it as the middle of a range, not a deadline.

Cycle length changes the date

The 280-day rule assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation around day 14. If your cycle is longer, ovulation happens later, and the due date moves by the same number of days; with a shorter cycle it moves earlier. For the example last period of March 10, 2026:

Ultrasound dating

An ultrasound measures the pregnancy’s age on the day of the scan, and the due date follows from that. A scan on May 4, 2026 that shows 8 weeks 0 days puts the start of the pregnancy on March 9, 2026 and the due date on December 14, 2026, a day earlier than the December 15, 2026 given by the last period. Early scans are usually the most accurate way to date a pregnancy, and providers generally change the due date to the scan date when the two differ by more than about a week, depending on how early the scan was.

IVF dating

With IVF the transfer date and embryo age are known exactly. A day-5 embryo transferred on March 29, 2026 has 261 days to go, so the due date is December 15, 2026. For a day-3 embryo transferred on the same date the due date would be December 17, 2026, two days later.

Full term and what the weeks mean

For the example, 37 weeks falls on November 24, 2026, 39 weeks on December 8, 2026, 40 weeks (the due date) on December 15, 2026, 41 weeks on December 22, 2026 and 42 weeks on December 29, 2026. A baby born between 39 weeks 0 days and 40 weeks 6 days is full term, early term starts at 37 weeks 0 days, late term at 41 weeks 0 days and post-term at 42 weeks.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the date of a positive test or of intercourse instead of the first day of the last period.
  • Ignoring a cycle that is not 28 days, which can move the date by a week or more.
  • Treating the due date as a deadline.
  • Using the estimate in place of your doctor’s official date.

How to read your result

Use the date to plan, and treat the weeks before and after as the likely window. Your doctor or midwife sets the due date used for care, usually after an early ultrasound, so follow their date if it differs from the calculator. Common questions

How is the due date calculated?

It is 280 days (40 weeks) after the first day of your last period. If your cycle is not 28 days long, the date moves by the difference. From the conception date it is 266 days.

How accurate is a due date?

Only roughly 1 baby in 20 is born on the due date. Most arrive within a couple of weeks before or after, so treat it as the middle of a range.

Which method is most accurate?

An early ultrasound is usually the most accurate way to date a pregnancy, followed by a known conception or IVF transfer date. The last period works well with regular 28-day cycles.

Why does the IVF method use the embryo age?

Embryo age is part of the count: a day-5 embryo is already five days old at transfer, so the pregnancy has 261 days to go from that date.

What if my cycle is not 28 days?

Choose your average cycle length. A 32-day cycle moves the due date four days later; a 24-day cycle moves it four days earlier.

Will my doctor use the same date?

Your doctor or midwife sets the official date, often after an early ultrasound. If it differs from the calculator, follow theirs.
