Real estate is a set of questions, not one calculation

Buying, financing, refinancing, renting and investing each have their own maths, so there is no single “real estate calculator”. This page is a map. It shows the order in which the questions usually come up, what each CalcBundle tool answers, and a worked example of what a home purchase costs, so you know which tool to open next.

Which calculator to use at each step

Before you look at homes. Start with the House Affordability Calculator to see what price fits your income and debts, and check your ratios with the Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator, because lenders look at them closely. The Down Payment Calculator shows how much to save and how a bigger down payment changes the loan.

While you compare loans. The Mortgage Calculator gives the monthly payment with taxes and insurance. If you may qualify for a government-backed loan, compare it with the FHA Loan Calculator or the VA Mortgage Calculator. Use the APR Calculator to compare offers with different fees, and the Amortization Calculator to see how each payment splits between principal and interest.

After you buy. The Mortgage Payoff Calculator shows what extra payments save, and the Refinance Calculator shows whether a lower rate pays for its costs and when you break even. To borrow against your home, compare the Home Equity Loan Calculator and the HELOC Calculator.

Renting or investing. The Rent vs. Buy Calculator compares the long-term cost of each choice, and the Rent Calculator helps you decide how much rent you can afford. For an investment property, the Rental Property Calculator estimates cash flow and return.

Worked example: what a $400,000 home really costs

This is an illustration, not a quote. Assume a $400,000 home, 10% down ($40,000) and a 30-year loan of $360,000 at 6.5%. Principal and interest is $2,275.44 a month. Add property tax at 1.2% of the price ($400), home insurance of $1,500 a year ($125) and, because the down payment is under 20%, an assumed private mortgage insurance of 0.5% of the loan a year ($150). The monthly total is $2,950.44. Over 30 years the interest alone comes to $459,160.

Cash needed at closing is also larger than the down payment. With closing costs assumed at 3% of the price ($12,000), you need about $52,000 before moving costs or repairs. Your own closing costs are shown on the lender’s Loan Estimate, so ask for one when you compare lenders.

Ratios lenders look at

Lenders compare your monthly debts with your income. The debt-to-income ratio adds your housing payment and other debts and divides by gross monthly income. For the illustration home, the monthly cost of $2,950 is only the housing part; add car loans, student loans and cards before judging whether it fits. A ratio of 43% has long been a common upper limit for many mortgages, and lower is easier to qualify with and safer to live with. Use the Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator for your own figures.

A few things to remember

  • Every purchase has costs beyond the mortgage: property tax, insurance, maintenance, closing costs and, with a small down payment, mortgage insurance.
  • Compare loans by APR and total cost, not only the monthly payment.
  • A refinance only pays if you stay long enough to recover the closing costs.
  • Your approved loan amount is not the amount you should borrow.
  • All calculations on CalcBundle run in your browser and give estimates. Confirm figures with a lender before you rely on them.

Common mistakes

  • Budgeting only for principal and interest.
  • Forgetting closing costs, moving costs and repairs.
  • Using the maximum a lender will approve as the budget.
  • Comparing a fixed payment with a rent that can rise, or the reverse, without a rent-versus-buy check.

Common questions

Which real estate calculator should I use first?

If you are buying, start with the House Affordability Calculator to find a price range, then the Mortgage Calculator for the full monthly cost. If you already own, the Refinance and Mortgage Payoff calculators are the usual next steps.

How much should I budget beyond the mortgage payment?

Include property tax, home insurance, maintenance, mortgage insurance if your down payment is small, and closing costs when you buy. The worked example on this page shows how much these add.

Is a bigger down payment always better?

It lowers the loan, the interest and any mortgage insurance, but it also uses cash you may need for repairs and emergencies. Compare the options in the Down Payment and Mortgage calculators.

How do I know if refinancing is worth it?

Compare the monthly saving with the closing costs to find the break-even point, then check you will stay in the home longer than that. The Refinance Calculator does this.

Does a calculator tell me if I will be approved for a loan?

No. Lenders also look at your credit history, income documents and other debts. The calculators give estimates for planning.

Should I rent or buy?

It depends on how long you will stay, the price, the rate and what you could do with the cash. The Rent vs. Buy Calculator compares the long-term costs.
