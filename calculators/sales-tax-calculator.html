Quick answer. Sales tax is added to the price at checkout. A $100.00 item at a 6.5% rate costs $106.50, of which $6.50 is tax. To go backwards, divide the receipt total by 1.065.

What sales tax is

Sales tax is a consumption tax collected by the seller at the time of purchase and passed to the government. The United States has no federal sales tax. States set their own rates, and counties and cities often add more, so the rate depends on where you buy. Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no statewide sales tax, though Alaska allows local taxes. Most other countries use a value-added tax (VAT) or goods and services tax (GST), which is charged differently but calculated with the same percentage arithmetic.

The one formula behind all three answers

After-tax price = before-tax price × (1 + rate ÷ 100). Fill in any two of the before-tax price, the rate and the after-tax price, and the calculator solves for the third:

  • Add tax: multiply the before-tax price by 1 plus the rate.
  • Remove tax: divide the after-tax total by 1 plus the rate.
  • Find the rate: divide the after-tax price by the before-tax price, subtract 1 and show it as a percentage.

Worked example with the default numbers

A $100.00 item with a 6.5% sales tax rate. Multiply by 1.065 to get $106.50, so the sales tax is $6.50.

Going backwards: a receipt total of $106.50 with a 6.5% rate. Divide by 1.065 and the before-tax price is $100.00.

Finding the rate: a $100.00 item that cost $108.25 at the register. Divide 108.25 by 100, subtract 1, and the rate is 8.25%.

A common mistake when removing tax

Do not take the tax rate off the total. If a receipt says $106.50 and the rate is 6.5%, 6.5% of $106.50 is $6.92, which is wrong. The tax was charged on the before-tax price, so you divide by 1.065, and the real tax is $6.50.

Why the rate depends on where you are

The table on this page lists the general state rate and the highest combined rate once local taxes are added. Tap a state’s rate to put it into the calculator. The difference between states is large, and the combined rate can be much higher than the state rate.

Discounts, coupons and several items

  • Several items: add the prices first, then apply the tax rate to the total, unless some items are exempt.
  • Discounts: in most places tax applies to the price after a store discount. A $80 item at 25% off is $60.00, and with 6.5% tax you pay $63.90. Rules for manufacturer coupons differ by state.
  • Restaurants: tax is normally charged on the bill before the tip.
  • Exemptions: many places tax food, clothing or medicine at a lower rate or not at all, so check your receipt.

Deducting sales tax on your income tax

In the United States you can deduct either state and local income taxes or state and local sales taxes on your federal return, not both, and only if you itemize. Most people deduct income taxes because the amount is usually larger, but someone with a big purchase such as a car may come out ahead with sales tax. This calculator works out the tax on one purchase; it does not prepare a deduction. The IRS has its own Sales Tax Deduction Calculator for that.

Sales tax and VAT

The arithmetic is the same, but VAT and GST are charged at each stage of production and are usually included in the displayed price, while US sales tax is added at the register. You can use this calculator for VAT by entering the VAT rate. For tax-inclusive prices, the VAT Calculator is set up for that.

Common mistakes

  • Using the state rate when a city or county adds its own tax.
  • Subtracting the rate from the total instead of dividing.
  • Leaving all three boxes filled. The after-tax price is then worked out again from the first two.
  • Entering 0.065 instead of 6.5. The rate box takes a percentage.

How to read your result

The large number is the after-tax price. Below it you see the before-tax price, the rate and the tax amount. Press the state rates in the table to compare what the same item would cost in different places. Use your receipt or your local tax authority for the exact rate, because rates change.

The rates in the table are general figures and change over time. Local rates, exemptions and special taxes vary, so confirm the rate for your exact location.

Common questions

How do I add sales tax to a price?

Multiply the before-tax price by one plus the tax rate as a decimal. For a 6.5% rate that means multiplying by 1.065, so a $100 item costs $106.50.

How do I take sales tax out of a total?

Divide the after-tax total by one plus the tax rate as a decimal. If a receipt shows $106.50 and the rate is 6.5%, divide by 1.065 to get the $100 before-tax price. Leave the before-tax box empty.

How can I find out what rate was charged?

Enter the before-tax price and the after-tax price and leave the rate empty. The calculator divides the after-tax price by the before-tax price, subtracts one and shows the result as a percentage.

Why is the combined sales tax higher than the state rate?

Many counties and cities add their own tax on top of the state rate. The combined rate depends on where the sale happens, so check the rate for your exact location.

Is this the same as VAT?

The arithmetic is the same, but VAT and GST are charged at each stage of production and are usually included in the displayed price, while US sales tax is added at the register. You can enter a VAT rate in this calculator.

Can I deduct sales tax on my federal return?

You can deduct either state and local income taxes or state and local sales taxes if you itemize, but not both. The IRS has a Sales Tax Deduction Calculator for working out the amount.
