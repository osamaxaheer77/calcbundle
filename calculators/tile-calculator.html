Tile Calculator

Measure the surface area and tile dimensions in matching units. Tiles are usually bought by full piece or box, so include an appropriate waste allowance for cuts and breakage.

Tile Calculator

Count the tiles for a floor, a wall or any other area.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Divide the area by the area of one tile plus its grout gap, and round up. 300 sq ft of 12 inch tiles with a 0.25 inch gap needs 288 tiles, or 303 to 317 with 5% to 10% waste.

How the tiles are counted

Each tile takes up its own size plus the gap, which is the grout line. A 12 inch tile with a 0.25 inch gap covers a 12.25 inch square. The calculator divides the area by the area of one tile with its gap and rounds up to a whole tile. If you also give the number of tiles in a box, it rounds up to a whole number of boxes. You can enter the area directly or give its length and width, in any mix of units.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens on an area of 300 sq ft, tiles of 12 × 12 inches and a gap of 0.25 inch. Each tile with its gap covers 12.25 × 12.25 = 150.0625 sq in, which is 1.042 sq ft. The area is 300 × 144 = 43,200 sq in, and 43,200 ÷ 150.0625 = 287.88, rounded up to 288 tiles. The tool also shows the area as 300 square feet, or 27.87 square metres. Allowing 5% to 10% for waste gives 303 to 317 tiles.

Allow for waste

Tiles are cut around edges and corners, and some break or have flaws. Most installers buy 5% to 10% more than the count. Diagonal and herringbone layouts use more tile, so add more in those cases. Buy the extra from the same batch, since the shade can change a little from one batch to the next, and keep a few spare tiles for future repairs.

Boxes

Enter the number of tiles in a box and the result also shows boxes. With 10 tiles to a box, the 288 tiles are 29 boxes, and the 5% to 10% waste range is 31 to 32 boxes. Boxes always round up, so you may end up with more tiles than the minimum.

Length and width, and metric units

Choose “Length and width” and the area becomes length times width: 20 ft by 15 ft is 300 sq ft, the same as the default. In metric, a 25 m² floor with 30 × 30 cm tiles and a 3 mm gap needs 273 tiles (25 ÷ 0.303² = 272.3).

Overlap

The gap box accepts a negative number, for tiles that overlap, such as shingle-style tiles. With 12 inch tiles overlapping by 0.5 inch, each tile takes up 11.5 × 11.5 inches, and the same 300 sq ft needs 327 tiles.

Pricing

You can price by the tile, by the box, or by area in square feet, inches, yards, metres or centimetres. Pricing by the tile uses the rounded-up number of tiles and by the box uses the rounded-up boxes, with the cost for the 5% to 10% waste range shown too. Pricing by area uses the exact area with no waste. At $3.50 per tile the default job costs $1,008. At $35 per box of 10 the 29 boxes cost $1,015, and at $3.50 per square foot the area costs $1,050. With waste, the box price runs from $1,085 to $1,120.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the grout gap. It reduces the tile count by about 4% on 12 inch tiles.
  • Mixing units, such as tile in inches and area in square metres, without setting the unit boxes.
  • Buying exactly the count with no waste.
  • Pricing by area and treating it as the final cost.
  • Using the room area without subtracting fixed items like a bathtub or cabinet, if you will not tile under them.

How to read your result

The large number is the tiles needed with no waste, followed by the boxes if you gave a box size, and the area covered. The next line gives the count with 5% to 10% extra. If you entered a price, the cost lines follow, and a note at the bottom explains how the gap was used.

This is an estimate. Complex layouts, patterned tiles and very uneven rooms can need more, so ask your supplier before you buy.

Common questions

How do I calculate how many tiles I need?

Divide the area by the area of one tile plus its grout gap, and round up. For 300 square feet of 12 inch tiles with a 0.25 inch gap, that is 288 tiles.

How much extra should I buy?

Most installers buy 5 to 10 percent more than the count to allow for cuts and breakage. Diagonal and herringbone layouts need more.

Does the grout gap matter?

Yes. Each tile takes up its size plus the gap. A 0.25 inch gap on 12 inch tiles reduces the count by about 4 percent compared with no gap.

How do boxes work?

Enter the number of tiles per box and the calculator rounds the tile count up to a whole number of boxes, with a range for waste.

Can I use metric units?

Yes. Every length and area has its own unit box, so you can mix inches, feet, centimetres, millimetres and metres.

What does a negative gap mean?

It means the tiles overlap. Each tile then takes up its size less the overlap, as with shingle-style tiles.
