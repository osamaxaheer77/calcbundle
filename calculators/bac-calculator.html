BAC Calculator

Quick answer. Blood alcohol is estimated with the Widmark formula from standard drinks of 14 g, body weight and time. For a 155 lb man with 2 drinks over 2 hours the estimate is 0.029%. It is rough and never a guide to driving.
This calculator gives a rough estimate for education. It cannot tell you whether you are safe to drive, and no calculator can. If you have been drinking, do not drive.

What counts as a standard drink

In the United States, one standard drink contains about 14 grams of pure alcohol, which is 0.6 fluid ounces. The National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism lists these examples: a 12-ounce can of regular beer at 5% alcohol by volume, a 5-ounce glass of wine at 12%, and a 1.5-ounce shot of distilled spirits at 40%. Stronger or larger pours count as more than one standard drink. Craft beers, large wine glasses and mixed drinks often hold more than one.

The Widmark formula

The calculator uses the classic Widmark equation: BAC = alcohol in grams ÷ (body weight in grams × distribution ratio) × 100, then subtracts an elimination rate of 0.015 percentage points per hour for the time since the first drink. The distribution ratio, 0.68 for men and 0.55 for women, reflects average differences in body water, which changes how diluted the alcohol becomes. It is an average, not a measurement of you.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens with a man weighing 155 lb (70.31 kg) who has had 2 standard drinks, with 2 hours since the first drink. The alcohol is 2 × 14 = 28 g. The raw estimate is 28 ÷ (70,306.7 × 0.68) × 100 = 0.0586%. Two hours of elimination take off 2 × 0.015 = 0.030, which leaves 0.029%. The tool says “Mild impairment possible.” and that it would take about 1.9 hours to reach 0.00%.

0.150% and aboveSevere impairment, risk of serious harm.

A level of 0.08% is the legal limit for driving in most US states. Other countries and some US states set lower limits, and limits for drivers under 21, commercial drivers and some other groups can be much lower or zero. Impairment can begin well below any legal limit.

Why this is only a rough estimate

  • It is an average. The Widmark model works best a few hours after a single dose in a fasted state, and even then its error can be around 20%.
  • Food and drinking pattern. Eating, drinking quickly and spacing drinks over hours all change the real peak.
  • Body and health. Body fat, age, medications, health conditions and liver function all change how alcohol is handled.
  • Drink strength. The tool counts standard drinks of 14 g. Your drinks may contain more.
  • Rising BAC. BAC can keep rising for a while after you stop drinking, so an estimate taken at the last drink can be too low.

Common mistakes

  • Using the result to decide whether to drive. Do not.
  • Counting a large cocktail or a tall pour as one drink.
  • Leaving out the hours since the first drink, or counting from the last drink.
  • Assuming coffee, water or a shower lowers the BAC. Only time does.
  • Entering the weight in the wrong unit.

How to read your result

The large number is the estimated BAC as a percentage. Below it are the hours until the estimate reaches zero and a short message. Treat all three as a rough illustration of how drink count, weight, sex and time interact, and as a reminder that the only safe amount to drive on is none.

Common questions

What is a standard drink?

In the United States, a standard drink contains about 14 grams of pure alcohol. That is roughly a 12-ounce beer at 5 percent, a 5-ounce glass of wine at 12 percent, or a 1.5-ounce shot of 40 percent spirits.

How does the Widmark formula work?

It divides the grams of alcohol by body weight in grams times a distribution ratio of 0.68 for men and 0.55 for women, multiplies by 100, and subtracts 0.015 percentage points for each hour since the first drink.

How accurate is a BAC calculator?

It is rough. Food, drinking pattern, body composition, medication and health all change the real level, and even in good conditions the model can be about 20 percent off.

Can I use this to decide whether I can drive?

No. It is not a test of fitness to drive. Impairment can begin well below the legal limit, and no calculator can say you are safe. If you have been drinking, do not drive.

Why does the result differ for men and women?

The formula uses a lower distribution ratio for women, 0.55 against 0.68 for men, based on average differences in body water. That makes the estimate about 24 percent higher for the same weight and drinks.

Does coffee or a shower sober you up?

No. Only time lowers blood alcohol, at a fairly steady rate.
