Exponent Calculator

Enter any two of base, exponent, and result to solve for the third — including fractional exponents.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Enter any two of base, exponent and result. 2 to the power 5 is 32, 2 to the power of −3 is 0.125 and 9 to the power 0.5 is 3. Solving 2x = 10 gives x = 3.3219280949.

What the calculator does

Enter any two of the base, the exponent and the result, and the calculator finds the third. With a base and an exponent it works out the power. With a base and a result it solves for the exponent using logarithms. With an exponent and a result it finds the base using roots. A tick box lets you use e, about 2.71828, as the base.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens with a base of 2 and an exponent of 5. Two to the fifth power is 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32. Now clear the exponent and type 32 as the result: the exponent is log 32 ÷ log 2 = 5. Clear the base instead and the base is the 5th root of 32, which is 2. A fractional exponent works as a root: 160.5 is the square root of 16, which is 4.

Using e as the base

Tick the box and the base becomes e, which is used for continuous growth and decay. With an exponent of 1 the result is 2.7182818285, and with an exponent of 2 it is 7.3890560989. Money growing continuously at 5% for 10 years is multiplied by e0.5 = 1.6487212707. When you tick the box, the base field is cleared and disabled, so enter an exponent or a result, but not both.

Where the calculator is used

Exponents describe anything that grows or shrinks by a constant factor. At 7% a year, $1 grows to $1.97 after 10 years, $3.87 after 20 and $7.61 after 30 (1.07 raised to those powers). To find how long that growth takes, solve for the exponent: the years to double at 7% satisfy 1.07x = 2, so x = 10.2447683511.

Large and small results

Results between 0.0000000001 and 1,000,000,000,000,000 are shown in full, with up to 10 decimal places. Anything outside that range switches to scientific notation with six decimals. So 249 is 562,949,953,421,312, but 250 is 1.125900e+15 and 2100 is 1.267651e+30. A tiny result such as 2−40 appears as 9.094947e-13.

When there is no real answer

  • Solving for the exponent needs a positive base that is not 1, and a positive result. Base 1 has the same value for every exponent.
  • An even root of a negative number, such as the base in x2 = −4, is not a real number. The calculator reports NAN.
  • A negative result with a fractional exponent has no real base. A negative result needs an odd whole-number exponent, like x3 = −8, which gives −2.
  • An exponent of 0 gives 1 for every base, so you cannot solve for the base from it.
  • A negative base with a fractional exponent, such as (−2)0.5, also needs an imaginary number and shows NAN.

Two special cases: (−2)3 is -8 and (−2)2 is 4, because a negative base keeps its sign for odd whole exponents only. The calculator shows 00 as 1.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up −22, which is −4, with (−2)2, which is 4. Enter −2 as the base to get 4.
  • Entering a fraction like 1/3 in the exponent box. Use the decimal form, 0.3333333333.
  • Filling in all three boxes. Only two should be filled, and the third is left blank.
  • Adding exponents when bases differ. The rule am × an = am+n needs the same base.
  • Mixing up the result and the exponent when solving for the exponent.

How to read your result

The large number is the answer. The line under it repeats the problem with the answer in bold, for example 25 = 32, 2x = 32 → x = 5, or x5 = 32 → x = 2. For very big results, use the Scientific Notation Calculator to read the exponent form, and the Big Number Calculator if you need every digit.

Results use ordinary floating-point arithmetic, so very long answers are rounded, and tiny rounding differences can appear in the last digits.

Common questions

Can I enter a fractional exponent?

Yes. Enter it in decimal form, such as 0.5 instead of 1/2. A fractional exponent works as a root, so 9 to the power 0.5 is the square root of 9, which is 3.

How do I solve for the exponent?

Leave the exponent blank and enter the base and the result. The calculator divides the logarithm of the result by the logarithm of the base. For 2 and 32, the exponent is 5.

How do I find the base?

Leave the base blank and enter the exponent and the result. The base is the result raised to the power of one over the exponent. For an exponent of 5 and a result of 32, the base is 2.

What does a negative exponent mean?

It means take the reciprocal. Two to the power of minus 3 is 1 divided by 2 cubed, which is 1 divided by 8, or 0.125.

Why do I see NAN?

NAN means there is no real-number answer, for example an even root of a negative number, or a negative base with a fractional exponent. Those need imaginary numbers.

What does the e box do?

It uses e, about 2.71828, as the base. This is common in growth and decay problems. With an exponent of 1 the result is 2.7182818285.
