Date Calculator

Choose between comparing two dates and adding or subtracting time. The option to include the end date changes the day count by one, which matters for bookings and inclusive schedules.

Days Between Two Dates

Add to or Subtract from a Date

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. To find the time between two dates, count the days from the earlier to the later one. From January 1 to December 25, 2026 is 358 calendar days, or 11 months and 24 days. To add time, start from a date and add years, months, weeks and days: January 15, 2026 plus 1 year, 2 months and 10 days is March 25, 2027.

What this date calculator does

The page has two tools. Days Between Two Dates tells you how long it is from one date to another, in years, months and days, in total weeks and days, and as a plain number of calendar days. Add or Subtract Time starts from a date, adds or subtracts years, months, weeks and days, and gives the resulting date with its day of the week.

Worked example: days between two dates

From January 1, 2026 to December 25, 2026:

ResultValue
Years, months and days11 months 24 days
Weeks and days51 weeks, 1 day
Calendar days (end day not counted)358
Calendar days (include end day)359

The year/month/day breakdown counts whole months from the start date: eleven months after January 1 is December 1, and December 25 is 24 days later. The total in days is a straight count of days and does not depend on that breakdown.

Should the end day be included?

By default the gap between January 1 and January 2 is 1 day: the time that has elapsed. Tick “Include end day” and the same span counts as 2 days, because both calendar dates are counted. Use the default for ages, countdowns and notice periods. Use the inclusive count for something that covers both dates, such as a rental from the 1st through the 10th, which is 10 days inclusive but 9 days elapsed.

Worked example: adding time to a date

Starting on January 15, 2026, add 1 year, 2 months and 10 days. The calculator adds the years and months first (March 15, 2027), then the 10 days, giving March 25, 2027, a Thursday.

Subtracting works the same way in reverse. For example, a 90-day notice period that ends on December 25, 2026 started on September 26, 2026. And 100 days after January 1, 2026 is April 11, 2026.

What happens at the end of a month

Months have different lengths, so adding months to a date near the end of a month needs a rule. The calculator keeps the day inside the target month. Adding one month to January 31, 2026 gives February 28, 2026, the last day of February, and adding a year to February 29, 2028 gives February 28, 2029. Weeks and days are added after the months, so they always move the date by exactly that many days.

Calendar facts that affect date maths

  • A common year has 365 days and a leap year has 366. A year is a leap year if it is divisible by 4, except century years that are not divisible by 400, so 2028 is a leap year and 2100 is not.
  • Months have 28 to 31 days, so “one month” is not a fixed number of days.
  • A week is always 7 days, so adding weeks is exact.
  • The calculator counts calendar days only. It does not remove weekends or holidays. As a rough guide, about 5 of every 7 days are weekdays, so 358 days is roughly 256 weekdays before holidays.

Common uses

  • Counting down to a wedding, trip or exam.
  • Finding the date a deadline, warranty or notice period ends.
  • Working out how long ago something happened.
  • Checking how many nights a booking covers (elapsed days) or how many calendar days (inclusive).

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting whether the end day counts. It changes the answer by exactly one.
  • Assuming all months are 30 days when planning “three months from now”.
  • Expecting business days. For working days, subtract weekends and holidays yourself or use the Day Counter.
  • Entering the start date after the end date. The first tool needs the earlier date first.

How to read your result

Look at the unit you need. For a deadline, the resulting date and weekday matter most. For a duration, the total days or weeks are easier to compare than a breakdown into months. If the answer will be used for a contract or a legal deadline, check how that document counts days, because some count both ends and some skip weekends.

This calculator counts calendar days and does not account for time zones, public holidays or the rules of any specific contract or law.

Common questions

Does the add/subtract tool handle month ends correctly?

Yes. Adding one month to January 31 gives the last day of February, because the day is kept inside the target month. Years and months are added first, then weeks and days.

Can I subtract time instead of adding it?

Yes. Switch the operation to Subtract in the second tool to count backward from the start date.

Why is the include end day option there?

Some counts include both the first and the last day, for example a rental from the 1st through the 10th. The option adds one day to the total for that case.

Does the calculator skip weekends or holidays?

No. It counts calendar days. For working days, remove weekends and holidays yourself or use the Day Counter.

Why do I get months and days as well as total days?

The breakdown shows whole months and the leftover days from your start date, which is easier to read for long spans. The total days figure is an exact count that does not depend on month lengths.

How are leap years handled?

The calculator uses the standard calendar, so February 29 exists in leap years and the day count is exact across years.
