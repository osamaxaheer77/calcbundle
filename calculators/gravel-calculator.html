How much gravel you need

The volume is the area times the depth. Choose a rectangle, a circle or a known area, enter the depth, and the calculator gives the gravel in cubic yards, cubic metres, cubic feet and litres, plus the weight in kilograms, pounds, metric tons and short tons. Add a price to get the cost, per unit or per bag.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens on a rectangle 20 ft by 10 ft covered 3 inches deep with ordinary gravel (stones 0.2 to 2 inches, 1,680 kg/m³). The area is 200 square feet, and 3 inches is 0.25 feet, so the volume is 200 × 0.25 = 50 cubic feet. Divided by 27 that is 1.85 cubic yards, or 1.42 cubic metres, or 1,416 litres. At 1,680 kg/m³ it weighs 2,379 kg (5,244 lb), which is 2.38 metric tons or 2.62 short tons.

Choosing the depth

Depth matters more than anything else. Commonly used depths run from about 2 to 3 inches for a garden path or decorative bed to 4 to 6 inches for a driveway or a base, but follow the advice for your own project and your local supplier. The table shows the 20 by 10 foot area at four depths.

Weight and density

Gravel is sold by weight as well as by volume. The weight is the volume times the density. The calculator offers four materials and lets you enter your own density in kg/m³, g/cm³ or lb/ft³. Wet material is heavier than dry, and sand packs differently from stone.

As a rule of thumb from the default density, a cubic yard of loose gravel weighs about 1.42 short tons (about 2,832 lb), so a rule of roughly 1.4 tons per cubic yard fits ordinary gravel.

Adding an allowance

Gravel settles and packs down after it is spread, so order a little extra, often 5% to 10%. For the default job, 10% more is 2.04 cubic yards. Delivery yards usually sell by the cubic yard or by the ton, and a truck load is a set size, so ask the supplier how much each load carries.

Price and cost

Enter a price and choose the unit: per cubic foot, cubic yard, cubic metre, litre, pound, short ton, kilogram, metric ton or bag. At $40 per cubic yard the default job costs 1.85 × $40 = $74.07. At $35 per short ton it costs 2.62 × $35 = $91.77. If you choose per bag, enter the bag size too. With bags of 0.5 cubic feet at $5, the 50 cubic feet need 100 bags, or $500.

Types and sizes of gravel

The calculator’s first material is gravel with stones from 0.2 to 2 inches. Suppliers sell many grades: small rounded stones such as pea gravel for paths and decorative beds, crushed angular stone that locks together for driveways and bases, and mixes with fines that compact into a firm layer. Angular crushed stone packs tighter than rounded stone, so a driveway base often uses crushed stone, while a path may use whatever looks best. Ask your supplier which grade suits the job, then choose the closest material in the calculator or enter the density they quote.

Preparing the ground

  • Clear the area and level the soil before you spread gravel.
  • Landscape fabric under the gravel helps keep weeds and soil from mixing in.
  • Edging holds the gravel in place and keeps the depth even.
  • Compact the base layer on a driveway before adding the top layer.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the depth unit. Three inches entered as 3 feet gives 12 times too much.
  • Ordering by volume when the supplier sells by weight, or the reverse.
  • Skipping the 5% to 10% extra for settling.
  • Using the dry density for wet material.
  • Measuring the area with the wrong shape setting.

How to read your result

The large number is the volume of gravel in cubic yards, with the same volume in other units below it. The next lines give the weight and the tons. If you entered a price, the last line shows the cost. Treat every figure as an estimate and ask your supplier for the actual load size.

This is only an estimate. The real amount depends on the type of gravel, how tightly it is packed and how wet it is.

Common questions

How many cubic yards of gravel do I need?

Multiply the length, width and depth in feet to get cubic feet, then divide by 27. A 20 by 10 foot area at 3 inches deep is 50 cubic feet, or about 1.85 cubic yards.

How much does gravel weigh?

Loose gravel weighs about 1,680 kg per cubic metre, which is about 105 pounds per cubic foot or roughly 1.4 short tons per cubic yard. Sand and wet material differ.

How much extra should I order?

Gravel settles and packs down after it is spread, so order a little extra, often 5% to 10%.

Should I buy by the yard, the ton or the bag?

Delivery yards usually sell by the cubic yard or by the ton, which is cheaper for large jobs. Bags suit small repairs. Enter the bag size and the calculator shows how many to buy.

What depth of gravel do I need?

It depends on the use. A path or decorative bed often uses 2 to 3 inches, and a driveway or base more. Check the advice for your project and supplier.

Can I enter my own density?

Yes. Choose the option to enter your own density, in kg/m3, g/cm3 or lb/ft3, if your material is different.
