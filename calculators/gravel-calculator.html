How much gravel is needed

The volume is the area multiplied by the depth. A 20 by 10 foot area covered 3 inches deep is 200 square feet times 0.25 foot, which is 50 cubic feet, or about 1.85 cubic yards. The calculator shows the volume in cubic yards, cubic meters, cubic feet and liters.

Weight

Gravel is sold by weight as well as by volume. The weight is the volume times the density of the material. Loose gravel weighs about 1,680 kilograms per cubic meter, which is about 105 pounds per cubic foot. Sand, wet material, and gravel mixed with sand are heavier or lighter, so pick the closest material or enter your own density.

Getting the amount right

Gravel settles and packs down after it is spread, so order a little extra, often 5 to 10 percent. Delivery yards usually sell by the cubic yard or by the ton. If you buy by the bag, enter the bag size to see how many to buy.

This is only an estimate. The weight depends on the type of stone, how tightly it is packed and how wet it is.

Common questions

How do I calculate cubic yards of gravel?

Multiply length by width by depth, all in feet, and divide by 27. For 20 by 10 feet at a depth of 0.25 foot, that is 50 divided by 27, or about 1.85 cubic yards.

How much does a cubic yard of gravel weigh?

About 1.4 to 1.5 short tons, or around 2,800 to 3,000 pounds, depending on the stone and how wet it is.

How deep should gravel be?

A driveway usually needs 4 to 6 inches, a path 2 to 3 inches, and a drainage bed anywhere from 6 inches to a foot.

Should I order extra?

Yes. Add 5 to 10 percent because gravel compacts after it is spread.

How do I find the area of a circle?

Pick the circle shape and enter the diameter. The area is pi times the radius squared.
