Day of the Week Calculator

Enter a complete calendar date to identify its weekday. This can help with historical dates, scheduling and checking which weekday a future anniversary will land on.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Enter a month, day and year to find the weekday. January 1, 2025 was a Wednesday, the 1st of 53 Wednesdays that year, and July 4, 2026 is a Saturday, the 185th day of the year.

What the calculator does

Enter a month, day and year, and the calculator names the weekday. It also shows which day of the year the date is, which occurrence of that weekday it is within the year, and a calendar for the month with the date highlighted. It works for any year from 1 to 9999.

The page opens on today’s date, so the examples below use fixed dates. Type them in to reproduce the results.

Worked example: January 1, 2025

January 1, 2025 is a Wednesday. It is day 1st of 365 in the year, with 364 left. It is also the 1st of 53 Wednesdays in 2025, with 52 left. There are 53 Wednesdays in 2025 because 365 days is 52 weeks plus one extra day, and that extra day is a Wednesday.

Seven days later, January 8, 2025, is a Wednesday as well, since a complete week always returns to the same weekday.

How the weekday is found

The calculator counts the days from a known date, January 1, 1970, which was a Thursday, and divides by 7. The remainder names the weekday. For July 4, 2026, there are 20,638 days since January 1, 1970. Counting Sunday as 0, Thursday is 4, so the weekday is (4 + 20,638) mod 7 = 6, which is Saturday. The tool also reports it as the 185th day of the year (185th of 365, 180 left) and the 27th Saturday of 52 or 53.

Why dates move one weekday each year

A common year has 365 days, which is 52 weeks and 1 day, so each date falls one weekday later than the year before. A leap year has 366 days, 52 weeks and 2 days, so dates after February 29 move forward two weekdays. Here is how January 1 and December 25 move over the next few years.

Leap years and dates that do not exist

The leap year rules are built in: a year is a leap year if it divides by 4, except century years, which must divide by 400. So 2024 and 2000 are leap years, while 1900 and 2100 are not. A date that does not exist, such as February 29, 2025, rolls on to the next valid day, March 1, 2025, a Saturday, and the result says so. The same applies to February 29, 1900, which becomes March 1, 1900, a Thursday. February 29, 2000 is real and was a Tuesday.

Friday the 13th

Because the calendar repeats exactly every 400 years, you can count how often the 13th falls on each weekday. Over a full cycle of 4,800 months it is a Friday 688 times, more than any other weekday. In 2026 there are 3 Fridays the 13th: February 13, 2026, March 13, 2026, November 13, 2026.

Old dates and calendars

The calculator uses the Gregorian calendar for every date. It was introduced in October 1582: the day after Thursday, October 4, 1582 was Friday, October 15, 1582 (a Friday in the tool). Countries switched at different times, some centuries later, so a historical date written in the old Julian calendar can fall on a different weekday. Dates before 1582 here show the weekday in the Gregorian calendar extended backwards.

Doing it in your head

The doomsday rule gives a shortcut. In any year, April 4, June 6, August 8, October 10 and December 12 fall on the same weekday, called the doomsday. For 2026 that weekday is Saturday. From there you can count to any date in the year. The calculator saves the arithmetic and covers every year.

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong month number. The month box lists names, so pick the right one.
  • Forgetting the year. The same date falls on a different weekday most years.
  • Applying a modern weekday to an old Julian date. Check which calendar the source used.
  • Entering February 29 in a year that is not a leap year. The calculator moves it to March 1.
  • Reading the week count as a calendar week number. It counts occurrences of that weekday in the year.

How to read your result

The large word is the weekday. Below it are the day of the year with the days that remain, and the position of that weekday in the year, such as the 40th Sunday of 52, with the number left. The calendar underneath marks the date and shades weekends.

The weekday follows the Gregorian calendar for every date. For historical research on dates before the calendar changed in your country, check which calendar the record used.

Common questions

How do I find the day of the week for a date?

Enter the month, day and year. The calculator counts the days since a known date, January 1, 1970, which was a Thursday, and names the weekday from the remainder after dividing by 7.

What day of the week was I born on?

Enter your date of birth. The result names the weekday, and the calendar shows the rest of that month.

Does it handle leap years?

Yes. A year is a leap year if it divides by 4, except century years that do not divide by 400. So 2000 was a leap year, but 1900 and 2100 are not.

What happens if I enter a date that does not exist?

The date rolls on to the next valid day. For example, February 29, 2025 becomes March 1, 2025, and the result says so.

Why is the 13th more often a Friday?

The Gregorian calendar repeats every 400 years, and over that cycle the 13th falls on a Friday 688 times, slightly more than any other weekday.

Does it work for dates before 1582?

Yes, but it uses the Gregorian calendar extended backwards. A date recorded in the old Julian calendar can fall on a different weekday.
