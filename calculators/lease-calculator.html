Lease Calculator

Find the monthly payment on a lease, or the interest rate hidden in a quoted payment, from the asset value, the residual value and the term.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A lease payment covers the asset's loss in value plus interest. A $20,000 asset with an $8,000 residual over 3 years at 6% costs $405.06 a month. A $400 quote on the same lease hides a rate of 5.58%.

How a lease payment works

In a lease you pay for the part of an asset you use, not the whole thing. The asset value is what it is worth at the start and the residual value is what it is expected to be worth at the end. Your payments have to cover the drop in value between the two, plus interest on the money tied up in the asset. That is why a higher residual value gives a lower payment, even though you hand the asset back.

The formula

The calculator treats the lease like a loan that ends with a balloon equal to the residual value. For a monthly rate i over n months, payment = (asset value − residual ÷ (1 + i)n) × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)−n). At a 0% rate this is just (asset value − residual) ÷ n.

Tool 1: payment from an interest rate

With the defaults of a $20,000 asset, an $8,000 residual, 3 years and 6% interest, the monthly payment is $405.06. Over 36 payments you pay $14,582, and total interest is $2,582 (payments plus residual minus asset value). At 0% the payment would be $333.33, which is the depreciation alone: $12,000 ÷ 36. The same idea, with a $12,000 drop over 36 months, is $333.33 a month before interest.

Tool 2: the rate hidden in a quoted payment

Dealers and lessors often quote a payment without the rate. Enter the asset value, residual value, term and payment, and the calculator finds the rate at which the payments and the residual are worth exactly the asset value today. For a $20,000 asset, an $8,000 residual and a $400 payment over 36 months, the hidden rate is 5.581% a year. You pay $14,400 in total and the interest is $2,400. Lease terms are usually short because the asset loses value quickly and the residual becomes harder to predict. The longer the term, the smaller the payment, but the more total interest you pay and the longer you are committed to the contract.

What this calculator leaves out

  • Fees, taxes, deposits and mileage or wear charges, which can change the real payment.
  • The cost of buying the asset at the end of the lease.
  • The money factor and capitalized cost reduction used on car leases. For a car lease, use the Auto Lease Calculator.
  • Payments at the start of each period. The calculator assumes payments at the end.

Common mistakes

  • Entering a residual that is equal to or higher than the asset value. The residual must be lower.
  • Comparing lease payments without comparing residual values and terms.
  • Treating the hidden rate as the full cost when fees are extra.
  • Forgetting that you own nothing at the end of a lease unless you buy it.

How to read your result

In Tool 1 the large number is the monthly payment, with the total payments, residual value and total interest below. In Tool 2 it is the rate in the quoted payment. Use the table of payments and rates to see how sensitive the answer is, and compare the rate with a loan before deciding to lease.

This calculator gives estimates. Real leases include fees, taxes and limits that change the payment. It is not financial advice.

Common questions

What is a residual value?

The residual value is the estimated worth of the asset at the end of the lease. The lessor sets it at the start, and it is the part of the asset you do not pay off through your monthly payments.

Why does a higher residual value lower the payment?

Your payments only need to cover the fall in value from the start to the end of the lease, plus interest. A higher residual value means the asset loses less value, so there is less to pay off each month.

How is the interest rate found from a payment?

The calculator searches for the monthly rate at which the present value of all the payments, plus the present value of the residual value, equals the asset value. That rate times twelve is the yearly rate shown.

Can I use it for a car lease?

You can get a rough figure, but car leases usually add a money factor, fees, sales tax and a down payment. The Auto Lease Calculator handles those details.

Why does it say the residual value must be lower?

If the residual value is equal to or greater than the asset value, the asset is not losing value, so there is nothing to pay off through the payments and the calculation does not make sense.

Are fees and taxes included?

No. Fees, taxes, deposits and usage charges vary by lease and are not included.
