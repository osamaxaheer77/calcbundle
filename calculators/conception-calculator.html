The fertile window

An egg can be fertilized for about a day after ovulation, and sperm can survive for three to five days in the body. That means the days when intercourse can lead to pregnancy run from about five days before ovulation to a day or two after it. The chance is highest in the two or three days just before ovulation and on the day itself. The calculator estimates the window from your cycle, so it works best if your cycles are regular.

How the dates are worked out

Ovulation usually happens about 14 days before the next period. The calculator counts from the first day of your last period, adds your cycle length to find the next period, and goes back 14 days for ovulation. It then shows:

  • Most probable conception days: 2 days before ovulation to 3 days after.
  • Ovulation window: 2 days either side of the estimated ovulation date.
  • Best days for intercourse: 5 days before ovulation to 2 days after.
  • Pregnancy test date: 5 days before the next period.
  • Due date if pregnant: 280 days after the last period, adjusted by the difference between your cycle length and 28 days.

Worked example with a 28-day cycle

The page starts with today as the first day of your last period. For an example with fixed dates, suppose the last period began on January 1, 2026 and the cycle is 28 days. The next period is due on Jan 29, 2026, so ovulation is estimated for Jan 15, 2026. The most probable conception days are Jan 13 – Jan 18, the ovulation window is Jan 13 – Jan 17 and the best days for intercourse are Jan 10 – Jan 17. A pregnancy test is suggested on Jan 24, 2026, and the due date if you conceive this cycle is Oct 8, 2026.

Test date and due date

A home pregnancy test is most reliable from about the day your next period is due, though some tests can pick up pregnancy a few days earlier, which is why the calculator suggests a date five days before. If you do become pregnant this cycle, your due date is 280 days after the start of your last period, adjusted if your cycle is longer or shorter than 28 days. When this works best, and when it does not

  • It works best with regular cycles. If your cycle length varies a lot, ovulation is harder to predict and the dates are less reliable.
  • Ovulation can shift from cycle to cycle because of stress, illness, travel or changes in routine.
  • Ovulation tests, basal body temperature charts or cervical mucus tracking can give a more precise picture.
  • The calculator is not a reliable form of birth control and cannot confirm fertility.

When to talk to a doctor

See a doctor if you have been trying for a year without success, or six months if you are over 35, or if your periods are very irregular, absent or painful. A doctor can check for causes that a calendar cannot.

Common mistakes

  • Counting from the end of the last period rather than the first day.
  • Using a typical 28-day cycle when yours is much shorter or longer.
  • Testing too early and reading a negative as final.
  • Treating the estimate as proof of conception.

How to read your result

The large range is the most probable days for conception, followed by the ovulation window, the best days, the test date, the next period and the due date. The calendar shades these days. Use the table to plan ahead for the next six cycles.

These dates are estimates from an average cycle. They are not medical advice. Speak to a doctor if you have trouble conceiving.

Common questions

When am I most likely to conceive?

In the days just before ovulation and on the day itself. The calculator shows the most probable conception days as a range of about six days around the estimated ovulation date.

How do I find my ovulation date?

Ovulation usually happens about 14 days before your next period starts. Count from the first day of your last period, add your cycle length, and subtract 14 days.

How long can sperm survive?

Sperm can live for three to five days in the female body, so intercourse a few days before ovulation can still lead to pregnancy.

When should I take a pregnancy test?

The most reliable time is on or after the day your period is due. The calculator suggests a date five days before the next expected period, which some sensitive tests can detect.

Does an irregular cycle change the result?

Yes. If your cycle length varies a lot, ovulation is harder to predict and these dates are less reliable. Ovulation tests or tracking can help.

Why does the page start with today's date?

It fills the first day of your last period with today so that results appear immediately. Change it to the real date for an accurate estimate.
