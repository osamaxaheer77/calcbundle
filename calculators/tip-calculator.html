Tip Calculator

Enter the pre-tip bill, preferred percentage and number of people. The split is based on the bill plus the calculated tip; check how tax, service charges or rounding are treated.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A tip is a percentage of the bill added to the total. On a $55.00 bill a 15% tip is $8.25, making the total $63.25, or $15.81 each when split four ways. At 20% the tip is $11.00.

What the calculator does

Enter the bill, the tip percentage and the number of people. The calculator multiplies the bill by the percentage to get the tip, adds it to the bill, and divides both the tip and the total by the number of people. It updates as you type, so you can try several percentages quickly. It splits everything equally.

Worked example with the default numbers

A $55.00 bill with a 15% tip: the tip is $55.00 × 0.15 = $8.25 and the total is $63.25. At 20% the tip is $11.00 and the total is $66.00. Split four ways, each person pays $15.81 in total and $2.06 of the tip.

Mental shortcuts

  • 10%: move the decimal point one place left. $55.00 becomes $5.50.
  • 20%: double the 10% figure. $5.50 doubled is $11.00.
  • 15%: add half of the 10% figure to the 10% figure. $5.50 + $2.75 = $8.25.
  • Rounding: round the total up to a whole dollar for an easy tip, as long as the extra is fair.

Before or after tax?

There is no universal rule. Some people tip on the bill before tax, others on the total including tax. With an 8% sales tax on the $55 bill the tax is $4.40. A 15% tip on the pre-tax $55.00 is $8.25; on the post-tax $59.40 it is $8.91, a difference of only $0.66. Enter whichever amount you tip on. If the bill already includes a service charge or gratuity, check it before adding more.

Splitting a bill

Equal splits are simple but not always fair when orders differ a lot. In that case, split by item cost and tip on each share. Rounding can leave a few cents over or under: for four people the exact share of the default total is $15.8125, so the calculator shows $15.81, and rounding each share up to $16.00 leaves a small extra for the server.

Tipping on a discounted bill

If your bill is reduced by a coupon or a promotion, you can tip on the amount you actually pay or on the original price. Many diners tip on the original price, because the server did the same work. For a $55.00 meal with 20% off, you pay $44.00. A 15% tip on that is $6.60, while a 15% tip on the original $55.00 is $8.25, a difference of $1.65. Use the Discount Calculator first to find the reduced bill, then enter whichever amount you choose.

Large groups and service charges

Some restaurants add an automatic gratuity for large parties. If one is already on the bill, you do not need to add the full amount again, though you can add a little for exceptional service. When a group is splitting, decide the tip percentage first on the whole bill, then divide, so that nobody is working out a different percentage on their own share. Entering the number of people in the calculator does this for you.

Tipping customs

Customs vary by country and by type of service. In the United States, 15% to 20% is typical for sit-down restaurant service, with more for exceptional service. Delivery, bars, salons and taxis follow different conventions, and in many countries a service charge is already included. Check local norms if you are somewhere unfamiliar. If you are a worker who receives tips, they count as taxable income, and the IRS explains the reporting rules.

Common mistakes

  • Tipping on a total that already includes a service charge.
  • Forgetting to enter the number of people when splitting.
  • Entering the tip as a dollar amount in the percentage box.
  • Splitting equally when one person ordered much more.

How to read your result

The large number is the tip. Below it are the total bill, and, if you entered more than one person, the tip and total per person. Compare two percentages by changing the box and watching the numbers update. For tax on a purchase use the Sales Tax Calculator, and for a discount before tipping use the Discount Calculator.

Tipping is a custom, not a rule. The suggested percentages are common figures, not requirements.

Common questions

Should everyone get an equal tip if orders differ a lot?

That is a personal or group decision. Equal splitting is simplest, but some groups prefer splitting in proportion to what each person ordered.

Is there a standard tip percentage?

No fixed standard exists, but 15% to 20% is common in the US for good restaurant service, with more for exceptional service.

Should I tip on the bill before or after tax?

Either is common. Enter whichever amount you tip on. The difference is small: on a $55 bill with 8% tax, a 15% tip changes by about 66 cents.

How do I calculate a 20% tip quickly?

Move the decimal point one place left to get 10%, then double it. For $55.00, 10% is $5.50 and 20% is $11.00.

Does the calculator split the tip as well?

Yes. When you enter more than one person, it shows the tip per person and the total per person, dividing both equally.

Are tips taxable?

Tips received by workers are taxable income and are subject to reporting rules. The IRS explains the details.
