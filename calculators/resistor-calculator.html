Reading the color bands

On a 4-band resistor, the first two bands are significant digits, the third is a multiplier (a power of ten to multiply by), and the fourth is tolerance. A 5-band resistor adds a third significant digit for extra precision before the multiplier and tolerance bands.

Parallel vs. series

Resistors in series simply add up: Rtotal = R1 + R2 + …. Resistors in parallel combine differently, since current splits between them: 1/Rtotal = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + … — the total is always less than the smallest individual resistor.

This tool covers 4-band and 6-band-minus-temperature-coefficient resistors (4 and 5 bands). Specialty resistors with a temperature coefficient band aren't included.

Common questions

Which end do I read the bands from?

Start from the side where the bands are closer together, or where the tolerance band (often gold or silver) is clearly separated at the opposite end — that separated band is always read last.

What if there's no tolerance band?

A resistor with only 3 significant/multiplier bands and no separate tolerance band is assumed to have a ±20% tolerance, per the standard convention — not covered by this calculator's band-count options.
