Quick answer. To add fractions, give them a common denominator and add the numerators. 2/7 + 3/8 = 16/56 + 21/56 = 37/56 (about 0.66071429). Multiply straight across, and divide by multiplying by the flipped fraction.

What a fraction measures

A fraction compares a part with a whole. The bottom number (denominator) says how many equal pieces the whole is cut into and the top number (numerator) says how many pieces you have. A chocolate bar with 8 squares, of which you break off 3, is 3/8 of the bar. Multiplying or dividing both numbers by the same amount does not change the value, so 3/8, 6/16 and 12/32 are the same size.

Mixed numbers and improper fractions

A mixed number such as 2 3/4 means two wholes plus three quarters. The same amount as an improper fraction is 11/4 (2 × 4 + 3 = 11). The calculator lets you type the whole part directly, so you do not have to convert first. Results larger than one are also shown as a mixed number and as a decimal.

Adding and subtracting

Pieces must be the same size before they can be combined, so both fractions are rescaled to a common denominator, the smallest number both denominators divide into. Take the page’s example inputs 2/7 + 3/8. The smallest common denominator is 56. Then 2/7 = 16/56 and 3/8 = 21/56, so the sum is 16/56 + 21/56 = 37/56, which is 0.66071429 as a decimal. Subtracting works the same way: 3/8 − 2/7 = 5/56.

The step-by-step panel shows every stage: finding the common denominator, rescaling each fraction, combining the numerators and simplifying. If both fractions already share a denominator, such as 1/5 + 2/5, the panel says so and moves straight to adding.

Multiplying and dividing

Multiplication needs no common denominator: multiply the numerators, multiply the denominators, then simplify. For 1 3/4 × 2/3, first write 1 3/4 as 7/4. Then 7/4 × 2/3 = 14/12, which simplifies to 7/6 or 1 1/6. Division is multiplication by the reciprocal (the flipped fraction): 5/6 ÷ 1/3 = 5/6 × 3/1 = 15/6 = 5/2, or 2 1/2. This works because dividing by 1/3 asks how many thirds fit into the amount, which is the same as multiplying by 3.

Simplifying

To simplify, divide the numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor until only 1 divides both. The simplify tool does this for you: 2/98 simplifies to 1/49 because 2 divides both. The value is unchanged; it is just written in its plainest form, which makes comparisons easier.

Converting decimals and fractions

To turn a decimal into a fraction, write the digits over a power of 10 and simplify. The calculator does this exactly: 1.375 = 1375/1000 = 11/8. Going the other way, divide: 11/4 = 2.75. Fractions with denominators such as 3 or 7 give repeating decimals, so the tool rounds the decimal to 8 places. The decimal-to-fraction tool expects plain decimals like 1.375 and the fraction-to-decimal tool expects simple fractions like 11/4, not mixed numbers.

Where fractions show up

  • Recipes: halving 3/4 cup gives 3/8 cup.
  • Measurements: inches are split into halves, quarters and eighths.
  • Probability and ratios: 3 favourable outcomes out of 8 is 3/8.
  • Discounts and shares: one third of a bill, or 5/6 of a task done.

Comparing fractions

To see which of two fractions is larger, rewrite both over the same denominator or convert them to decimals. For example 3/8 is 0.375 and 2/7 is about 0.2857, so 3/8 is larger. Cross-multiplying gives the same answer: 3 × 7 = 21 is greater than 2 × 8 = 16. A larger denominator means smaller pieces, so 1/8 is smaller than 1/3. When the numerators are equal, the fraction with the smaller denominator is always the larger one, and a fraction whose numerator is bigger than its denominator is always greater than one whole.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the denominators as well as the numerators: 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5.
  • Forgetting to flip the second fraction when dividing.
  • Leaving the answer unsimplified, such as 14/12 instead of 7/6.
  • Dividing by a fraction equal to zero, which is undefined, or using a denominator of 0.

How to read your result

Common questions

How do I add fractions with different denominators?

Find a common denominator, rescale both fractions, then add the numerators. For 2/7 + 3/8 the common denominator is 56, giving 16/56 + 21/56 = 37/56.

How do I divide fractions?

Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction. For 5/6 divided by 1/3, multiply 5/6 by 3/1 to get 15/6, which simplifies to 5/2.

How do I turn a mixed number into an improper fraction?

Multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator. For 2 3/4 that is 2 x 4 + 3 = 11, so the fraction is 11/4.

Why simplify a fraction?

Simplifying divides the numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor. The value does not change, but the result is easier to read and compare.

How do I convert a decimal to a fraction?

Write the digits over a power of 10 and simplify. For 1.375 that is 1375/1000, which simplifies to 11/8.

Can I enter negative fractions?

Yes. Enter a negative numerator or a negative whole number, and the calculator carries the sign through the steps.

Common questions

Can a denominator be zero?

No. A denominator of zero would mean splitting something into zero pieces, which is undefined — there's no such quantity. Our calculator will flag this rather than guess at an answer.

How do negative fractions work?

Exactly like positive ones, except one part of the fraction carries a minus sign. You can put the sign on the whole number, the numerator, or think of it as applying to the fraction as a whole — the calculator normalizes it either way and always returns the denominator as positive.

Is the decimal version always exact?

Only when the denominator's prime factors are limited to 2s and 5s (like 4, 8, 20, 25). Otherwise the decimal repeats forever — 1/3 becomes 0.333..., which we round to a fixed number of places for display rather than showing an infinite string of digits.

Does simplifying ever change what a fraction means?

Never. Simplifying only rewrites the fraction in smaller numbers — the amount it represents is identical before and after, the same way "one dozen" and "twelve" refer to the same quantity.
