The one formula behind all of this

Every percentage problem boils down to the same equation: P × V1 = V2, where P is the percentage (as a decimal), V1 is the value being modified, and V2 is the result. Which of the three you're solving for just depends on which two you already know — that's what the first calculator above does automatically.

Difference vs. change

"Percentage difference" and "percentage change" sound similar but answer different questions. Percentage difference treats two values symmetrically — there's no "before" or "after," just a comparison. Percentage change (increase/decrease) is directional: it measures how much a specific starting value grew or shrank.

A common mistake: a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease doesn't return you to the original value. $100 up 50% is $150; $150 down 50% is $75 — not $100. Percentage changes don't cancel out symmetrically.

A worked example

To find 5% of 30, enter 5 and 30: the answer is 1.5. For the percentage difference between 10 and 6, the tool divides the gap of 4 by the average of 8 and gets 50%. For a percentage change, start with 500 and increase it by 10% to reach 550. Each of the three tools is on the page, with its formula.

Tips for percentages

Percentage change and percentage difference are different things. Change is measured from a starting value, so going from 50 to 75 is a 50% increase, while going from 75 back to 50 is a 33.3% decrease. Difference uses the average of the two values and has no direction. Do not add percentages that are based on different amounts, and remember that a 20% discount followed by another 20% discount is not 40%.

Common questions

Why is percentage difference always positive?

It uses the absolute value of the difference between the two numbers, so it doesn't matter which value is "first" — the result reflects how far apart they are, not which one is bigger.

How do I convert a percentage to a decimal?

Divide by 100 — 35% becomes 0.35. This is what the calculators above do internally before running the underlying math.
