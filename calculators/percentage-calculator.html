By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A percentage is a part out of 100. 5% of 30 is 1.5, the percentage difference between 10 and 6 is 50%, and increasing 500 by 10% gives 550. To find a percentage change, divide the change by the old value.

The one formula behind all percentage problems

Every percentage problem fits P × V1 = V2, where P is the percentage as a decimal, V1 is the whole and V2 is the part. The first calculator on this page fills in whichever of the three you leave blank. Percentages are just fractions out of 100, so 35% is 35/100 = 0.35.

Tool 1: percentage of a value

  • Find the part: enter the percentage and the whole. 5% of 30 = 0.05 × 30 = 1.5, which is the page’s default.
  • Find the percentage: leave the percentage blank. If 12 is the part of 48, the percentage is 12 ÷ 48 × 100 = 25%.
  • Find the whole: leave “Of Value” blank. If 7.5 is 25% of a number, the number is 7.5 ÷ 0.25 = 30.

Tool 2: percentage difference

Percentage difference compares two values with no before or after. It divides the gap by the average of the two: |V1 − V2| ÷ ((V1 + V2) ÷ 2) × 100. With the defaults 10 and 6 the gap is 4, the average is 8 and the difference is 4 ÷ 8 = 50%. Swapping the values gives the same answer, so it is always positive. It cannot be calculated if the two values add up to zero.

Tool 3: increase or decrease by a percentage

Pick increase or decrease, enter a starting value and a percentage, and the tool applies it: start × (1 + P) for an increase and start × (1 − P) for a decrease. The default is 500 increased by 10%: 500 × 1.10 = 550.

Percentage change between two numbers

To find how much a value changed, subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. A price rising from $80 to $100 is (100 − 80) ÷ 80 = 25%. Direction matters: going from 50 to 75 is a 50% increase, but from 75 back to 50 is a 33.3333% decrease.

Why increases and decreases do not cancel

$100 up 50% is $150, and $150 down 50% is $75, not $100. The second percentage applies to a bigger number. For the same reason, two successive 20% discounts are not 40%: 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64, a total discount of 36%.

Percentages above 100 and below zero

A percentage can be larger than 100. If a company’s sales grow from 40 to 120, the new value is 300% of the old one, which is an increase of 200%. A negative percentage change means a decrease: a result of −25% says the value fell by a quarter. In the calculators, the percentage box accepts any number, so you can enter 150 to find one and a half times a value, but the difference tool needs values that do not add to zero.

Percent versus percentage points

If an interest rate rises from 4% to 5%, it has risen by 1 percentage point but by 25% in percent terms (1 ÷ 4). News and reports often mix these up, so check which one is meant.

More worked examples

  • Tax on a bill: 8% of $65 is 0.08 × 65 = $5.20, so the total is $70.20.
  • Test score: a score of 36 out of 45 is 36 ÷ 45 = 80%.
  • Salary rise: $52,000 increased by 4% is 52,000 × 1.04 = $54,080, a rise of $2,080.
  • Shrinking price: $240 reduced by 35% is 240 × 0.65 = $156.
  • Working back: if a jacket costs $90 after a 25% discount, the original price was 90 ÷ 0.75 = $120.

Everyday uses

  • Sale prices: 30% off $80 is $80 × 0.70 = $56.
  • Tips and tax: add the percentage with Tool 3 or use the Tip and Sales Tax calculators.
  • Grades: 42 correct out of 50 is 42 ÷ 50 = 84%.
  • Growth: how much a price, population or balance has changed.

Common mistakes

  • Using the new value instead of the old value as the denominator for change.
  • Adding percentages that apply to different amounts.
  • Confusing percentage difference (symmetric) with percentage change (directional).
  • Typing 0.35 instead of 35 in a percentage box. The boxes take percentages.

How to read your result

Check the sentence under the large number: it restates the problem in words, such as “5% of 30 is 1.5”. If it reads differently from what you meant, you have probably put a value in the wrong box. For comparing a measured value with a reference, use the Percent Error Calculator, and for prices after a sale use the Discount Calculator.

Percentages describe a relationship to a base value. Always know what the 100% is.

Common questions

Why is percentage difference always positive?

It uses the absolute value of the gap between the two numbers, so it does not matter which value comes first. The result shows how far apart they are, not which is bigger.

How do I convert a percentage to a decimal?

Divide by 100. 35% becomes 0.35. The calculators do this internally before running the math.

How do I calculate a percentage change?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. From 80 to 100 that is 20 divided by 80, which is 25%.

Why does a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease not return to the start?

Each percentage applies to a different base. $100 up 50% is $150, and 50% of $150 is $75.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

A rise from 4% to 5% is 1 percentage point but a 25% increase in the rate itself.

How do I find the original number from a percentage?

Divide the part by the percentage as a decimal. If 7.5 is 25% of a number, the number is 7.5 divided by 0.25, which is 30.
