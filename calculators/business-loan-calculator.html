Business Loan Calculator

Find the payback amount and the true cost of a business loan, including fees, with fixed payments, interest-only payments or a single payment at the end.

Amortization Schedule

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A business loan costs more than its interest rate once fees are counted. A $10,000 loan at 10% over 5 years with a 5% origination fee and $750 documentation fee has a payment of $212.47 a month and a real rate (APR) of 15.93%.

What a business loan really costs

The interest rate is only part of the price. Lenders usually add an origination fee (a percentage of the loan), a documentation fee (a flat amount) and sometimes other charges. They are typically taken out of the money you receive or paid up front, but you still repay the whole amount. The calculator adds them up and then finds the real rate: the yearly rate at which your payments repay the cash you actually received. This is the APR, and it lets you compare loans fairly.

Worked example with the default numbers

Borrow $10,000 at 10% interest, compounded monthly, over 5 years with monthly payments, a 5% origination fee ($500) and a $750 documentation fee. Fees total $1,250, so you receive only $8,750. The payment is $212.47 a month, the total of 60 payments is $12,748.23 and interest is $2,748.23. Counting the fees, the cost of borrowing is $3,998.23 and the real rate is 15.933% instead of 10%.

Three ways to repay

  • Regular payments (daily, weekly, every two weeks, half-monthly, monthly, quarterly, every six months or yearly): each payment covers the interest due plus part of the principal.
  • Interest only: you pay just the interest each month and repay the whole principal at the end.
  • In the end: no payments until maturity, when you pay the principal plus all the interest that built up.

On the default loan, interest-only payments are $83.33 a month and the full $10,000 is due at the end; total interest is $5,000 with a real rate of 13.447%. If everything is repaid at maturity you owe $16,453.09, a real rate of 12.696%.

Fees are fixed costs spread over the life of the loan, so a short loan carries them for less time. With the same 5% + $750 fees:

Compounding and payback frequency

The compounding setting decides how often interest is added to the balance, and the payback setting decides how often you pay. More frequent compounding adds interest sooner, so the same stated rate costs slightly more. The real rate is shown on the basis you choose: Annually gives an annual percentage yield (APY) and Monthly gives an APR.

Reading the schedule

With regular payments the table can be shown by year or by each payment. For the default loan the first payment has about $83.33 of interest (10% ÷ 12 × $10,000) and the rest reduces the principal. Interest-only loans have no schedule, and a repay-in-the-end loan shows how the balance grows each year.

Before you borrow

  • Ask for the APR and every fee in writing.
  • Check for prepayment penalties and what happens on late payments.
  • Compare with government-backed options such as SBA loans.
  • Match the repayment style to the cash your business will actually have.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing interest rates when fees differ.
  • Choosing interest-only or balloon repayment without a plan for the large final payment.
  • Forgetting that fees reduce the cash you receive.
  • Ignoring how short terms inflate the real rate.

How to read your result

The large number is the payback per period. Below it are the total of the payments, interest, fees and the real rate. If the real rate is far above the quoted rate, the fees are heavy for the term. Test a longer term, lower fees or a different payback frequency.

This calculator gives estimates. Real business loans may have other charges, different interest conventions or prepayment rules, so check the lender’s disclosure. It is not financial advice.

Common questions

What is the real rate of a business loan?

The real rate is the yearly rate that makes your payments repay the cash you actually received after fees. It is also called the APR, and it is higher than the quoted interest rate whenever the loan has fees.

What is an origination fee?

An origination fee is a charge for processing the loan, quoted as a percentage of the amount borrowed. A documentation fee is a flat charge for preparing the paperwork. Both are counted in the real rate.

What is an interest-only business loan?

You pay just the interest on the full balance each month, so the payment stays low and the balance does not fall. The whole principal is due at the end of the term.

What does repaying in the end mean?

There are no payments during the loan. At maturity you pay the principal plus all the interest that built up with compounding. This is common with short-term commercial loans.

Why does the compounding setting change the payment?

It decides how often interest is added to the balance. More frequent compounding adds interest sooner, so the same stated rate costs slightly more and the payment is a little higher.

Why is the real rate higher on shorter loans?

Fees are fixed costs spread over the life of the loan. On a short loan they are spread over less time, so they add more to the yearly rate.
