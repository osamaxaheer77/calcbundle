Grade Calculator

Weighted grading multiplies each category score by its contribution to the final grade. Check that the category weights add to 100% and that missing scores are treated appropriately.

Weighted Grade Calculator

Final Exam Grade Calculator

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A weighted grade is the sum of score times weight divided by the total weight. Scores of 90% (worth 5%), 83% (20%) and 88% (20%) give 86.00%. To find the final exam score you need, use (desired − current × (1 − weight)) ÷ weight: a 88% grade, a target of 85% and a 40% final need 80.5%.

How a weighted grade is calculated

Most courses weight categories differently, so a simple average of scores is wrong. The weighted formula is overall = Σ(score × weight) ÷ Σ(weight). Each score is multiplied by its weight, the results are added, and the total is divided by the sum of the weights. A 20%-weight exam therefore moves your grade four times as much as a 5%-weight assignment.

Worked example with the starting rows

The calculator starts with three scores: 90% worth 5%, 83% worth 20% and 88% worth 20%.

Overall = 3870 ÷ 45 = 86.00%. The weights add up to only 45%, so the calculator normalises the result to the weight entered. That is exactly what you want for a partial course: it shows your grade so far instead of treating missing work as zero.

Another example: a complete course

Homework 95% (30% of the grade), quizzes 88% (30%) and a midterm 79% (40%): overall = (95 × 30 + 88 × 30 + 79 × 40) ÷ 100 = 86.5%. The weakest category carries the most weight, so improving the midterm score would help the most.

What score do I need on the final?

The second tool treats your grade as a mix of what you have now and the final: desired = current × (1 − final weight) + required × final weight. Solving for the final gives required = (desired − current × (1 − weight)) ÷ weight.

With the starting values (current grade 88%, target 85%, final worth 40%): (85 − 88 × 0.6) ÷ 0.4 = 80.5%. You can score below your current average and still reach the target.

When the answer is over 100% or below 0%

  • Above 100%: the target cannot be reached without extra credit or a curve. Lower the target or ask about extra credit.
  • Below 0%: you have already secured that grade, even with a zero on the final.
  • Between: the number is the score you need, assuming the final is graded on the same scale as the rest.

Details that change the result

  • Dropped scores: if the lowest quiz is dropped, remove it before entering the scores.
  • Extra credit: enter total points earned over total points possible for the category, or add the extra credit to the score.
  • Points versus weights: use the weight of each category from the syllabus, not the number of points, unless your course is graded on total points.
  • Rounding: some instructors round up at 89.5%. Check the syllabus.

Common mistakes

  • Averaging scores without weights.
  • Using the number of points instead of the category weights.
  • Treating missing work as zero when you want the grade so far (the calculator already normalises).
  • Entering a grade as a letter. The calculator works with percentages; use the GPA Calculator for letter grades.
  • Forgetting that the final-exam tool assumes the rest of the grade is fixed.

How to read your result

Use the overall percentage to see where you stand and the required final score to plan your effort. If the number needed is over 100%, talk to your instructor early about options. If you want to translate a percentage into a grade point average, use the GPA Calculator with your school’s scale.

Common questions

What if my item weights don't add up to 100%?

The weighted calculator still works. It divides by whatever total weight you entered, so a partial course grade, such as the first half of a semester, is fine and shows your grade so far.

Does this handle letter grades?

Enter scores as percentages. For letter grades and the 4.3 scale, use the GPA Calculator.

Why is the required final score sometimes over 100%?

The target needs more points than the final can give. You would need extra credit, a curve, or a lower target.

Can a final score below my current grade still reach my target?

Yes. If your target is lower than your current grade, the final can be lower and still give the grade you want, as in the example where 80.5% is enough to move from 88% to 85%.

How do I handle a dropped lowest score?

Remove the lowest score from the category before entering it, because the calculator treats every row as a counting score.

Is the final-exam tool accurate for a category-based course?

It assumes everything except the final is already fixed and combines it with the final using the final's weight. It works well when you enter your current overall grade from the syllabus weights.
