Quick answer. A tire size such as 265/70R17 gives its diameter: wheel diameter plus twice the sidewall. That tire is 31.6 inches (802.8 mm) tall with 638.1 revolutions per mile. Swapping to 265/40R23 changes the diameter by -0.8%, so a 60 mph reading is really 59.5 mph.

Reading a tire size code

A code like 265/70R17 breaks down as: 265 is the section width in millimetres, 70 is the aspect ratio (the sidewall height as a percentage of the width), R means radial construction, and 17 is the wheel diameter in inches. A letter in front, such as P for passenger, shows the tire type. The sizes are moulded into the sidewall and also appear on the vehicle’s tire placard.

The formulas

  • Sidewall height = width × aspect ratio ÷ 100.
  • Overall diameter = wheel diameter + 2 × sidewall height.
  • Circumference = π × diameter.
  • Revolutions per mile = 63,360 inches ÷ circumference in inches (per kilometre: 1,000,000 mm ÷ circumference in mm).

Worked example with the default numbers

A 265/70R17 tire has a sidewall of 265 × 0.70 = 185.5 mm (7.3 in). The overall diameter is 17 × 25.4 + 2 × 185.5 = 802.8 mm (31.6 in). The circumference is 99.3 in (2,522.1 mm), which is 638.1 revolutions per mile or 396.5 per kilometre.

Why speedometer accuracy changes

A speedometer counts wheel rotations and assumes a fixed distance per rotation. A taller tire covers more ground per rotation, so the speedometer and odometer read low; a shorter tire makes them read high. For example, 285/70R17 is 3.5% taller than 265/70R17, so a 60 mph reading really means about 62.1 mph and the odometer shows only about 9,663.0 miles for every 10,000 true miles.

How much change is acceptable?

A common rule of thumb is to keep the overall diameter within about 3% of the original, which for the default tire is 0.9 inches either way. Beyond that, the speedometer, odometer, gearing, traction control and clearance can all be affected. Check the load rating and the fit in the wheel well and suspension as well.

Revolutions per mile and gearing

At 60 mph a car covers one mile a minute, so the default tire turns about 638.1 times a minute. A taller tire turns fewer times for the same speed, which acts like a slightly taller gear: the engine runs at lower revolutions at a given speed, which can help fuel economy on the highway but cost some acceleration. The 285/70R17 above turns 616.6 times per mile, 3.4% fewer than the default.

Before you buy new tires

  • Find the size on the sidewall of the tire you have, on the tire placard (usually inside the driver’s door) and in the owner’s manual.
  • Use the compare tool to check the new size against the old one, and aim to stay close to zero difference.
  • Check the load index and speed rating are at least what the vehicle requires.
  • If you replace only two tires, ask the tire shop which axle to put them on and keep the diameters matched.

Plus sizing: bigger wheels, lower profile

If you fit larger wheels, choose a tire with a shorter sidewall to keep the diameter. For example, 275/55R20 has a diameter of 31.9 in, 1.0% different from the default. A wider tire with a lower aspect ratio can have a similar or even smaller diameter than a narrower tire with a taller sidewall.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the wheel size in millimetres. It is in inches.
  • Using the aspect ratio as a number of millimetres. It is a percentage of the width.
  • Assuming a wider tire is always taller.
  • Ignoring load rating and clearance.

Common questions

Does a wider tire always mean a bigger diameter?

Not necessarily. A wider tire with a lower aspect ratio (shorter sidewall) can end up with a similar or even smaller overall diameter than a narrower tire with a taller sidewall.

Can I put a different wheel size on my car?

Often yes, as long as the resulting tire's overall diameter stays close to the original. This is called plus sizing. Always check clearance and load rating too.

How much can I change the tire diameter?

A common rule of thumb is within about 3% of the original, to keep the speedometer, odometer and gearing close to correct.

Why does my speedometer read wrong with different tires?

It is calibrated to the original tire's wheel rotations. A taller tire travels farther per rotation, so it reads low; a shorter tire makes it read high.

What does the R in 265/70R17 mean?

It means radial construction, the standard type for modern passenger tires.

How is revolutions per mile calculated?

It is 63,360 inches in a mile divided by the tire's circumference in inches.
