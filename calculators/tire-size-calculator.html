Reading a tire size code

A code like 265/70R17 breaks down as: 265 = section width in mm, 70 = aspect ratio (sidewall height as a percentage of width), R = radial construction, 17 = wheel diameter in inches. Overall tire diameter = wheel diameter + 2 × sidewall height, where sidewall height = width × aspect ratio.

Why speedometer accuracy changes with tire size

A speedometer is calibrated for a specific tire diameter (via wheel rotation count). Fitting a different-diameter tire changes how far the car actually travels per wheel rotation, so the speedometer — and odometer — read slightly off from the true speed and distance.

Tire professionals generally recommend keeping overall diameter within about ±3% of the original spec to avoid speedometer error and clearance or handling issues.

A worked example

A 265/70R17 tire is 31.6 inches tall (802.8 mm), with a sidewall of 7.3 inches, a circumference of 99.3 inches, and 638.1 revolutions per mile. Compare it with a 265/40R23, which is 31.3 inches tall: the diameter differs by −0.8%, so when your speedometer shows 60 mph you are actually going 59.5 mph. Tips for changing tire sizes

Keep the overall diameter within about 3% of the original so that the speedometer, odometer and gearing stay close to correct. If you install larger wheels, choose a tire with a lower profile to keep the same diameter. Check the load rating and the clearance in the wheel well and the suspension. A tire size reads width in millimeters, aspect ratio as a percentage of the width, then the wheel diameter in inches.

Common questions

Does a wider tire always mean a bigger diameter?

Not necessarily — a wider tire with a lower aspect ratio (shorter sidewall) can end up with a similar or even smaller overall diameter than a narrower tire with a taller sidewall.

Can I put a different wheel size on my car?

Often yes, as long as the resulting tire's overall diameter stays close to the original — this is called "plus sizing." Always check clearance and load rating too.
