Quick answer. IRR is the yearly rate that makes the present value of an investment's cash flows zero. $1,000 out and $1,100 back a year later is a 10% IRR. For the default uneven flows (-$10,000, -$10,000, +$30,000, +$50,000) the IRR is 92.0%.

What IRR is actually answering

The internal rate of return is the single, constant yearly rate that makes an investment’s cash flows net to exactly zero in present-value terms: money going out and money coming back, weighted by when each moved. It answers the question every investment asks: what rate of return am I actually earning, given what I put in, what I got back, and when? Its equation is 0 = CF0 + CF1/(1 + r) + CF2/(1 + r)2 + …, which has to be solved by trial, so the calculator searches for the r that works.

Tool 1: a regular deposit or withdrawal

Use this when money moves at a fixed frequency, such as a monthly withdrawal from an account. Enter the starting amount, the holding period, the ending balance, the recurring amount, whether it is a deposit or withdrawal, the frequency and whether it happens at the beginning or end of each period. With the defaults, $10,000 held for 2 years 6 months, $100 withdrawn at the end of each month and an ending balance of $15,000, the IRR is 29.768% per year. Cumulative withdrawals are $3,000, so the account must have earned $8,000 on the way to $15,000 after paying out $3,000.

Tool 2: uneven yearly cash flows

Use this when cash flows differ from year to year, with up to ten years of entries. Cash flows may be negative, such as a repair bill or a follow-on investment. With the defaults, an initial $10,000, then −$10,000 in year 1, $30,000 in year 2 and $50,000 in year 3, the IRR is 91.964% per year. Further investments total $10,000, the investment lasts 3 years, the total return is $60,000 and the gross return is 300% (return divided by money invested).

IRR and gross return

Gross return (profit divided by total invested) ignores timing completely: a 20% gain over one year and a 20% gain over ten years look identical. IRR annualizes the result, so the same gain earned faster always has a higher IRR.

Simple checks

  • $1,000 out and $1,100 back after one year is a 10% IRR.
  • $1,000 out and $500 back each year for three years is a 23.38% IRR.
  • Negative IRR means you got back less than you put in, after allowing for timing.

When IRR can mislead

IRR assumes every inflow can be reinvested at the same rate, which may be unrealistic. It ignores the size of the investment: a huge IRR on a tiny amount may be worth less in dollars than a modest IRR on a large one. And when cash flows change sign more than once, there can be no single IRR or several. For example, −100, then +230, then −132 has IRRs of both 10% and 20%. In such cases this calculator says that no single IRR could be found, instead of showing a misleading number. Compare projects with NPV as well as IRR.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the initial investment as a negative number. Enter it as a positive amount.
  • Mixing up deposits and withdrawals in Tool 1.
  • Comparing IRRs of projects of very different size or length.
  • Forgetting that IRR is a yearly rate. Tool 1 converts its periodic rate to a yearly one.

How to read your result

The large number is the IRR per year. Below it are the supporting figures: cumulative deposits or withdrawals and the ending balance for Tool 1, or further investments, investment length, total return and gross return for Tool 2. To compare with a hurdle rate, such as your cost of borrowing, an IRR above it suggests the investment adds value. For simple profit, use the ROI Calculator.

IRR is a model of cash flows you enter. It does not predict returns and is not financial advice.

Common questions

Which calculator should I use?

Use the first if your cash flows are a regular deposit or withdrawal at a fixed frequency, like a monthly distribution. Use the second if your cash flows vary year to year, as with a lumpy payout or an uneven series of returns.

Can IRR be negative?

Yes. A negative IRR means the investment returned less than what was put in, once the time value of money is taken into account.

Is a higher IRR always a better investment?

Generally, but IRR alone does not capture risk, scale or how realistic the assumed cash flows are. A huge IRR on a tiny investment may be worth less in dollars than a modest IRR on a large one.

Why does the calculator say no single IRR was found?

When cash flows change sign more than once there can be no IRR or more than one. In those cases the calculator does not show a misleading number. Use NPV at a chosen discount rate instead.

What is the difference between IRR and gross return?

Gross return is total profit divided by total invested and ignores timing. IRR is a yearly rate that accounts for when each cash flow happened.

How is the rate for monthly cash flows turned into a yearly IRR?

Tool 1 finds the rate for each period and converts it to an effective yearly rate: (1 + periodic rate) raised to the number of periods a year, minus 1.
