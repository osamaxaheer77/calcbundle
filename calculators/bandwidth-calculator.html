Bits, bytes and bandwidth

A bit is the smallest unit of information, 0 or 1. A byte is 8 bits. File sizes are usually written in bytes, while network speeds are written in bits per second, which is why a 100 Mbit/s connection moves at most 12.5 megabytes each second. In this calculator, kilo, mega, giga and tera follow the decimal system, so a kilobit is 1,000 bits and a megabyte is 1,000,000 bytes. Disk and memory tools sometimes use 1,024-based units, written KiB, MiB and GiB: a 500 MiB file is 524.288 MB.

The download tool opens on a 500 MB file and a speed of 5 Mbit/s. The file is 4,000 megabits, so the time is 4,000 ÷ 5 = 800 seconds, shown as 13 minutes 20 seconds. The tool also lists 800 total seconds, 13.33333333 minutes and 0.2222222222 hours.

Why real downloads take longer

The tool gives the best case. Real transfers are slower for several reasons: protocol overhead, encryption, Wi-Fi interference, a connection shared with other devices, and limits at the server. Even on a clean wired Ethernet link, TCP over IPv4 carries 1,460 bytes of data in 1,538 bytes on the wire, an efficiency of about 94.9%. At that efficiency the 500 MB file at 5 Mbit/s takes about 14 minutes 3 seconds rather than 13 minutes 20 seconds, and Wi-Fi is usually further below the link rate.

Website bandwidth

Enter the page views in a period and the average page size, and the tool spreads the data over the seconds in the period. The page opens on 5,000 page views a day with pages of 500 KB and a redundancy factor of 2. The data is 5,000 × 500 KB = 2,500,000 KB a day, which is 20,000,000,000 bits spread over 86,400 seconds: 0.2314814815 Mbit/s on average. The redundancy factor of 2 doubles it to 0.462962963 Mbit/s, which is about 152.1875 GB per month (the tool counts a month as 30.4375 days). Without the factor, the monthly total is 76.09375 GB.

More examples

  • An online backup. Uploading a 200 GB backup at an upload speed of 20 Mbit/s is 1,600,000 megabits ÷ 20 = 80,000 seconds, or 22 hours 13 minutes 20 seconds. Upload speeds are usually lower than download speeds, so this is the number to check for backups.
  • A 4K movie. A 20 GB file at 50 Mbit/s is 160,000 megabits ÷ 50 = 3,200 seconds, or 53 minutes 20 seconds.
  • A software update. A 2 GB update at 100 Mbit/s takes 2 minutes 40 seconds in the best case.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing Mbit/s with MB/s. Divide by 8 to go from megabits per second to megabytes per second.
  • Using binary sizes with decimal speeds. A MiB is about 4.9% larger than a MB.
  • Expecting the quoted speed in practice. Real throughput is lower.
  • Sizing a server for the average rather than the peak. Use the redundancy factor.
  • Forgetting bots and background traffic in page views.

How to read your result

The large number is the answer for that tool: the converted amount list, the transfer time, the speed needed, or the converted allowance. Time is shown in days, hours, minutes and seconds, and the download tool also lists total seconds, minutes and hours. Treat every figure as an estimate and leave a margin.

These calculations are theoretical. Latency, congestion, overhead and limits at the other end all reduce real speeds.

Common questions

How many megabytes per second is 100 Mbit/s?

Divide by 8. A 100 Mbit/s connection moves at most 12.5 megabytes each second.

How is download time calculated?

Convert the file size to bits, convert the speed to bits per second, and divide. A 500 MB file is 4,000 megabits, so at 5 Mbit/s it takes 800 seconds, or 13 minutes 20 seconds.

Why is my real download slower than the calculator says?

The calculator gives the best case. Protocol overhead, encryption, Wi-Fi, shared connections and server limits all reduce real throughput.

What does the redundancy factor do?

It multiplies the average bandwidth to leave headroom for traffic spikes. A factor of 2 doubles the average.

How is a monthly data allowance turned into Mbit/s?

The allowance is spread evenly over a month of 30.4375 days. A 1,000 GB allowance works out to about 3.04 Mbit/s.

Does the calculator use 1,000 or 1,024 for kilo and mega?

It uses 1,000, the decimal system, so a megabyte is 1,000,000 bytes. A mebibyte (MiB) is 1,048,576 bytes.
