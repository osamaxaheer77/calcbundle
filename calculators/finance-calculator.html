Quick answer. Any four of PV, PMT, N, I/Y and FV fix the fifth. $20,000 at 6% with $2,000 paid a year leaves a balance of $9,455.36 after 10 years. Doubling money at 6% takes 11.90 years, and doubling in 10 years needs 7.18%.

Five numbers, one equation

Every compound-interest situation, whether a loan, a savings plan or a bond, relates five quantities: the present value (PV), the regular payment (PMT), the number of periods (N), the interest rate per year (I/Y) and the future value (FV). Know any four and the fifth is fixed. The calculator has five tabs, one for each unknown, and works like the time-value-of-money keys on a financial calculator such as the BA II Plus or HP 12C.

The sign convention

Money you receive is positive and money you pay out is negative. A loan is positive PV (the bank gives you money) with negative payments (you pay it back). Savings is the opposite: a negative payment into the account and a positive FV later. Getting the signs right is the difference between a sensible answer and a confusing one. A negative result just means that cash flows the opposite way to the numbers you entered as positive.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens on the FV tab with N = 10, I/Y = 6%, PV = $20,000, PMT = −$2,000, and payments at the end of each year. Read it as a $20,000 loan at 6% on which you pay $2,000 a year. The future value is PV × 1.0610 + PMT × (1.0610 − 1) ÷ 0.06 = 35,816.95 − 26,361.59, which gives -$9,455.36. The minus sign means you still owe $9,455.36 after ten payments. You paid $20,000 in all, so the total interest is $9,455.36.

Solving for each variable

Payment (PMT)

To repay $20,000 in 10 years at 6%, set FV to 0 and solve for PMT: -$2,717.36 a year. That is the loan payment the 5-key method gives, and it is more than the $2,000 of the example above because that payment left $9,455 unpaid.

Interest rate (I/Y)

To find the rate you are earning, enter a negative PV (the money you put in), PMT = 0 and a positive FV. Turning $10,000 into $20,000 in 10 years needs an I/Y of 7.1773%. The same method finds the APR on a loan: $20,000 repaid with 60 monthly payments of $400 (P/Y = 12, C/Y = 12) works out to 7.4201%.

Number of periods (N)

To find how long a goal takes, solve for N. Doubling $10,000 at 6% takes 11.8957 years. With regular saving, set PV to 0, PMT to −$500, P/Y and C/Y to 12, I/Y to 6% and FV to $100,000: end-of-month payments take 138.9757 months (about 11.6 years), and payments at the start of each month take 138.4763 months. The tool uses the beginning or end setting when it solves for N.

Present value (PV)

To find what a future sum is worth today, enter FV and solve for PV. To have $50,000 in 10 years at 6%, you need to put in -$27,919.74. The minus sign shows it is money you pay out now.

Payments at the beginning or end of the period

The last box sets when each payment is made. A payment at the start of a period earns interest for that whole period, so the future value is higher. Saving $2,000 a year for 10 years at 6% gives $26,361.59 with payments at the end of each year and $27,943.29 with payments at the start.

P/Y and C/Y

P/Y is the number of payments a year and C/Y is the number of times the rate compounds a year. Leave them equal in nearly every case. They differ when the compounding and payment schedules differ. A 6% rate compounding daily (C/Y = 365) with $500 monthly payments (P/Y = 12) gives a periodic rate of 0.5012% instead of 0.5000%, so ten years of payments grow to $82,004.32 instead of $81,939.67.

When there is no answer

Some combinations have no solution. For example, a positive PV and a positive FV with no payments cannot grow into each other, since you cannot gain money without paying anything in or out. The calculator now says there is no solution and asks you to check the signs, instead of showing a meaningless figure.

Common mistakes

  • Entering all payments and values as positive, which gives results with the wrong sign.
  • Mixing monthly payments with a yearly N. N counts periods, so ten years of monthly payments is 120.
  • Setting P/Y and C/Y to different numbers by accident.
  • Forgetting that the beginning-of-period setting raises the future value.
  • Treating the total interest line as the interest on the original amount only. It covers all payments.

How to read your result

The large number is the variable you solved for, in dollars, years or percent. Below it are the sum of all periodic payments and the total interest. The chart and the period table show the balance and the interest period by period, using the same signs you entered.

This is a standard time-value-of-money calculation. Real loans and accounts may add fees, rounding rules and other terms that change the exact figures.

Common questions

Why is my answer negative?

That is the sign convention at work, not an error. A negative result means that value flows in the opposite direction from the ones you entered as positive. A negative future value on a loan, for example, is the balance you still owe.

What's the difference between beginning and end of period payments?

It changes how many periods each payment has to earn interest. A payment at the start of a period earns interest for that whole period, so annuities due always produce a slightly larger future value than ordinary annuities, all else equal.

Why would I ever need to solve for N or I/Y?

Solving for N answers how long it will take to reach a goal. Solving for I/Y answers what rate of return you are actually getting. Both are common questions.

What do P/Y and C/Y mean?

P/Y is the number of payments per year and C/Y is the number of compounding periods per year. Leave them the same unless the rate compounds on a different schedule from the payments.

How do I use it to work out a loan payment?

Choose PMT, enter the loan amount as PV, set FV to 0, and enter the term as N and the rate as I/Y. For $20,000 at 6% over 10 years, the annual payment is $2,717.36.

Why do I see that there is no solution?

Some combinations of signs and amounts cannot be solved, such as a positive PV and a positive FV with no payments. Check that money paid out is negative and money received is positive.
