Lean Body Mass Calculator

Estimate your lean body mass and body fat from height, weight and sex, using several well-known formulas side by side.

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Lean body mass is your weight minus fat. For a 180 cm, 60 kg man the formulas give 53.3 kg (Boer), 51.8 kg (James) and 51.2 kg (Hume), roughly 85% to 89% of body weight.

What lean body mass is

Lean body mass is everything in your body except fat: bones, muscle, organs, blood, skin and water. It equals your total weight minus your body fat weight. For most adults it is between about 60% and 90% of body weight, and men usually have a higher share than women. Doctors use it to set doses of some drugs, and people who train use it to follow muscle gain separately from fat loss.

What the calculator does

Enter your sex, whether you are 14 or younger, your height and your weight in metric or US units. The calculator shows the lean body mass from three reference formulas, Boer, James and Hume, and adds the Peters formula for children aged 14 or younger. For each it gives the lean mass, its share of your weight and the body fat percentage that is left over.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens with a man, 180 cm tall, weighing 60 kg. The Boer formula gives 0.407 × 60 + 0.267 × 180 − 19.2 = 53.3 kg, which is 89% of his weight and leaves 11% body fat. James gives 1.1 × 60 − 128 × (60 ÷ 180)² = 51.8 kg (86%, 14% fat). Hume gives 51.2 kg (85%, 15% fat). The first result shown is the large number, the others are in the table.

The US units example

Switch to US units and the page uses 5 feet 10 inches and 160 pounds, which is 177.8 cm and 72.57 kg. The three formulas give 127.5 lb (Boer), 129.0 lb (James) and 120.4 lb (Hume), shown in pounds because you chose US units.

A woman’s example

For a woman 165 cm tall and weighing 60 kg, the formulas give 44.9 kg (Boer), 44.6 kg (James) and 43.4 kg (Hume), between 72% and 75% of her weight. Women have a higher share of essential body fat, so for the same weight the lean mass is lower than for a man of the same size.

Children aged 14 or younger

Tick “Age 14 or younger?” and the Peters formula appears first. For a child of 140 cm and 35 kg it gives 29.1 kg (83% of weight), against 32.4 kg from Boer, 30.5 kg from James and 29.5 kg from Hume. The adult formulas were not built for children, so rely on the Peters line for this age group.

How the formulas react to weight

Each formula treats extra weight differently. For a 180 cm man, Boer adds 0.407 kg of lean mass for every kilogram of weight, Hume adds 0.328 kg, and James starts at 1.1 kg and subtracts more as weight rises.

When the formulas break down

The formulas use height and weight only, so they cannot tell a muscular person from a heavy one, and they drift for very large or very small bodies. For a man 160 cm tall weighing 200 kg, Boer gives 104.9 kg (52%), Hume gives 90.4 kg (45%), but James gives only 20.0 kg (10%), a value that does not make sense. When the three disagree widely, treat the result with caution.

Lean body mass and fat-free mass

The two terms are close but not identical. Lean body mass includes a small amount of essential fat inside organs and the nervous system, while fat-free mass excludes all fat. The gap is about 2% to 3% of body weight in men and 5% to 12% in women.

Working backwards from body fat

If you know your body fat percentage from a scan or a measurement, the sum is simple. An 80 kg person with 25% body fat has 60 kg of fat-free tissue (80 × 0.75). That is more reliable than any formula based on height and weight alone. The Body Fat Calculator estimates the percentage from body measurements.

Common mistakes

  • Entering pounds in the kilogram box, or the other way round. Check the units box.
  • Treating one formula as the truth. Use the range from all three.
  • Using adult formulas for a child, or forgetting to tick the child box.
  • Reading the body-fat column as a measurement. It is only what is left after the formula.
  • Using the result for drug dosing without professional advice.

How to read your result

The large number is the lean body mass from the first formula in the list, with its share of your weight underneath. The table lists every formula with the lean mass and the body fat that remains. Look for the range, not a single number, and compare it with a direct measurement if you can get one.

These are estimates from height and weight only. They do not measure body composition and are not medical advice. For dosing or health decisions, use measurements and guidance from a medical professional.

Common questions

What is the difference between lean body mass and fat free mass?

Lean body mass includes a small amount of essential fat inside organs, while fat free mass excludes all fat. The difference is about 2% to 3% of body weight in men and 5% to 12% in women.

Which lean body mass formula is the most accurate?

None is best for everyone. Boer is often used in medical settings, and James and Hume are older formulas. Looking at all three together gives you a range instead of a single number.

Why does the calculator show several formulas?

There is no single agreed way to estimate lean body mass without a scan. Showing the formulas side by side shows how much the estimates differ.

Why is there a separate option for children?

The Peters formula was developed for children aged 14 or younger. The adult formulas were not built for growing bodies.

Can lean body mass show muscle gain?

Only roughly. The formulas use height and weight alone, so they cannot separate muscle from fat. A body scan or skinfold measurement tracks changes better.

Why do I see odd values for very large or very small bodies?

The formulas are averages built from typical people. At extreme heights or weights a formula can give a result that does not make sense, so treat those results with caution.
