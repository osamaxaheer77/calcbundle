Calorie Calculator

Estimate how many calories you need each day to maintain, lose, or gain weight at a chosen rate.

Quick answer. Maintenance calories are your BMR times an activity factor. A 25-year-old man who is 5 ft 10 in and 165 lb with moderate activity needs about 2,549 calories a day to hold his weight, around 2,049 to lose about a pound a week and 3,049 to gain one.

How the estimate is built

The calculator works in two steps. First it estimates your basal metabolic rate (BMR), the calories your body burns at rest, using one of three formulas. Then it multiplies BMR by an activity factor to estimate your daily maintenance calories. Goals are set by adding or subtracting calories from that number: roughly 500 calories a day corresponds to about 1 pound of weight change a week, because a pound of body weight stores about 3,500 calories.

The three BMR formulas

  • Mifflin-St Jeor (default): 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5 for men, − 161 for women.
  • Revised Harris-Benedict: an older equation, 13.397 × kg + 4.799 × cm − 5.677 × age + 88.362 for men.
  • Katch-McArdle: 370 + 21.6 × lean body mass. It needs your body fat percentage and can suit lean or muscular people better.

Worked example with the default numbers

A 25-year-old man who is 5 ft 10 in and 165 lb with moderate activity (exercise 4 to 5 times a week, factor 1.465), using Mifflin-St Jeor: BMR = 10 × 74.8 + 6.25 × 177.8 − 125 + 5 = 1,740. Maintenance = 1,740 × 1.465 = 2,549 calories a day.

Activity level matters most

Example: a woman, age 30

A woman who is 5 ft 5 in, 140 lb and lightly active (factor 1.375) has a BMR of 1,356 and maintenance of 1,864 calories. A 500-calorie daily deficit gives 1,364 calories, about 1 lb of loss a week by the rule of thumb.

What happens over time

The 3,500-calorie rule is a simplification. As you lose weight your maintenance calories fall, so the same intake produces less loss later. For long-term plans, research-based tools such as the NIDDK Body Weight Planner model how needs change as weight changes.

How low is too low?

  • Cutting more than about 1,000 calories below maintenance risks muscle loss and fatigue, and is hard to sustain.
  • Commonly cited minimums are around 1,200 calories a day for women and 1,500 for men without medical supervision. Individual needs differ.
  • Aim for 0.5 to 2 pounds a week. Slower is usually easier to keep up.
  • Talk to a doctor or dietitian before an aggressive plan, or if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, a teenager or have a medical condition.

Adjusting after a few weeks

Weigh yourself under the same conditions and average the weekly numbers. If the trend does not match your goal after two to three weeks, change your intake by 100 to 200 calories rather than making a large cut. Recalculate whenever your weight changes by more than a few pounds.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a higher activity level than you really have. Most desk-based people with a few workouts a week are moderate at best.
  • Treating the result as exact. Each formula is a population estimate.
  • Cutting calories sharply, then rebounding. Gradual change lasts longer.
  • Ignoring protein and food quality while chasing a calorie number.
  • Counting exercise calories twice: the activity factor already includes them.

How to read your result

The big number is maintenance, the calories at which your weight should stay stable. The table shows what a gain or loss goal does to your daily intake. Pick a goal you can follow, track your weight for a few weeks and adjust. For the split between protein, carbs and fat, use the Macro Calculator.

Common questions

Why do the numbers change so much with activity level?

Activity factors run from 1.2 (sedentary) to 1.9 (extra active), nearly a 60% difference. Exercise and daily movement are often the largest variable in energy use, far more than small differences in BMR.

Is this exact?

No. Every formula is a population estimate. Treat the result as a starting point and adjust based on your actual weight trend over two to three weeks.

Which formula should I use?

Mifflin-St Jeor is the default and is a good general choice. Katch-McArdle can be better if you know your body fat percentage, especially if you are very lean or muscular.

How many calories should I cut to lose weight?

A common starting point is 250 to 500 calories below maintenance, about half a pound to a pound a week. Cutting more than 1,000 is not advised without medical supervision.

Why does my weight loss slow down?

As you lose weight your maintenance calories fall, and your body adapts. The same intake therefore gives smaller changes later, so re-run the calculator after significant weight changes.

Should I eat back exercise calories?

Not usually. The activity level already includes your usual exercise. Add food for unusually long or hard sessions only if you are hungry or losing too fast.
