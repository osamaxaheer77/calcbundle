How ovulation is estimated

Ovulation typically happens about 14 days before your next period starts, regardless of how long your overall cycle is — it's the follicular phase (before ovulation) that varies in length, not the luteal phase (after ovulation) that follows it. This calculator estimates your next period from your cycle length, then counts back 14 days to estimate ovulation.

Why the fertile window is wider than one day

An egg only survives 12-24 hours after release, but sperm can survive in the body for up to five days. That's why the "intercourse window for pregnancy" starts several days before the estimated ovulation date rather than on it — timing intercourse across that window, not just on the predicted day, meaningfully improves the odds of conception.

This calculator estimates typical timing from cycle tracking alone. For more precision, consider combining it with basal body temperature tracking or an ovulation predictor kit — and don't rely on it as a form of birth control, since actual ovulation timing varies.

Common questions

What if my cycles are irregular?

This calculator assumes a consistent cycle length, so irregular cycles will make its estimates less reliable. Tracking your actual cycle over a few months, or using basal body temperature or an ovulation kit, can help refine your personal pattern.

What does the "Pregnancy Test" date mean?

It's an estimate of when a home pregnancy test might reliably detect a pregnancy if this cycle resulted in conception — a few days before your next expected period, once hCG levels have had time to build up.
