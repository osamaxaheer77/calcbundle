Carbohydrate Calculator

Work out the daily grams of carbohydrate that fit 40% to 75% of your calorie needs, for maintaining, losing or gaining weight.

Exercise means 15 to 30 minutes of raised heart rate activity. Intense exercise is 45 to 120 minutes, and very intense exercise is 2 hours or more.

Daily carbohydrate by goal

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Carbohydrate is usually 45% to 65% of daily calories. A 25-year-old, 180 cm, 60 kg man with light activity needs about 2,207 calories a day, which is 324 g of carbohydrate at 55% (counting 3.75 calories per gram).

How the carbohydrate amounts are worked out

The calculator first estimates the calories you burn each day: your basal metabolic rate (BMR) from the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, or the Katch-McArdle formula if you know your body fat, multiplied by an activity factor. For weight loss or gain it subtracts or adds about 500 calories a day for each 1 pound (0.5 kg) per week. It then turns a share of those calories into grams of carbohydrate: grams = calories × share ÷ 3.75.

Worked example with the default numbers

A 25-year-old man who is 180 cm and 60 kg with light activity (exercise 1 to 3 times a week, factor 1.375). His BMR is 1,605 calories, so he needs 2,207 calories a day to maintain his weight. At 55% of calories from carbohydrate that is 2,207 × 0.55 ÷ 3.75 = 324 g.

How much carbohydrate is recommended?

  • The Institute of Medicine (now the National Academies) sets an acceptable range of 45% to 65% of calories from carbohydrate for adults.
  • The Food and Agriculture Organization and World Health Organization suggest 55% to 75%, with no more than 10% directly from sugars.
  • The calculator shows 40%, 55%, 65% and 75% so you can pick a level that suits your diet, including one below the lowest official range.

For total daily energy, a very rough rule is that carbohydrate-rich diets fit people who train a lot and need fuel, while lower-carbohydrate approaches suit people who prefer fats and proteins. The ranges here are for general healthy eating.

Why 3.75 calories per gram?

This calculator counts carbohydrate as 3.75 calories per gram, the figure for carbohydrate measured as simple sugars. Food labels usually round to 4 calories per gram. At a 50% share of a 2,000-calorie day, the difference is 267 g at 3.75 and 250 g at 4.

The 130 gram minimum

The brain uses glucose as its main fuel, and 130 grams of carbohydrate a day is the U.S. recommended dietary allowance for adults. If your maintenance calories are below about 1,219 (the point at which 40% is less than 130 g), the calculator does not show a table. It states the amount for each goal in a sentence and holds it to the 130 g minimum instead of a lower figure. Very low calorie intakes are not safe for most people without medical supervision.

Goals change the calories, not the percentages

For a loss or gain target the calories move up or down and the same shares are applied to the new total. At 55% the default person would eat 324 g to maintain, 250 g to lose 0.5 kg a week and 397 g to gain 0.5 kg a week.

A 324 g day could be a bowl of oats at breakfast, rice or pasta at lunch and dinner, fruit as snacks and some bread or potatoes. Choosing whole grains, fruit, vegetables and legumes keeps the quality of the carbohydrate high and adds fibre. The Dietary Guidelines for Americans suggest about 14 g of fibre per 1,000 calories and limiting added sugars to under 10% of calories.

Who should be careful

  • People with diabetes need to match carbohydrate to their medicines and should follow their care team’s advice.
  • People with kidney disease or other conditions may need different amounts.
  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women have different needs.
  • Low-carbohydrate and ketogenic diets use far smaller amounts than this tool shows and need professional planning.

Common mistakes

  • Using a percentage without knowing your calories first.
  • Mixing up 3.75 and 4 calories per gram when comparing with a food label.
  • Treating every carbohydrate the same. Sugary drinks and whole grains are not equal.
  • Cutting calories so far that the result falls below 130 g without medical advice.

How to read your result

Use the calorie number to manage weight and the gram range to shape meals. Start in the middle of the range, track your energy, hunger and weight for two to three weeks, then adjust. For all three macronutrients at once, use the Macro Calculator; for protein and fat on their own, use the Protein and Fat Intake calculators.

These amounts are estimates for healthy adults, not medical advice. Ask a doctor or dietitian about your own needs.

Common questions

How many carbs should I eat a day?

Most guidelines suggest 45% to 75% of daily calories. At 2,000 calories a day that is about 240 to 400 grams at 3.75 calories per gram. The calculator shows the amount for your own calorie needs.

How many calories are in a gram of carbohydrate?

This calculator uses 3.75 calories per gram, the figure for carbohydrate counted as simple sugars. Food labels usually use 4 calories per gram.

Why does the calculator show 130 grams?

The U.S. recommended dietary allowance for carbohydrate is 130 grams a day for adults. If your calorie needs are very low, the calculator holds the amount to that minimum.

Does this work for a low carb or keto diet?

No. The ranges here are for general healthy eating. Low carb and ketogenic diets use far smaller amounts and should be planned with a health professional.

How many calories do I need to lose weight?

Eating about 500 calories a day below your maintenance level leads to a loss of about 1 pound, or half a kilogram, a week. The table shows these calorie levels for each goal.

Are all carbohydrates the same?

No. Whole grains, fruit, vegetables and legumes bring fibre and nutrients, while sugary drinks and sweets mostly add calories. Keep added sugars under 10% of calories.
