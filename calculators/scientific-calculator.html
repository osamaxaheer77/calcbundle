Quick answer. A scientific calculator adds trig, logarithms, powers, roots and factorials to the basic operations. It follows the order of operations, so 2 + 3 × 4 is 14 and (2 + 3) × 4 is 20. It starts in degree mode, where sin(30) is 0.5.

What this scientific calculator can do

Besides the four basic operations it handles powers (x², x³ and xy), square and cube roots and any root with the rt key, reciprocals (x−1), percentages, factorials, the natural logarithm (ln) and the base-10 logarithm (log). It has the constants π and e, the trigonometric functions sin, cos and tan with their inverses, and an EXP key for scientific notation such as 3.2 × 105. Parentheses let you build long expressions on one line.

Degrees and radians

The calculator starts in degree mode. Use the Deg/Rad switch to change the angle unit for the trigonometric keys. In degree mode sin(30) is 0.5; in radian mode you would enter sin(π ÷ 6) to get the same 0.5, and sin(30) would instead give -0.9880. If a trig answer looks wrong, the angle mode is the first thing to check. Tangent of an odd multiple of 90° (or π/2 radians) is undefined, and the calculator shows Error rather than a huge number.

Order of operations

Expressions are worked out in the standard order: parentheses first, then exponents, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction. So 2 + 3 × 4 is 14, while (2 + 3) × 4 is 20. Use parentheses to make your intent clear, especially with division and negative numbers, as in 1 ÷ (2 + 3). Powers group from the right, so 2 ^ 3 ^ 2 is 29 = 512.

A growth example

A growth of 5% a year for 10 years multiplies a balance by 1.0510 = 1.62889, so $1,000 becomes about $1,629. Enter 1.05 ^ 10 and press equals. The same keystrokes with 20 in place of 10 give 2.6533.

Scientific notation and very large numbers

Use the EXP key to enter a number such as 3.2E5, which means 3.2 × 105 = 320,000. Very large and very small results are shown in plain digits as long as the browser can display them; for step-by-step conversions between standard and scientific notation, use the Scientific Notation Calculator.

Memory, Ans and history

M+ adds the last result to the memory, M− subtracts it and MR puts the stored value back into the expression. Ans inserts the previous answer so you can continue a calculation. Every calculation is also listed in the history panel (the last 50 are kept), and you can click an entry to reuse its result. You can type on your keyboard as well: digits and operators, Enter to calculate, Backspace to delete and Escape to clear.

Precision and limits

  • Results are rounded to 10 decimal places so that floating-point noise, such as 0.30000000000000004 for 0.1 + 0.2, does not show.
  • Factorials work up to 170! = 7.257e+306. Larger values overflow and show Error.
  • Division by zero shows Error.
  • Computers store most decimals approximately, so tiny differences can appear in very long calculations.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to check Deg or Rad before using sin, cos or tan.
  • Leaving out parentheses in a fraction, such as 1 ÷ 2 + 3 instead of 1 ÷ (2 + 3).
  • Expecting −2 ^ 2 to equal 4. The power comes first, so it is −4; use (−2) ^ 2 for 4.
  • Using % as a unit of the whole bill. The % key divides the number before it by 100.

How to read your result

The expression appears on the upper line and the result below it, with a live preview as you type. After you press equals, the answer becomes the start of the next expression. For explicit logarithms in any base, use the Log Calculator, and for powers and roots, the Exponent Calculator.

Common questions

How do I find a cube root or another root?

Raise the number to a fractional power. The cube root of 27 is 27^(1/3) = 3, and the fourth root of 81 is 81^(1/4) = 3. You can also use the rt key, as in 3 rt 27, or the cube root key.

How do I switch between degrees and radians?

Use the Deg/Rad switch on the calculator. Degree mode is the default. Make sure the mode matches the angles you enter before using sin, cos or tan.

Why is the answer shown with at most 10 decimal places?

Results are rounded to 10 decimal places so floating-point noise, such as 0.30000000000000004, does not appear in your answer.

Does it follow the order of operations?

Yes. Parentheses come first, then exponents, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction, so 2 + 3 x 4 gives 14.

Why does tan(90) show Error?

The tangent of 90 degrees is undefined. The calculator reports Error instead of showing a huge number caused by rounding.

What is the largest factorial it can do?

170! is the largest. 171! is too large for the calculator and shows Error.
