Fat Intake Calculator

Work out how many grams of fat and saturated fat fit in your day for maintaining, losing or gaining weight.

Daily fat by goal

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Adults are advised to get 20% to 35% of daily calories from fat. A 25-year-old, 180 cm, 60 kg man with light activity needs about 2,207 calories, so about 50 to 88 g of fat a day, with saturated fat under 25 g.

How much fat to eat

Dietary guidelines for adults suggest 20% to 35% of daily calories from fat. Fat is the most concentrated source of energy, about 9 calories per gram on food labels, so a small amount covers a large share of calories. The calculator uses 8.8 calories per gram, a common figure for mixed dietary fat, and works out grams from your daily calorie needs for the goal you choose.

Worked example with the default numbers

A 25-year-old man who is 180 cm and 60 kg with light activity (factor 1.375). His BMR is 1,605 calories, so he needs 2,207 calories a day to maintain weight. Twenty percent of that is 441 ÷ 8.8 = 50 g and 35% is 88 g, so the range is 50 to 88 g of fat a day. Saturated fat should stay under 25 g (10% of calories), or under 18 g (7%) for people who want to lower heart-disease risk.

Why grams of fat look small

Fat has more than twice the energy per gram of carbohydrate or protein. At 30% of a 2,207-calorie day, fat provides 662 calories, but that is only about 75 g, while the same calories from carbohydrate would be 166 g.

Why 8.8 and not 9?

Labels round fat to 9 calories per gram. This calculator uses 8.8, so the gram amounts come out slightly higher. At 2,000 calories and 30% from fat the difference is 68 g at 8.8 against 67 g at 9. Either is within the margin of error of any food label.

Saturated fat

Saturated fat is found mostly in butter, cheese, fatty meat and some oils such as coconut and palm oil. The Dietary Guidelines for Americans advise keeping it under 10% of daily calories. The American Heart Association advises about 5% to 6% for people who want to lower their LDL cholesterol, which is stricter than the 7% limit the calculator shows for heart health. The table above shows the 10% and 7% limits in grams. Replacing some saturated fat with unsaturated fat from fish, nuts, seeds and olive oil is generally better for your heart than replacing it with refined carbohydrate.

How your calorie goal is set

Calories come from your BMR (the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, or Katch-McArdle if you know your body fat) multiplied by an activity factor. About 500 calories a day less leads to a loss of roughly 1 pound or half a kilogram a week, and 500 more leads to a gain. The fat allowance moves with the calories for each goal. If a large deficit pushes the target below zero, the row shows negative numbers: that is a sign the deficit is too big.

Types of fat

  • Unsaturated (olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado, fish): generally favoured for heart health.
  • Saturated (butter, cheese, fatty meat, coconut and palm oil): keep within the limit.
  • Trans fat (partially hydrogenated oils): avoid as far as possible.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the grams as a target for each meal instead of the day.
  • Forgetting cooking oil, dressings and nuts, which add fat quickly.
  • Cutting fat so low that you miss essential fats and fat-soluble vitamins.
  • Assuming fat alone makes you gain weight. Total calories matter most.

How to read your result

The large range is your total fat target and the two lines below it are the saturated fat limits. Aim for the middle of the range if you have no special goal, and keep saturated fat toward the lower limits if heart health is a concern. These figures are general guidance for healthy adults, not medical advice. People with heart or metabolic conditions should follow their doctor’s advice.

Common questions

How much fat should I eat in a day?

Most guidelines suggest 20% to 35% of your daily calories. On 2,000 calories a day, that is about 45 to 80 grams of fat.

How many calories are in a gram of fat?

Food labels use 9 calories per gram. This calculator uses 8.8 calories per gram, a common figure for mixed dietary fat.

How much saturated fat is too much?

Less than 10% of daily calories is the usual limit. Stricter advice, about 5% to 7%, applies to people trying to reduce heart disease risk or lower LDL cholesterol.

Does eating fat make you gain weight?

Weight change depends on total calories. Fat is calorie dense, so it is easy to eat a lot of calories from it, but what matters most is whether you eat more or less than you burn.

Why are some numbers negative?

A very large calorie deficit can take your target below zero. That is not a real or safe goal, so treat those rows as a sign the deficit is too big.

Which fats are better for me?

Unsaturated fats from fish, nuts, seeds, avocado and olive oil are generally better for your heart than saturated fats from butter, fatty meat and some tropical oils.
