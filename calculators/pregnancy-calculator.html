Week-by-Week Pregnancy Schedule

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. A due date is the first day of your last period plus 280 days (40 weeks). If your last period began on April 17, 2026, the due date is January 22, 2027 and conception was most likely around May 1, 2026. Only about 1 baby in 20 arrives on the due date.

Why pregnancy is counted from the last period

Pregnancy is dated from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), not from conception. Week 1 therefore begins about two weeks before conception happens. This is called gestational age. Doctors use it because the first day of the last period is usually known, while the exact moment of conception usually is not.

The calculation

  • Due date = first day of last period + 280 days (40 weeks).
  • Conception is estimated at about 14 days after the last period starts, assuming a 28-day cycle.
  • From conception: due date = conception date + 266 days.
  • From a due date: work backward 280 days to find the start of the count.

The calculator accepts any one of the three dates (last period, conception or due date) and fills in the other two plus a week-by-week schedule.

Worked example with the default dates

If the first day of the last period was April 17, 2026, the due date is 280 days later: January 22, 2027. Conception most likely happened around May 1, 2026, and counting 266 days from that date gives the same due date.

How reliable is a due date?

Only the centre of a window. Roughly 1 baby in 20 arrives on the estimated due date, and most arrive within a couple of weeks either side. A pregnancy is considered full term from 39 weeks 0 days to 40 weeks 6 days, early term from 37 weeks 0 days and late term from 41 weeks 0 days. Treat the date as a guide, not a prediction.

When the date changes

  • Irregular or long cycles: the LMP method assumes ovulation around day 14 of a 28-day cycle, so the estimate is less accurate if your cycles differ.
  • Early ultrasound: if an ultrasound gives a date that differs from the LMP date by more than about a week (the exact limit depends on how early the scan was), your care provider will usually change the due date to the scan date.
  • IVF or known conception: use the conception or transfer date for a more precise estimate. Your clinic will tell you how to count from an embryo transfer.
  • Multiple pregnancies: twins and higher-order pregnancies usually deliver earlier, so a single-baby timeline does not apply.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the date of intercourse or a positive test instead of the first day of the last period.
  • Expecting the baby on the due date.
  • Using the calculator after a very irregular cycle without checking with a doctor.
  • Treating the calculator as medical advice. Your care provider sets the official due date.

How to read your result

Use the due date to plan appointments and leave, and the trimester dates to understand where you are. The earlier a scan is done, the more accurate it is, so if your doctor gives a different date than the calculator, follow your doctor’s date.

This calculator gives estimates for a single pregnancy and is not medical advice. Your doctor or midwife sets the due date used for your care.

Common questions

Which input method should I use?

The last period is the standard default and works well for regular cycles. Use the conception date if you know it precisely, for example from fertility tracking, and the due date option if you have one from your doctor and want the full schedule.

Why does pregnancy start before conception?

Gestational age counts from the first day of the last period because that date is usually known. Conception happens about two weeks later, so the first two weeks of pregnancy happen before you are actually pregnant.

How accurate is the due date?

It is an estimate. Roughly 1 baby in 20 is born on the due date, and most arrive within a couple of weeks before or after.

Will an ultrasound change my due date?

It can. If an early ultrasound dates the pregnancy more than about a week away from the last-period date, providers usually switch to the ultrasound date. Once set, the date normally stays the same.

Does this work for twins?

No. Twin and multiple pregnancies usually follow a shorter timeline, so ask your doctor for dates specific to your pregnancy.

What are the trimesters?

The first trimester runs to the end of week 13, the second from week 14 to the end of week 27 and the third from week 28 until birth.
