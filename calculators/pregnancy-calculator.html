Week-by-Week Pregnancy Schedule

Why pregnancy is measured from the last period, not conception

Confusingly, "week 1" of pregnancy starts on the first day of your last menstrual period — about two weeks before conception actually happens. This is called gestational age, and it's the standard doctors use because the exact date of conception usually isn't known precisely, while the start of the last period usually is.

How reliable is the due date?

Not very, in terms of the exact day — fewer than 4% of babies are born exactly on their due date. About 60% arrive within a week of it, and nearly 90% within two weeks. Treat the due date as the center of a several-week window, not a precise prediction.

Results here are estimates for a single pregnancy based on averages. Twin or multiple pregnancies typically follow a different timeline — consult your doctor for guidance specific to your pregnancy.

A worked example

If the first day of the last period was April 17, 2026, the estimated due date is January 22, 2027, which is 280 days later, and conception most likely happened about May 1, 2026. On that schedule you would be in week 25, at 24 weeks and 6 days, in the second trimester, about 62% of the way through. The week-by-week table shows the date range and milestones for each week.

Tips for using the due date

Only about 4 in 100 babies arrive on the due date, and most are born within two weeks either side, so treat it as a guide. A first-trimester ultrasound is more accurate than the last period if the two disagree by more than a week, and your doctor or midwife will set the date used for your care. Common questions

Which input method should I use?

Last period is the standard default and works well for regular cycles. If you know your conception date precisely (for example from fertility tracking), or already have a due date from your doctor, either of those will give an equally valid schedule.

What determines the trimester boundaries?

This calculator uses the common convention of weeks 1-12 for the first trimester, 13-27 for the second, and 28-40+ for the third — though exact boundaries are sometimes defined slightly differently between sources.
