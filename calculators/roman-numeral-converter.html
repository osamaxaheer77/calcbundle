Convert numbers from 1 to 3,999 into Roman numerals and Roman numerals back into numbers, with a short guide to how the numerals work.

Number to Roman Numeral

Roman Numeral to Number

By Osama · Last reviewed November 6, 2026

Quick answer. Roman numerals use I, V, X, L, C, D and M. 2026 is MMXXVI and 1994 is MCMXCIV. The largest standard numeral is MMMCMXCIX, which is 3,999. Only I, X and C can be subtracted.

How Roman numerals work

Seven letters stand for fixed values. Numerals are normally written from largest to smallest and added together, so VI = 5 + 1 = 6. When a smaller value comes immediately before a larger one, it is subtracted instead, so IV = 5 − 1 = 4. That keeps any letter from being repeated more than three times in a row.

The two tools

The first box turns a whole number from 1 to 3,999 into a Roman numeral. The second does the reverse: type a numeral, in capitals or lower case, and it gives the number. The second box checks that the numeral you typed is written in the standard way before it accepts it.

Worked example with the default numbers

The page opens on 2026 and MMXXVI. The year 2026 is MM (2,000), XX (20) and VI (6), which makes MMXXVI. To go the other way, MMXXVI is M + M + X + X + V + I = 1,000 + 1,000 + 10 + 10 + 5 + 1 = 2,026. Another example is 1994 = MCMXCIV, made from M (1,000), CM (900), XC (90) and IV (4).

Why the limit is 3,999

M (1,000) is the largest single letter, and standard Roman numerals build up large values by repeating letters, with no more than three in a row. So 3,999, MMMCMXCIX, is the largest number that can be written without extra notation. Historically, a bar (vinculum) over a numeral multiplied it by 1,000, which this tool does not support.

Valid and invalid forms

For each number there is one standard spelling. The converter accepts a numeral only if it turns back into the same text. Only I, X and C can be subtracted, and only from the next two larger letters: I from V and X, X from L and C, C from D and M. So 49 is XLIX, not IL.

Tips for reading numerals

  • Read from left to right and add the values, unless a smaller letter comes before a larger one, in which case subtract it.
  • Split the numeral into thousands, hundreds, tens and ones: MCMXCIV is M, CM, XC, IV.
  • Roman numerals have no zero and no decimals, so they cover whole numbers only.
  • Lower case works in the second box. Spaces between letters do not.

Where Roman numerals are still used

  • Clock faces, where 4 is often written IIII.
  • Chapter numbers, outlines and book prefaces.
  • Copyright years on films and buildings.
  • Monarchs and popes, like Elizabeth II, and sporting events such as the Super Bowl.
  • Names of events in a series, and some formal documents.

Common mistakes

  • Writing IIII, VIIII or XXXX. The standard forms are IV, IX and XL.
  • Subtracting more than one letter, as in IIX for 8. The standard form is VIII.
  • Subtracting V, L or D. Only I, X and C can be subtracted.
  • Entering a decimal, 0 or a number above 3,999 in the first box.
  • Typing letters other than I, V, X, L, C, D and M in the second box.

How to read your result

The large line shows the numeral for your number, or the number for your numeral. If the tool says the numeral is not valid, compare it with the standard form in the tables above. A message that only 1 to 3,999 are covered means your number is outside the standard range.

The converter uses the modern standard form. Ancient inscriptions sometimes used other forms, such as IIII, so a numeral that is rejected here may still appear in old sources.

Common questions

What is the largest number in Roman numerals?

Without extra notation it is 3,999, written MMMCMXCIX. Larger numbers historically used a bar over a numeral to multiply it by 1,000, which this converter does not support.

How do you write 2026 in Roman numerals?

MMXXVI, which is MM (2,000), XX (20) and VI (6).

Why is IL not a valid way to write 49?

Only I, X and C can be subtracted, and only from the next two larger numerals. 49 is written XLIX, which is XL (40) plus IX (9).

Is IIII valid?

Not in the standard form. The converter requires IV for 4. IIII is sometimes seen on clock faces.

Can I type Roman numerals in lower case?

Yes. The converter changes the letters to capitals before it checks them.

Is there a zero in Roman numerals?

No. Roman numerals have no zero and cover only whole numbers from 1 up.
