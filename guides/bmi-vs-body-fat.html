BMI vs Body Fat Percentage: What Each One Tells You

What BMI and body fat percentage each measure, how to work them out, and how to use both without being misled.

By Osama · Published October 13, 2026 · 8 min read

Body mass index, or BMI, is the number most doctors, charts and calculators start with. It is quick and needs only a scale and a tape measure. But it is also one of the most criticised numbers in health, because it does not tell you what your weight is made of. Body fat percentage fills in part of that gap. This guide explains what each one measures, how they are calculated, and how to use them together.

The short version

  • BMI compares your weight with your height. It is a screening tool and cannot tell fat from muscle.
  • Body fat percentage estimates how much of your weight is fat. It is more informative, but home estimates are only approximate.
  • Two people with the same BMI can have very different amounts of body fat, and the reverse is also true.
  • Use both, and add your waist measurement and how you feel and perform. No single number gives the full picture.

What BMI is, and how it is calculated

BMI divides your weight by the square of your height. In metric units it is kilograms divided by metres squared. In US units you multiply the result by 703.

BMI = weight (lb) × 703 ÷ height (in)2Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)2

For an example, take a man who is 70 inches tall (5 ft 10 in) and weighs 175 lb. His height squared is 4900, so BMI = 175 × 703 ÷ 4900 = 25.1. You can check any figure with our BMI Calculator.

What BMI cannot tell you

BMI is calculated from two numbers only, so it knows nothing about muscle, bone or where fat is stored. That leads to well-known blind spots:

  • Muscular people can be classed as overweight because muscle is dense, even when their body fat is low.
  • People who have lost muscle, such as some older adults, can have a healthy BMI while carrying a high proportion of fat.
  • Fat distribution matters. Fat around the waist is linked with higher health risk than the same amount carried on the hips and thighs.
  • Ancestry and age change the picture. Some health bodies suggest lower BMI cut-offs for people of Asian descent, for example.

For these reasons, BMI works best as a first screen for groups of people, and less well as a verdict on one individual.

What body fat percentage measures

Body fat percentage is the share of your total weight that is fat: fat mass divided by body weight. The rest is lean mass, which includes muscle, bone, organs and water. Some fat is essential for hormones, nerves and protecting organs, so zero is neither possible nor healthy.

Imagine two people who are both 5 ft 10 in and 175 lb. Both have a BMI of 25.1. One has 12% body fat and the other 28%.

How body fat is estimated at home

The most accurate methods (DEXA scans, underwater weighing and similar) need a clinic or lab. At home, the usual choice is the US Navy method, which estimates body fat from height, neck and waist (plus hips for women) using tape measurements. For men the metric version is:

Body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) − 450Measurements in centimetres. Women use waist + hip − neck, with different constants.

Using the same man, with a 34 inch waist and 15 inch neck: waist − neck = 48.3 cm and height = 177.8 cm. The formula gives about 17.4% body fat, which is 31 lb of fat and 144 lb of lean mass. That is in the "fitness" band for men, even though his BMI says overweight. Our Body Fat Calculator uses this method.

How accurate is it? Tape measurements are sensitive to where you place the tape and how relaxed you are, and a one-centimetre error in the waist moves the result by a noticeable amount. Treat the answer as a rough estimate that can be several percentage points off, and use it to track change over time, measuring the same way each time.

Which should you use?

  • For a quick check, BMI is fine. If it is in the healthy range and your waist is within a healthy size, there is little more to learn.
  • If you lift weights or are very active, body fat percentage describes you better than BMI.
  • If BMI is high or low, check your waist. NIH guidance flags a waist above about 40 inches for men and 35 inches for women as a risk marker.
  • For health decisions, look at blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, fitness and family history, and talk to a doctor. The numbers here are starting points.

Common questions

Is BMI accurate?

It is a reasonable screening tool for large groups, but it can misclassify individuals. It cannot tell muscle from fat, so very muscular people may be labelled overweight and people with little muscle may be labelled healthy.

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

It depends on age and sex. Commonly used fitness ranges put average men at about 18 to 24% and average women at about 25 to 31%, with lower numbers for athletes. Essential fat sets a floor of roughly 2 to 5% for men and 10 to 13% for women.

Is the Navy body fat formula reliable?

It is a convenient estimate and is often close for average builds, but it can be off by several percentage points. Use it to track trends, measuring in the same way each time, rather than as a precise figure.

Why is BMI different for athletes?

Muscle weighs more than the same volume of fat, so people with a lot of muscle can have a BMI above 25 with low body fat. For them, body fat percentage is the more useful number.

Does BMI work for children?

Not with adult cut-offs. For children and teenagers, BMI is compared with growth charts for the same age and sex, and a doctor interprets the result.
